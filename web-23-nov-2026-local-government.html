02 / 15

Identify the urban body before applying its rules

Article 243P defines a municipality as an institution of self-government constituted under Article 243Q. Article 243Q distinguishes a Nagar Panchayat for a transitional area, a Municipal Council for a smaller urban area and a Municipal Corporation for a larger urban area. These are not three interchangeable labels for the same organisation. Nor should a development authority, electricity utility or contractor be renamed the municipality because it works in the same town. In an actual case, identify the body's legal status and applicable law before importing its composition or decision procedure.

03 / 15

Do not turn a ward-committee threshold into a city-classification rule

Article 243Q does not set a single all-India population number that by itself makes every urban area a Municipal Corporation. Classification by the Governor's public notification takes account of population, density, local revenue, non-agricultural employment, economic importance and other relevant factors. Its industrial-township proviso is another reason not to assume one automatic municipal form. The three-lakh number encountered in Article 243S concerns mandatory Wards Committees, not a universal constitutional dividing line between a Municipal Council and a Corporation. Keep each number attached to the provision it belongs to.

04 / 15

Distinguish an elected seat from professional advice

Under Article 243R, the general rule is direct election to municipal seats from territorial constituencies called wards, subject to the representation allowed by clause (2). A State Legislature may provide by law for representation of people with special knowledge or experience in municipal administration. The Constitution expressly denies those specialists the right to vote in meetings of the municipality. Their expertise can inform a lighting proposal; it does not give that category a municipal vote. This limitation concerns that specified category, not a rule that every person other than a directly elected councillor always lacks voting rights.

05 / 15

Ask how the municipal chairperson is elected

Article 243R leaves the manner of election of the municipal chairperson to State legislation. It does not impose one universal direct-election method for every municipality in India. When evaluating a proposed leadership change, distinguish the constitutional permission for State law from the method the particular State law actually adopts. Do not infer the answer from the fact that councillors are generally directly elected. A provision about filling ward seats and a provision about choosing the chairperson answer different institutional questions.

06 / 15

Bring a ward problem to the right committee

Article 243S requires Wards Committees within a municipality having a population of three lakhs or more. A committee can consist of one ward or several wards. The State Legislature may determine composition, territorial area and the manner of filling seats by law. A municipal member representing a ward within the committee's territory must be a member of that committee. For the lighting case, that creates a connection between neighbourhood representation and discussion of local priorities; it does not itself allocate an unlimited budget or substitute for the municipality's lawful decision procedure.

07 / 15

Distinguish a constituency link from voter registration

Article 243R allows State legislation to provide municipal representation for Lok Sabha and State Assembly members whose constituencies include all or part of the municipal area. For Rajya Sabha and State Legislative Council members, the stated connection is being registered as electors within that municipal area. These are different tests, and neither means every MP from the State automatically belongs to every municipality. Keep this legislative-representation route separate from the special municipal-administration experts, whose municipal-meeting voting restriction is express. In the lighting debate, first identify the category under which a person participates before assuming the constitutional basis of that participation.

08 / 15

Find the legal route for municipal money

Article 243X enables State legislation to authorise municipal taxes, duties, tolls and fees subject to procedures and limits; assign specified State-levied and collected revenues; provide grants-in-aid from the State's Consolidated Fund; and provide municipal funds and withdrawal arrangements. These are different routes. A lighting duty does not by itself establish an unrestricted power to create any new tax. Conversely, a State grant is not the same thing as a tax levied by the municipality. The hypothetical grant's conditions must be read as part of the stated case, not invented from the constitutional list.

09 / 15

Separate advice on finances from authorising expenditure

Article 243Y gives the Finance Commission constituted under Article 243I a municipal-finance role too. It reviews municipalities' financial position and recommends principles for revenue sharing, assignment, grants and financial improvement to the Governor. The Governor must have its recommendations and an explanatory memorandum on action taken laid before the State Legislature. This is not a second commission that directly buys the town's lamps. A recommendation, a legislative revenue framework, an approved budget and an actual payment are different stages.

Do not confuse funding a project with operating it

A real programme illustrates why the case separates roles. A January 2025 Press Information Bureau description of the Street Lighting National Programme says Energy Efficiency Services Limited can make the upfront investment, recover it through municipal annuity payments and maintain the lights. A 2022 official factsheet also describes alternative equipment-supply, installation and energy-service-company models. These dated descriptions do not establish the contract in a particular town or a universal rule for every present project. They do show why an outside implementing agency and continuing municipal payments can coexist. The company's remote-monitoring capability is a technical function, not a transfer of constitutional status.

11 / 15

Keep the municipal term clock separate from a project clock

Article 243U gives a municipality five years from the date appointed for its first meeting unless it is sooner dissolved under law. It requires a reasonable opportunity of being heard before dissolution. A lamp contract lasting longer or shorter than that period does not redefine the constitutional term. Nor does an amendment of an existing law itself permit the premature dissolution prohibited by clause (2). For exam purposes, identify which clock belongs to the elected institution and which belongs to a project or contract; do not make a term-length claim merely from the service agreement.

12 / 15

Distinguish expiry from dissolution and identify the election authority

The constitutional election must be completed before the ordinary five-year term expires. If a municipality is dissolved early, Article 243U ordinarily requires the election before six months from dissolution expire. Where less than six months of the original term remain, the provision does not require an election to constitute a municipality for that short remainder. A replacement constituted after early dissolution serves only the unexpired remainder, not a fresh five years. Article 243ZA places municipal electoral rolls and elections under the State Election Commission, not the Election Commission of India.

13 / 15

Use district planning to connect rural and urban plans

Article 243ZD provides for a District Planning Committee to consolidate Panchayat and municipal plans and prepare a draft district development plan. At least four-fifths of its total membership must be elected by and from the elected members of district-level Panchayats and municipalities in the district, with the rural-urban population ratio respected. The State Legislature provides the wider composition and selection framework. A district lighting corridor may require coordination beyond one municipal boundary, but the committee's planning purpose is not a licence to ignore the constitutional membership rule or assume that every service contract is its own operating responsibility.

14 / 15

Do not copy the district formula into metropolitan planning

Article 243ZE provides for a Metropolitan Planning Committee for the metropolitan area. Its minimum elected share is two-thirds, not four-fifths. That share is elected by and from elected municipal members and Panchayat chairpersons in the metropolitan area, in the relevant municipal-Panchayat population proportion. District planning instead refers to elected members of district-level Panchayats and municipalities. Both the numerical floor and the eligible pool matter. Under Article 243P, a metropolitan area also needs the specified population, territorial composition and Governor's notification; it is not simply any town with an elaborate lighting system. In the lighting-corridor case, preparing the metropolitan draft plan does not by itself displace the supplied scheme's municipal expenditure-approval rule. Check the source of each decision power separately.

Turn the case into an argument with limits

A compact Mains answer can proceed through four linked questions: what does the governing law assign, which representative institution can decide, what financial route supports the decision, and who must execute and report under the actual arrangement? State one benefit of locating decisions near local information, then one constraint such as technical capacity, fiscal dependence or cross-boundary coordination. Use the relevant committee and election rules precisely rather than writing that decentralisation means every decision must be local. It connects with the analytical decentralisation task in the official 2025 GS-II paper.

Question 1 / 5

Under Article 243Q, consider:
1. A Nagar Panchayat is the municipal form for a transitional rural-to-urban area.
2. A Municipal Council is the constitutional municipal form for a larger urban area.
3. The Governor must classify urban areas solely by population, ignoring density, local revenue and non-agricultural employment.
4. A Governor's notified industrial township may fall within the proviso allowing a municipality not to be constituted.
Which statements are correct?

Correct answer: A

A. Correct. Statement 1 identifies the transitional-area form. Statement 4 reflects the industrial-township proviso. Statement 2 confuses a Municipal Council for a smaller urban area with a Municipal Corporation for a larger one. Statement 3 excludes classification factors that Article 243Q expressly includes.

B. Wrong. Statements 1 and 4 are correct, but statement 3 excludes classification factors that Article 243Q expressly includes. Population is not the sole criterion; density, revenue generated for local administration and non-agricultural employment are among the listed factors.

C. Wrong. Statement 1 is correct but the option omits statement 4. The Governor's notified industrial-township proviso can permit a municipality not to be constituted, having regard to the area's size and the municipal services provided or proposed by an industrial establishment, among relevant factors.

D. Wrong. Statements 1 and 4 are correct, but statement 2 confuses the municipal forms. Article 243Q provides a Municipal Corporation for a larger urban area, not a Municipal Council.

Question 2 / 5

Under Article 243X, State legislation may provide for:
1. Municipal taxes subject to specified procedures and limits.
2. Assignment of specified taxes levied and collected by the State Government.
3. Grants-in-aid from the State's Consolidated Fund.
4. Unrestricted municipal taxing power solely because a service appears in the Twelfth Schedule.
Which statements are correct?

Correct answer: D

A. Wrong. Statement 1 is correct, but statement 4 invents an unrestricted taxing power. A listed service does not replace the authorising law, procedures or limits. Statements 2 and 3 are also correct.

B. Wrong. Both listed statements are correct, but this answer omits statement 1. Article 243X permits State law to authorise municipal levies with procedures and limits.

C. Wrong. Statements 1, 2 and 3 describe constitutional financial routes. Statement 4 does not: a Twelfth Schedule entry does not by itself confer an unlimited power to tax.

D. Correct. Article 243X distinguishes municipal levies, assigned State revenues and State grants. Each depends on the legal framework; a service listing is not a free-standing unlimited taxing power.

Question 3 / 5

Article 243R permits State law to provide municipal representation for:
1. Lok Sabha and State Assembly members whose constituencies include all or part of the municipal area.
2. Rajya Sabha and State Legislative Council members registered as electors within the municipal area.
3. Every MP from the State, solely by virtue of membership of Parliament.
A student also claims that Article 243R requires every municipal chairperson to be directly elected by the residents.
Which assessment is correct?

Correct answer: B

A. Wrong. Categories 1 and 2 match the constitutional criteria, but the chairperson claim is false. Article 243R leaves the manner of the chairperson's election to State legislation rather than imposing one direct-election method.

B. Correct. Category 1 uses constituency overlap; category 2 uses registration as an elector within the municipal area. Neither is a blanket entitlement for every MP from the State. State legislation may provide the chairperson-election method, so the universal direct-election claim fails.

C. Wrong. Category 2 qualifies, but category 3 removes the relevant local connection. Category 1 should be included. The option correctly rejects the chairperson claim, but its representation pairing is wrong.

D. Wrong. Category 3 does not qualify solely on the stated ground, and the universal direct-election claim is also false. Categories 1 and 2 are the constitutionally specified routes in this question.

Question 4 / 5

A municipality is dissolved with fourteen months of its original term remaining. Applying Articles 243U and 243ZA, consider:
1. Its replacement election must ordinarily be completed before six months from dissolution expire.
2. The replacement municipality receives a fresh five-year term.
3. Municipal electoral rolls and elections fall under the State Election Commission.
4. An ordinary term's expiry automatically permits the same six-month delay.
Which statements are correct?

Correct answer: C

A. Wrong. Statement 1 is correct, but statement 2 is false. The municipality constituted following premature dissolution serves the original term's unexpired remainder, not a fresh five years. Statement 3 is also correct.

B. Wrong. Statement 3 identifies the right election authority, but statement 2 gives the replacement a term the Constitution does not provide. Statement 1 is also correct.

C. Correct. The short-remainder exception does not apply to the stated fourteen-month remainder. The dissolution election deadline is ordinarily six months, the replacement serves the unexpired remainder, and the State Election Commission is the constitutional election authority.

D. Wrong. Statements 1 and 3 are correct, but statement 4 confuses dissolution with ordinary expiry. The ordinary election must be completed before the term expires.

Question 5 / 5 · HARD

A fictional metropolitan lighting scheme reserves expenditure approval to participating municipalities. Its 30-member Metropolitan Planning Committee has 20 members elected by local residents from elected municipal members and Panchayat chairpersons. It prepares a draft corridor development plan. Assume the required population proportions and all unmentioned requirements are met.
Which proposal repairs the stated election defect while respecting the supplied spending arrangement?

Correct answer: D

A. Wrong. Twenty of thirty already meets the metropolitan two-thirds floor. Twenty-four borrows the district committee's four-fifths number and, more importantly, does not repair the resident-electorate defect. The metropolitan elected share must be elected by as well as from the specified municipal-member/Panchayat-chairperson pool.

B. Wrong. The membership number and electoral pool are repaired, but the proposal also shifts expenditure approval away from the municipalities contrary to the supplied scheme. Article 243ZE's draft-planning role does not by itself create that spending power. No other authority for the transfer is supplied.

C. Wrong. The number and retained municipal spending approval are not the problem. The proposal imports the district-level Panchayat-member pool from Article 243ZD into the metropolitan rule. Article 243ZE instead specifies elected municipal members and Panchayat chairpersons in the metropolitan area.

D. Correct. Twenty of thirty meets the two-thirds metropolitan floor. Election by and from the specified metropolitan eligible pool repairs the defect, unlike election by residents generally. Keeping municipal expenditure approval respects the fictional scheme; preparing the draft development plan does not automatically transfer that power. This answer addresses only the stated defect and supplied spending arrangement, not every possible legal requirement.
