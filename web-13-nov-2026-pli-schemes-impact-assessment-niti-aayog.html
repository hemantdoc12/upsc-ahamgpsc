One news item, fifteen exam angles · 13 Nov 2026 · about eight minutes

Pay for production: NITI Aayog's report card on the PLI schemes

Less to read. More to connect. NITI Aayog has published its assessment of the Production Linked Incentive schemes: Rs 2.4 lakh crore invested and 8.49 lakh jobs by March 2026, with barely a fifth of the outlay spent - by design, not delay. The design, the scorecard, and the 22 bids for battery factories that landed the same day.

Employment is the laggard. Investment has reached 76 per cent of its full-tenure target and production 64 per cent - but employment only 28 per cent. The report calls it sequencing, not failure. The exam calls it a data point.

Fifteen connected angles

01 · News

The report card

On 12 November 2026 NITI Aayog released the 'Impact Assessment of PLI Schemes', prepared in collaboration with IIM Ahmedabad as knowledge partner. It is a comprehensive, sector-wise evaluation of the Production Linked Incentive programme, tracking four outcomes: investment, production, employment and exports.

02 · News

Twenty-two bids for battery factories

The same day, the Ministry of Heavy Industries announced that 22 bids had been received for 10 GWh of Advanced Chemistry Cell (ACC) capacity earmarked for Grid-Scale Stationary Storage (GSSS), tendered under the PLI-ACC scheme. The RFP was floated on the CPP portal on 15 July 2026; bids closed on 10 November and technical bids were opened on 11 November 2026 through a two-stage Quality and Cost Based Selection process.

03 · Economy

The headline numbers

As of March 2026, the 14 PLI schemes have drawn cumulative investment of Rs 2,40,138 crore, generated production and sales of Rs 22,66,412 crore, and created 8,49,069 jobs. The combined financial outlay across the 14 sectors is Rs 1,91,049 crore.

04 · Economy

Only 18.51 per cent spent - by design

Cumulative disbursement stood at Rs 35,354 crore as of March 2026, just 18.51 per cent of the outlay. The report's defence: incentives are not front-loaded but released only as firms meet specified investment and production thresholds. Achievement against the annual disbursement plan has improved from 63 per cent in FY 2023-24 to 71 per cent in FY 2024-25 and 100 per cent in FY 2025-26.

05 · Concept

How PLI actually pays

A firm earns the incentive as a percentage of its incremental sales of manufactured goods over a base year, for a fixed number of years, only after crossing notified threshold investment and production levels. The design rewards performance and scale, not mere entry or promises.

06 · Economy

The 58 per cent test

The portfolio has completed 58 per cent of its aggregate implementation period (578 of 996 months). Against full-tenure targets, achievement stands at 76 per cent for investment, 64 per cent for production and sales, and 28 per cent for employment. The report reads this as the normal sequencing of manufacturing: invest, commission, ramp up production, then hire at scale.

07 · Economy

Four verdicts

Targeted segments are classified into four groups: On-Track, Partial Impact, Insignificant Evidence of Impact, and Data unavailable. Nearly 70 per cent of targeted segments are broadly on track, judged against a 50 per cent mid-course benchmark of full-tenure targets at roughly the halfway point of implementation.

08 · Economy

Winners and slow movers

Strong evidence of progress: large-scale electronics (mobiles), pharmaceuticals drugs, food processing, drones, medical devices and white goods. Encouraging but needing stronger employment, exports and domestic value addition: automobiles and auto components, telecom, and speciality steel. Newer, capital-intensive schemes such as ACC batteries and technical textiles are moving gradually, while solar PV has made significant progress.

09 · Economy

The bigger manufacturing picture

Real manufacturing GVA rose from Rs 34.82 lakh crore in FY 2022-23 to Rs 47.47 lakh crore in FY 2025-26, a CAGR of 10.88 per cent and cumulative growth of 36.3 per cent over the period, according to the report.

10 · History

From mobiles to fourteen sectors

The first PLI scheme, for Large Scale Electronics Manufacturing, was notified in April 2020. Mobile phone production grew roughly thirty-fold, from about Rs 18,000 crore in 2014-15 to Rs 5.45 lakh crore by 2024-25. The programme expanded to 14 sectors; the ACC battery scheme was approved by the Union Cabinet in May 2021 with an outlay of Rs 18,100 crore for 50 GWh. The IT Hardware scheme was redesigned as PLI 2.0 in May 2023 with a Rs 17,000 crore outlay after industry feedback.

11 · Polity

NITI Aayog, the assessor

NITI Aayog is neither a statutory nor a constitutional body: it was created by a Union Cabinet resolution on 1 January 2015, replacing the Planning Commission. Chaired by the Prime Minister, it functions as the government's think tank and runs cooperative-federalism platforms such as the Governing Council. Its Industry and Foreign Investment Division steered this assessment.

12 · Concept

QCBS, the two-stage sieve

Quality and Cost Based Selection is a two-stage procurement method: technical bids are evaluated first, and only the financial bids of technically qualified bidders are opened. It is used where quality matters as much as price, and it is the mechanism MHI is using to select beneficiary firms for the 10 GWh ACC tender.

13 · International

Why now: the GVC opening

The report situates PLI in the ongoing realignment of global value chains, as firms diversify production networks and reduce concentration risks. The scheme is India's bet to emerge as a trusted manufacturing destination and to move from assembly towards deeper domestic value addition, advanced technology and export competitiveness.

14 · Economy

The honest gaps

The assessment is candid about weaknesses. Employment, at 28 per cent of target, lags investment (76 per cent) and production (64 per cent). Disbursement is under a fifth of the outlay, and some targeted segments show insufficient evidence of impact or lack usable data. The report frames these as gestation effects to watch, not verdicts of failure.

15 · Economy

Reward, not protection

The report describes PLI as the mark of an 'enabling State' that builds productive capabilities and rewards performance rather than relying on protection alone. As schemes mature, sunset discipline and the quality of domestic value addition will decide whether incentives create lasting industrial strength or permanent dependence.

Five-question Prelims challenge

The companion reels have no answers. Choose an option here to reveal the correct answer and explanation. These are original practice questions, not past UPSC questions.

Question 1

With reference to the 'Impact Assessment of PLI Schemes' report released by NITI Aayog in November 2026, consider the following statements:

  1. It was prepared in collaboration with the Indian Institute of Management, Ahmedabad.
  2. It found that cumulative investment under the PLI schemes had crossed Rs 2.4 lakh crore as of March 2026.
  3. It classifies all 14 PLI sectors as 'On-Track' on every parameter.

Which of the statements given above is/are correct?

Correct answer: B

Statements 1 and 2 are correct: the report was prepared with IIM Ahmedabad as knowledge partner, and it records cumulative investment of Rs 2,40,138 crore as of March 2026. Statement 3 is incorrect: segments are classified into four groups - On-Track, Partial Impact, Insignificant Evidence of Impact and Data unavailable - with nearly 70 per cent of targeted segments broadly on track.

Source: NITI Aayog, Impact Assessment of PLI Schemes, Nov 2026 (IIM Ahmedabad knowledge partner; cumulative investment Rs 2,40,138 crore; four outcome groups); PIB PRID 2335594, 12 Nov 2026

Question 2

With reference to the PLI scheme for Advanced Chemistry Cell (ACC) battery storage, consider the following statements:

  1. It is implemented by the Ministry of Heavy Industries.
  2. The Union Cabinet approved it in May 2021 with an outlay of Rs 18,100 crore.
  3. It targets a total ACC manufacturing capacity of 10 GWh.

Which of the statements given above is/are correct?

Correct answer: B

Statements 1 and 2 are correct: the Cabinet approved the National Programme on ACC Battery Storage in May 2021 with an outlay of Rs 18,100 crore, and it is run by the Ministry of Heavy Industries. Statement 3 is incorrect: the target is 50 GWh in total; 10 GWh earmarked for Grid-Scale Stationary Storage was the capacity offered in the recent tender, which drew 22 bids.

Source: PIB (MHI), 12 Nov 2026, PRID 2335459 (22 bids for 10 GWh GSSS; two-stage QCBS; May 2021 Cabinet approval; Rs 18,100 crore; 50 GWh total target)

Question 3

With reference to the design of the Production Linked Incentive schemes, consider the following statements:

  1. Incentives are calculated on incremental sales of manufactured goods over a base year.
  2. The entire incentive is disbursed upfront once a firm is approved.
  3. All 14 sector schemes were notified together in April 2020.

Which of the statements given above is/are correct?

Correct answer: A

Only statement 1 is correct: the incentive is paid on incremental sales over a base year, after notified investment and production thresholds are met. Statement 2 is incorrect because incentives are not front-loaded. Statement 3 is incorrect: the first scheme (Large Scale Electronics Manufacturing) was notified in April 2020, and the programme expanded to 14 sectors over the following period, with ACC approved in May 2021.

Source: NITI Aayog, Impact Assessment of PLI Schemes, Nov 2026 (incentive on incremental sales over base year, not front-loaded; PLI-LSEM notified April 2020; ACC approved May 2021)

Question 4

According to the report, as of March 2026, which of the following parameters had the lowest achievement against its full-tenure target under the PLI schemes?

Select the correct option:

Correct answer: B

Employment achievement stood at 28 per cent of the full-tenure target, against 76 per cent for investment and 64 per cent for production and sales. Disbursement against the annual plan in fact reached 100 per cent in FY 2025-26. The report attributes the employment lag to the normal sequencing of manufacturing investments, where fuller hiring follows commissioning and production ramp-up.

Source: NITI Aayog, Impact Assessment of PLI Schemes, Nov 2026 (achievement vs full-tenure targets: investment 76 per cent, production/sales 64 per cent, employment 28 per cent; FY 2025-26 disbursement 100 per cent of annual plan)

Question 5

Consider the following pairs:

  1. Grid-Scale Stationary Storage: the application earmarked for the recently tendered 10 GWh of ACC capacity
  2. Telecom and networking products: classified in the report among the sectors with the strongest evidence of progress across multiple parameters
  3. ACC battery storage: among the sectors notified under PLI in April 2020

How many of the above pairs are correctly matched?

Correct answer: A

Only pair 1 is correctly matched: the tendered 10 GWh is earmarked for Grid-Scale Stationary Storage applications. Pair 2 is wrong: telecom shows encouraging progress but needs strengthening, while the strongest performers include large-scale electronics, pharmaceuticals, food processing, drones, medical devices and white goods. Pair 3 is wrong: the first PLI scheme (Large Scale Electronics Manufacturing) was notified in April 2020, while the ACC battery scheme was approved by the Union Cabinet only in May 2021.

Source: PIB (MHI), 12 Nov 2026, PRID 2335459 (GSSS earmark); NITI Aayog report, Nov 2026 (sector progress groups; LSEM notified April 2020; ACC approved May 2021)
