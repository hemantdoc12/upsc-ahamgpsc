What happened

Primary-source checked

The meeting

The 30th FSDC meeting (23 Oct 2026) noted a Financial Sector Cyber Security Strategy, discussed tokenisation of assets and digital KYC, pushed Account Aggregator and ULI for MSMEs, and urged faster refunds of unclaimed assets (PIB).

Cyber and infrastructure

The Council reviewed the cyber resilience of the financial sector, took note of the Financial Sector Cyber Security Strategy (FSCSS) formulated by an Inter-Ministerial Group of financial regulators and cybersecurity agencies with the DEA, DFS and MeitY, and discussed the protection of Critical Information Infrastructure and the cyber preparedness of Regional Rural Banks.

Innovation and inclusion

On the agenda: fintech innovation and tokenisation of financial and real-world assets; investment flows including into GIFT-IFSC; simpler digital KYC and onboarding for NRIs and FPIs; scaling the Account Aggregator and the Unified Lending Interface with an MSME focus; and rationalising credit guarantee schemes.

Unclaimed assets and risks

The FM urged faster refunds of unclaimed deposits, dividends, shares, post office accounts, insurance and pension funds, noting the district-level "Your Money, Your Right" campaign. The Council flagged geopolitical uncertainty, the AI boom and a steep rise in global bond yields, and the FM asked for more frequent meetings on a predefined schedule.

The hub: one council of regulators ties cyber resilience, tokenised finance and forgotten money to financial stability.

Six essay seeds

Themes this one item opens. Not topics to write to; places to start thinking.

  1. Stability at the speed of code

    Tokenised assets and instant credit promise efficiency; a breach can now trigger a bank run. Can regulation run at the speed of code?

  2. The unclaimed billions

    Deposits, dividends and pensions sit in limbo while owners stay untraced. What does a refund campaign say about institutional trust?

  3. A council without teeth

    The FSDC convenes powerful regulators but is not a statutory body. Does coordination need law, or is convening power enough?

  4. Whose token is it anyway

    Tokenisation turns real assets into code and promises liquidity. When the asset is code, who guards the guard?

  5. Cyber as capital

    A cyber strategy for finance treats resilience like capital adequacy. Should cyber strength be measured and mandated like capital?

  6. Rails as public goods

    Account Aggregator and ULI are state-built rails on which private credit runs. When the state builds rails, who captures the value?

Today's MCQ: answer and why

One Prelims-style question on today's item. This is the only drafted question on the page. The carousel slide shows the question. The key lives here.

With reference to the Financial Stability and Development Council (FSDC), consider the following statements:

  1. It is chaired by the Union Finance Minister.
  2. It is a statutory body established by an Act of Parliament.
  3. Its Sub-Committee is chaired by the Governor of the RBI.
  4. CERT-In is not associated with its work.

Answer: (a) 1 and 3 only

(a) is correct: the FSDC is chaired by the Union Finance Minister, and its Sub-Committee is chaired by the RBI Governor (PIB).

(b) includes false statement 2: the FSDC is not a statutory body; it was created by a government resolution in 2010 (static: check).

(c) omits true statement 1 and includes false statement 4: CERT-In is associated with the FSDC’s work - its Director General attended the 30th meeting (PIB).

(d) includes both false statements 2 and 4. Sources: PIB release of 23 October 2026; FSDC background is static knowledge (check).
