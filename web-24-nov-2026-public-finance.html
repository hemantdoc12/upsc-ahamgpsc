Public finance and service affordability

The lamps are bought. Who pays to keep them on?

A fictional lighting-service file separates purchase, running costs, grant conditions and annual affordability.

All grant terms, amounts, options and municipal files are fictional. No actual tariff, city performance, spending power or procurement award is asserted. Historical NMAM accounting concepts, explicit Union Budget classification and current institutional-page examples retain their separate scopes. The lamps are bought. Is the service funded?

Separate the physical asset, the accounting character of each outlay, the source of funds, restrictions on their use and the time at which cash is available. 02 / 15

Classify the work, not the size of its bill

In the National Municipal Accounts Manual, capital expenditure generally adds fixed-asset units or improves an existing asset's capacity, efficiency, lifespan or economy of operation. Revenue expenditure benefits the current period. Thus a small new asset and a large current electricity bill need not share the same classification. Amount alone cannot decide the category. Apply the relevant accounting policy in an actual institution rather than treating this historical glossary as current State spending law.

National Municipal Accounts Manual, Ministry of Urban Development

03 / 15

Repair is not a complete accounting description

Routine maintenance that sustains current operation differs from an eligible improvement that lengthens useful life or raises capacity. The word repair does not tell us which has occurred. Ask what work was performed, what benefit it produces and what the applicable capitalisation policy requires. The manual supplies the conceptual distinction; it does not mean that every replacement, every long-lasting benefit or every work labelled an upgrade must automatically be capitalised.

National Municipal Accounts Manual, Ministry of Urban Development

04 / 15

A budget is not a bank balance

A budget is a financial plan. A receipt is evidence that money arrived, and a payment is a further event. The official Amdavad Municipal Corporation (AMC) finance page describes budget and cash-flow checks separately when bills are forwarded for payment. In an invented file, a budget provision of Rs1 lakh therefore does not prove receipt or payment of Rs1 lakh. This is not evidence that AMC has delayed a payment, nor a universal legal rule that cash must precede every contract or bill.

Amdavad Municipal Corporation, About AMC Finance · National Municipal Accounts Manual, Ministry of Urban Development

05 / 15

An unpaid bill can still matter in accrual accounts

The manual defines accrual recognition by when revenues are earned and costs incurred, not simply by when cash moves. An unpaid bill cannot be described as a cash payment merely because it is recorded or approved. Equally, an absence of cash payment does not by itself prove that no expense or liability can be recognised. The exact accounting entry depends on what was received, the obligation and the applicable policy. Keep cash-payment questions separate from accrual-recognition questions.

06 / 15

Trace the source before using the balance

A tax receipt, a grant and borrowed money are not interchangeable descriptions of incoming cash. Article 243X provides a State-law framework for municipal taxes, assigned revenues, grants and funds. It does not itself authorise any municipality to invent any tax, and it is not a direct municipal borrowing power. For a real transaction, the applicable law, sanction and conditions still matter. Here the grant restrictions and borrowing examples are supplied case facts, not an account of Gujarat law.

07 / 15

A purchase grant need not pay the electricity bill

Suppose a grant letter in our fictional file permits only the acquisition of new lighting units. Cash received under that letter is not thereby unrestricted operating money. Electricity and ordinary maintenance still need an eligible funding source. We do not claim that every real grant excludes maintenance: another grant could permit it. The inference comes from this letter's stated terms. If conditions may be changed, obtain the required lawful change rather than silently assuming permission.

National Municipal Accounts Manual, Ministry of Urban Development

08 / 15

Borrowing moves cash and creates an obligation

A loan can supply cash now while leaving repayment obligations for later. That is different from assuming a non-repayable grant. The 2026-27 Union Budget guide classifies loans raised by the Union Government among its capital receipts. That Union accounting description does not establish a municipality's power to borrow. A municipal repayment plan must consider the amount, timing and terms actually applicable. This lesson neither guarantees repayment from an unspecified tax nor infers that all debt-financed assets are bad investments. It asks whether the future obligation was included in the service-financing picture.

National Municipal Accounts Manual, Ministry of Urban Development · Ministry of Finance, Key to Budget Documents 2026-2027

09 / 15

Purchase price is only one part of ownership cost

The Department of Expenditure's 2024 goods manual includes acquisition, operation, maintenance and disposal in its total-cost-of-ownership concept. A price-only comparison may therefore omit costs the purchaser will bear later. In a real evaluation, specify the service requirement, comparison horizon, relevant costs, risks and permitted evaluation method. The concept is not a legal instruction to override a particular municipal tender or retrospectively change its award criteria.

Department of Expenditure, Manual for Procurement of Goods, Second Edition 2024

Compare the same service for the same period

Option B costs Rs1.25 lakh plus Rs10,000 each year: Rs1.55 lakh. B is Rs5,000 lower on these inputs. This is not yet a complete lifetime appraisal: service quality, useful life, disposal, financing, inflation, discounting and omitted risks are not established merely by that arithmetic.

11 / 15

A grant changes who pays, not the price of the resources

If an outside grant pays a purchase bill, the municipality's own upfront contribution may fall. The lamps and their running requirements have not become resource-free. Keep total listed project cost, the local contribution and the annual funding gap in separate columns. The same project can look attractive from one column and difficult from another. In the fictional numerical exercise, grant funding and operating resources are explicitly specified so that none of those columns is silently reused.

12 / 15

A three-year total can hide an annual funding gap

Adding a project's outlays over several years does not establish that eligible cash is available when each payment is due. A purchase-only grant cannot fill an annual operating shortfall just because the total grant looks large. Conversely, a recurring allocation does not prove that an upfront capital contribution is already available. In this lesson's HARD case, test purchase funding and annual operating capacity separately before comparing the total listed three-year costs.

13 / 15

An asset built with a grant is not always capital expenditure in the grantor's books

The 2026-27 Union Budget guide expressly treats grants to States, Union Territories and other parties as revenue expenditure in the Union Government's books, even where a grantee uses them to create capital assets. The physical existence of an asset therefore does not settle the grantor's accounting classification. The scope matters: this is not a rule for recognising a grant receipt in a municipality's accounts or for classifying its own acquisition.

Ministry of Finance, Key to Budget Documents 2026-2027

14 / 15

Capital payments extend beyond visible construction

The same Union guide includes acquisition of assets, investments in shares and loans or advances to States, Union Territories and other parties within capital payments. A definition limited to new roads and buildings is therefore too narrow for a Union Budget question. Do not replace the tested government-accounting category with the everyday meaning of construction. Equally, distinguish this Union classification from the historical municipal fixed-asset glossary used earlier.

Turn the file into an argument, not a slogan

A compact Mains response can start with the service obligation, separate asset acquisition from continuing operation, identify each lawful funding source and its restrictions, test timing and annual affordability, and then compare like-for-like service options. Conclude with the missing evidence or conditional recommendation. Neither capital spending is always better nor revenue spending is wasteful follows from these accounts. Adequately maintained assets need current operating resources; financial feasibility still does not by itself prove actual service quality.

Five Prelims questions

Questions 1-2 form Prelims 1/3; questions 3-4 form Prelims 2/3; question 5 alone is HARD. Choose first, then read the explanation for every option.

Question 1 / 5

Using the National Municipal Accounts Manual's accounting concepts, consider:
1. Adding new fixed-asset units is generally capital expenditure.
2. An electricity bill must be capital expenditure if it exceeds the price of new lamps.
3. Every repair must be revenue expenditure, even an eligible useful-life improvement.
4. An eligible improvement extending an existing asset's useful life can be capitalised.
Which statements are correct?

Correct answer: B

A. Wrong. Statement 1 is correct, but statement 2 substitutes bill size for the nature of expenditure. The option also omits correct statement 4, concerning eligible useful-life improvements.

B. Correct. Statement 1 reflects adding fixed-asset units, and statement 4 reflects an eligible useful-life improvement. Statement 2 is false because relative bill size does not decide classification. Statement 3 is false because the label repair cannot override the substance of an eligible capital improvement. Actual capitalisation remains subject to the applicable policy.

C. Wrong. Statement 4 is correct, but the option omits correct statement 1. Adding new fixed-asset units is generally capital expenditure under the manual's concept; useful-life improvement is not its only route.

D. Wrong. Statements 1 and 4 are correct, but statement 3 wrongly makes every repair revenue expenditure. Routine maintenance differs from an eligible improvement that extends useful life. The name repair alone does not settle classification.

Question 2 / 5

Under the 2026-27 Union Budget guide, consider:
1. A Union grant to a State can be revenue expenditure even when the State creates capital assets with it.
2. Every payment producing a physical asset anywhere must be capital expenditure in the Union's books.
3. Union loans and advances to States are included in capital payments.
Which statements are correct?

Correct answer: C

A. Wrong. Statement 1 is correct, but the Union guide also includes loans and advances to States in capital payments, making statement 3 correct.

B. Wrong. Statement 3 is correct, but statement 2 ignores the explicit treatment of grants creating grantee assets as Union revenue expenditure. Statement 1 is also omitted.

C. Correct. The guide explicitly supports both the grantor-book distinction in statement 1 and the inclusion of loans and advances in statement 3. Statement 2 wrongly substitutes an asset-anywhere rule for the stated Union accounting treatment.

D. Wrong. Statements 1 and 3 are correct, but statement 2 contradicts the grant treatment in statement 1 and the official guide.

Question 3 / 5

A fictional municipal file records a budget provision, a received purchase-only grant and an unpaid supplier bill. Consider:
1. Budget provision alone proves that the corresponding cash has arrived.
2. Recording the bill alone does not prove a cash payment.
3. Grant receipt alone makes its balance available for any operating expense.
4. An unpaid bill may still give rise to a recognised liability under accrual accounting.
Which statements are correct?

Correct answer: A

A. Correct. Statement 2 separates a recorded bill from evidence of cash payment. Statement 4 recognises that an obligation may be recognised under accrual accounting before cash is paid. Statement 1 confuses provision with receipt, while statement 3 disregards the supplied purchase-only condition. The actual entry depends on the underlying transaction and policy.

B. Wrong. Statement 2 is correct, but statement 1 turns a budget provision into evidence that cash arrived. The option also omits correct statement 4: absence of payment does not itself preclude accrual recognition of a liability.

C. Wrong. Statements 2 and 4 are correct, but statement 3 treats restricted grant money as available for any operating use. Cash arrival does not remove the supplied grant restriction.

D. Wrong. Statement 4 is correct, but the option omits correct statement 2. A bill's recording alone is not proof that it has been paid; liability recognition and cash payment are distinct.

Question 4 / 5

Two fictional lighting options are compared over three years:
A: Rs1 lakh purchase plus Rs20,000 each year.
B: Rs1.25 lakh purchase plus Rs10,000 each year.
Only those costs are supplied; service quality and useful life are not established. Which conclusion is justified?

Correct answer: D

A. Wrong. A's lower purchase price is real but does not settle three-year outlays or service quality. A totals Rs1.60 lakh, compared with B's Rs1.55 lakh on the supplied figures.

B. Wrong. B totals Rs1.55 lakh and A Rs1.60 lakh, a difference of Rs5,000, not Rs25,000. The Rs25,000 figure is B's additional purchase cost, not its three-year saving.

C. Wrong. The supplied horizon is three years, not an established whole useful life. Relevant costs and quality evidence may be missing. A simple listed-outlay sum cannot prove complete lifetime cost.

D. Correct. A totals Rs1,00,000 + 3 x Rs20,000 = Rs1,60,000. B totals Rs1,25,000 + 3 x Rs10,000 = Rs1,55,000. The Rs5,000 difference is established, but suitability and full-life value are not.

Question 5 / 5 · HARD

A fictional town compares equally suitable lamps for three years. A Rs1 lakh grant is already received. It may fund all or part of either purchase, combined with local purchase funds, but no running costs. Local funds are Rs20,000 for purchase and Rs15,000 yearly for running.
A: purchase Rs1 lakh; running Rs30,000 yearly.
B: purchase Rs1.20 lakh; running Rs10,000 yearly.
No other funds or costs apply; ignore discounting. Which assessment is correct?

Correct answer: B

A. Wrong. The grant covers A's purchase, but its Rs30,000 annual running requirement exceeds the identified Rs15,000 recurring funds by Rs15,000 each year. Upfront grant coverage does not establish continuing affordability.

B. Correct. B uses the Rs1 lakh grant plus Rs20,000 local purchase funds, exactly within the capital limit. Its Rs10,000 annual running requirement is within Rs15,000. Total listed project outlays are A: Rs1,00,000 + 3 x Rs30,000 = Rs1,90,000; B: Rs1,20,000 + 3 x Rs10,000 = Rs1,50,000. B is Rs40,000 lower. The grant changes local financing, not these total project costs.

C. Wrong. B does fit the identified funding limits, but Rs5,000 is the unused portion of the annual Rs15,000 allocation after paying Rs10,000. It is not B's running cost.

D. Wrong. A's purchase is covered, but its annual running cost breaches the recurring limit. A also has Rs40,000 higher, not Rs20,000 lower, total listed three-year cost. The purchase-price difference alone is not the three-year comparison.
