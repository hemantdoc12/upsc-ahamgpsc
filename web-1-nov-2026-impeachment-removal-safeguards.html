Impeachment and removal: how the Constitution protects five offices

Today is a concept build, not a news event: how the Constitution removes its highest officeholders - President, judges, CAG, Election Commission, UPSC - and why the removal routes differ by office - and which offices share one (Arts 61, 124(4), 148(1), 324(5), 317).

One design question runs through five offices: how do you let Parliament remove a President, a judge, the CAG, the CEC or a UPSC member without letting a passing majority own them? The Constitution answers with different doors - an impeachment charge for the President, an address after proved misbehaviour or incapacity for judges and the CAG, a court inquiry for UPSC, and a recommendation lock for the election commissioners. Each angle is a doorway, not a question.

The design

Today is a concept build, not a news event: how the Constitution removes its highest officeholders - President, judges, CAG, Election Commission, UPSC - and why the removal routes differ by office - and which offices share one (Arts 61, 124(4), 148(1), 324(5), 317).

The President: impeachment, Article 61

The President can be removed only by impeachment for violation of the Constitution. A written charge, signed by at least one-fourth of a House's total membership after fourteen days' notice, must pass that House by two-thirds of its total membership; the other House investigates (the President may appear or be represented), and if it also resolves by two-thirds of its total membership, the removal takes effect from the resolution's date (Art 61).

Judges, CAG, CEC: the same address

A Supreme Court or High Court judge, the CAG and the CEC are removed on the ground of proved misbehaviour or incapacity after each House passes an address in the same session, supported by a majority of the House's total membership and two-thirds of members present and voting (Arts 124(4), 218, 148(1), 324(5)). The Judges (Inquiry) Act, 1968, made under Article 124(5), supplies the investigation: a motion by 100 Lok Sabha or 50 Rajya Sabha members, admitted or refused by the presiding officer, then a three-member committee - a Supreme Court judge, a High Court Chief Justice and a distinguished jurist.

UPSC and the election commissioners

A UPSC member can be removed for misbehaviour only after the President refers the matter to the Supreme Court and the Court reports, following an inquiry, that the member ought to be removed (Art 317). Removal is by the President, never the Governor; during the reference the President (for a State Commission, the Governor) may suspend. Election commissioners and regional commissioners can be removed only on the CEC's recommendation (Art 324(5) second proviso) - the lock that keeps the multi-member commission under one shield.

The hub: five independent offices, one safeguard in different shapes - independence is protected by making removal hard, rare and multi-stage, while keeping it possible.

The balance problem

High offices must answer for misconduct, yet stay fearless. The Constitution's answer: make removal possible but deliberately hard - special majorities, independent inquiry, final orders, not a simple vote (Arts 61, 124(4)).

Today's MCQ: answer and why

One Prelims-style question on today's item. This is the only drafted question on the page. The carousel slide shows the question. The key lives here.

With reference to the removal of constitutional functionaries in India, consider the following statements:

  1. The CAG can be removed from office in like manner and on like grounds as a Judge of the Supreme Court.
  2. A member of a State Public Service Commission can be removed by the Governor on the ground of misbehaviour after an inquiry by the Supreme Court.
  3. An Election Commissioner other than the CEC can be removed only on the recommendation of the Chief Election Commissioner.
  4. A charge for the impeachment of the President can be preferred only in the Lok Sabha.

Answer: (a) 1 and 3 only

(a) is correct: the CAG is removed in the same manner and on the same grounds as a Supreme Court judge (Art 148(1)), and election commissioners and regional commissioners can be removed only on the CEC's recommendation (Art 324(5) second proviso).

(b) and (c) include false statement 2: a State Public Service Commission member is removed by the President, not the Governor; the Governor may only suspend during the Article 317 reference.

(d) includes false statement 4: the impeachment charge against the President can be preferred by either House, not only the Lok Sabha (Art 61(1)). Sources: Constitution of India, Articles 61, 148(1), 317, 324(5) (legislative.gov.in).
