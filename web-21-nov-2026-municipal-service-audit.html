Municipal water-service audit · Five questions, fifteen connected angles

The dashboard is green. Is the water service working?

Audit municipal water-service indicators, measurement methods, legal duties and complaint redressal.

All town figures and cases are hypothetical. No allegation about a real municipality. Service benchmarks, measurement reliability and legal duties are distinct. A green indicator alone proves neither universal success nor misconduct. State-specific duties require applicable law.

Fifteen connections

01 / 15

Start with a service question, not a colour

A hypothetical town reports a green water-service dashboard. Ask which service it measures: connections, continuity, quality or complaint redressal. The Service Level Benchmarking handbook treats these as separate indicators. A green connection-coverage indicator may accurately describe access to direct connections while leaving continuity and drinking-water quality to their own tests. This lesson is an audit exercise, not a finding about any real municipality.

Turn the shared water chain into a Mains answer

Use the week's runoff, groundwater, drinking-water, sanitation and school-services learning as one governance problem. For a Mains answer, move from the physical mechanism to the service affected, then the assigned institution, measurable result and corrective action. For example, identifying a sanitation-chain health risk calls for risk-based management and monitoring, not merely counting constructed toilets. This answer structure is a teaching synthesis; WHO supplies the chain-based risk-management approach.

03 / 15

Separate money, assets and service

Money spent is an input. A completed pipe or treatment plant is an asset or output. Delivery to users is a service result. An audit needs these distinct lines: expenditure against sanctioned work, functioning assets, and measured service. The handbook explains why investment alone is insufficient to establish service improvement. In the hypothetical town, the next question after pipe completion is therefore what changed in household connections, supply hours or quality, rather than treating construction as the final outcome.

04 / 15

Distinguish connection coverage from receipt of supply

The handbook defines direct-connection coverage as connected households divided by all households in the service area. It counts households, not merely connection accounts; an apartment common connection can serve several households. Public standposts and tankers are excluded from this particular numerator. In a town of 200 households with 100 directly connected, connection coverage is 50 percent. If 90 connected households receive a stated supply, receipt is 90 percent of connected households but 45 percent of all households. These are three different indicators, not rival answers to one question.

05 / 15

Keep zone results beside the city average

For continuity, the handbook uses the operational zone or District Metering Area (DMA), with zone figures averaged into city data. Its minimum measurement jurisdiction is Zone/DMA. A municipal ward is an administrative geography and should not be silently substituted for a hydraulic zone. A city average can summarise different zone results without showing that supply is equal in each zone. Keep the underlying geography and zone values visible when selecting corrective work.

06 / 15

Define a supply hour before counting it

Under the handbook, continuity is average hours of pressurised supply per day, with at least 7 metres of head at the connection's ferrule or meter point. Measure each operational zone/DMA continuously for seven days; use the seven-day average for that month and average zone figures for city data. Exclude hours below the required pressure. A running pump or open valve alone therefore does not establish an hour of qualifying consumer-end supply.

07 / 15

Read reliability grades indicator by indicator

The handbook separates reported performance from reliability of measurement. D is lowest and A highest/preferred; intermediate B and C depend on the indicator, and continuity has no applicable C grade. Continuity grade B uses detailed valve-operation records but assumes adequate consumer-end pressure. Grade A also assesses consumer-end pressure and supply hours using a statistically valid sample survey across all zones. A identifies the stronger measurement method, not a higher supply figure or proof of chemical and microbial safety.

08 / 15

Make before-and-after comparisons comparable

The handbook calls for consistent, repeated measurement. A practical analytical inference is to check the definition, population, geography, period and collection method before attributing a changed percentage to service improvement. If a new survey replaces an estimate, explain that measurement break alongside the reported change. The newer figure may be better evidence, but subtracting the old value from it cannot by itself isolate the effect of an operational repair.

09 / 15

Separate preventive management from surveillance

WHO's safe-drinking-water framework has complementary functions: health-based standards and targets, water safety plans, and independent surveillance. Water safety plans manage risks across the supply system. Independent surveillance checks that plans are effective and targets are met. In the audit, ask separately for the operator's risk-control records and the relevant independent oversight evidence, rather than treating either function as a substitute for the other.

10 / 15

Calculate redressal from timely satisfactory resolution

The handbook's complaint indicator is b/a multiplied by 100: a is all water-supply complaints received in the month; b is those satisfactorily redressed within 24 hours or the next working day, within that month. The complainant should endorse satisfactory resolution in writing on the tracking proforma. A portal closure timestamp is insufficient on its own. The handbook says the benchmark may be set at 80 percent; this is a suggested benchmark, not an automatic legal entitlement. Late endorsed repairs remain outside this timely-redressal numerator.

11 / 15

Audit the complaint system as well as its result

The handbook calls for effective, accessible complaint channels. Its complaint reliability grade B allows multiple channels but lacks systems to aggregate, sort and track complaints, using some months' data to report trends for others. Grade A requires categorisation, collation, daily tracking, status records and consumer endorsement on the municipal proforma. These are measurement-system properties, not proof that 80 percent was achieved. Separately, checking whether barriers deter reporting is an analytical inference from the accessibility requirement, not a finding that barriers exist.

12 / 15

Read devolution through the applicable State law

Twelfth Schedule item 5 is water supply for domestic, industrial and commercial purposes; item 6 covers public health, sanitation conservancy and solid waste management. Under Article 243 W, the State Legislature may, by law, endow municipalities with powers and authority. The law may devolve powers and responsibilities subject to specified conditions, including functions and schemes entrusted in relation to Schedule matters. Thus the Schedule alone does not identify every local operator. Check the applicable State law and allocation, including any legally assigned board or other agency.

13 / 15

Use a water balance to choose coordinated action

AMRUT 2.0's operational guidelines distinguish City Water Balance Plans from City Water Action Plans: the balance exercise helps identify service gaps and potential projects, while the action plan develops the proposed response. This illustrates why sources, coverage, losses and service gaps belong in one planning exercise rather than unrelated asset lists. It does not merge operating and oversight duties. Nor does citing this design establish current funding availability or results for a particular project.

14 / 15

Prioritise health risks across the sanitation chain

WHO sanitation safety planning identifies and prioritises health risks across toilet, containment or storage/treatment, conveyance, treatment, and end use or disposal. It guides management and investment and selects operational monitoring and regulatory oversight priorities. For the hypothetical town, first locate the risk in that chain, then choose a check or corrective action aimed at it. This adds a health-risk priority to the service audit without making an unsupported accusation about an institution.

15 / 15

Close the audit with an action-and-evidence table

For each finding, record the indicator and denominator, place and period, measurement method, assigned function, proposed action and follow-up evidence. A low continuity result calls for investigating operations or network constraints; weak measurement calls for better data. They may coexist but require different actions. An overdue complaint needs both the service response and verified closure evidence. This table is a practical audit synthesis, not a separate handbook rule. It connects devolution, measurement, oversight and feedback in a Mains conclusion.

Five Prelims questions

Questions 1-2 form Prelims 1/3, 3-4 form Prelims 2/3, and question 5 is HARD. Choose first, then examine every option.

Question 1 / 5

The Twelfth Schedule includes water supply. Which approach best identifies the legally assigned water-network operator in a particular town under Article 243 W?

Correct answer: C

A. Wrong. The Schedule lists matters; Article 243 W allows endowment by State law subject to conditions, rather than automatically assigning every operational duty.

B. Wrong. State legislative competence does not establish that the State government must itself run every network or that municipalities have no assigned role.

C. Correct. Article 243 W is permissive and conditional. The applicable State law and allocation must establish who has the particular operating duty.

D. Wrong. A complaint channel is evidence about reporting, not by itself the legal allocation of the operating duty.

Question 2 / 5

A hypothetical service area has 200 households; 100 have direct household network connections. A survey finds that 90 of these connected households received the stated daily supply, and no other households did. What are direct-connection coverage, receipt among connected households, and receipt among all households, respectively?

Correct answer: A

A. Correct. Connection coverage is 100/200; receipt among connected households is 90/100; receipt among all households is 90/200. These give 50, 90 and 45 percent.

B. Wrong. The receipt ratios are reversed: the connected-group denominator is 100, not 200.

C. Wrong. It substitutes receipt among connected households for the handbook's connection-coverage ratio.

D. Wrong. The final ratio uses the connected count of 100 rather than the 90 households receiving the stated supply.

Question 3 / 5

A water complaint was marked closed within 24 hours. The utility's tracking proforma has no complainant's written endorsement of satisfactory resolution. When testing the Service Level Benchmarking handbook's complaint-redressal standard, which conclusion is justified?

Correct answer: D

A. Wrong. A timely closure timestamp does not replace written complainant endorsement of satisfactory resolution.

B. Wrong. Reliability of the complaint-record system and the percentage of timely satisfactory resolutions are distinct; A does not establish achievement of 80 percent.

C. Wrong. The complaint was received and belongs in the denominator. Missing endorsement concerns evidence for the numerator, not whether a complaint was received.

D. Correct. The handbook requires timely satisfactory resolution with written complainant endorsement on the tracking proforma. The record gap alone does not prove that no repair occurred.

Question 4 / 5

Two continuity reports use detailed valve-operation records. P assumes adequate pressure at consumers' premises. Q also assesses consumer-end pressure and supply hours through a statistically valid sample across all zones. Which pairing follows the handbook's continuity reliability table?

Correct answer: B

A. Wrong. P assumes consumer pressure; that is grade B, not A.

B. Correct. These are the handbook's B and A methods respectively. Better measurement can confirm, increase or reduce a reported supply figure.

C. Wrong. Continuity has no applicable C grade, and grade A specifies a statistically valid sample across all zones rather than a household census.

D. Wrong. The grades are correctly identified, but reliability is not the level of service. Q need not show more supply hours.

Question 5 / 5 · HARD

In one month, Zone E receives 80 water complaints; 64 are satisfactorily resolved with written complainant endorsement within 24 hours. Zone W receives 240 complaints; 120 meet that condition, another 24 are satisfactorily resolved and endorsed on the next working day after a holiday, and 48 only later. All these events occur within the month. These are the city's only zones and complaints. Applying the handbook, consider:
1. The city's timely-redressal indicator is 65 percent.
2. Excluding W's next-working-day cases gives the correct city indicator of 57.5 percent.
3. Averaging the two zone percentages gives the correct city complaint indicator of 70 percent.
4. Falling below the handbook's suggested 80 percent benchmark does not, by itself, establish breach of a legal entitlement.
Which statements are correct?

Correct answer: B

A. Wrong. Statement 1 is correct: (64 + 120 + 24)/(80 + 240) = 208/320 = 65 percent. Statement 3 gives equal weight to zones with unequal complaint counts.

B. Correct. Statement 1 includes timely endorsed next-working-day cases, excludes later resolutions, and divides by all complaints received. Statement 4 respects the handbook's suggested benchmark rather than treating it as a legal entitlement.

C. Wrong. Statement 4 is correct, but statement 2 wrongly excludes the 24 endorsed next-working-day resolutions permitted by the handbook. The 48 later cases are excluded from the timely numerator.

D. Wrong. Statement 4 is correct, but statement 3 averages 80 percent in E and 60 percent in W without weighting by complaints. The city indicator is total qualifying resolutions divided by total complaints, not the simple mean of zone rates.
