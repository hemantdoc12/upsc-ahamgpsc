Question 1

With reference to constitutional adoption and commencement, consider:

1. Constitution Day commemorates adoption on 26 November 1949.

2. Every provision first came into force on 26 January 1950.

3. Article 394 provided immediate commencement for specified provisions.

4. The 2015 notification made 26 November a universal bank holiday.

Which statements are correct?

Correct answer: C

A. Wrong. Statement 1 is correct, but 2 ignores Article 394 immediate-commencement exceptions. The option also omits correct 3.

B. Wrong. Statement 3 correctly identifies the immediate-commencement exceptions, but statement 2 contradicts those exceptions. This option also omits correct statement 1.

C. Correct. The notification supports 1; Article 394 supports 3 and contradicts 2. Statement 4 is outside what the observance notification establishes.

D. Wrong. Statements 1 and 3 are correct, but adding 4 imports a holiday consequence not established by the notification.

Question 2

Using the express wording of the Constitution, consider:

1. Article 14 uses any person.

2. Article 19(1) begins all citizens.

3. Article 21 uses no person.

4. These provisions establish that every Fundamental Right is citizen-only.

Which statements are correct?

Correct answer: B

A. Wrong. Statement 1 is correct, but 4 contradicts the person wording in 14 and 21. It also omits correct 2 and 3.

B. Correct. Statements 1-3 track the selected clauses; their different addressees defeat the universal citizen-only conclusion in 4.

C. Wrong. Statements 2 and 3 are correct, but Article 14 also expressly uses any person, making 1 correct.

D. Wrong. The first three textual statements are correct; 4 incorrectly universalises citizenship despite their differences.

Question 3

Concerning speech and peaceful assembly under Article 19, consider:

2. Public order appears in both 19(2) and 19(3).

3. Defamation is separately listed in 19(3) as a ground restricting assembly.

4. The restriction clauses 19(2) and 19(3) are textually identical.

Which statements are correct?

Correct answer: A

A. Correct. 1 matches the assembly right; 2 matches both restriction clauses. Defamation is listed in 19(2), not 19(3), and the clauses differ, defeating 3 and 4.

B. Wrong. 1 is correct, but 3 copies a speech-ground into the assembly clause; 2 is also omitted.

C. Wrong. 2 is correct, but 4 ignores their different enumerated grounds and scope. 1 is also omitted.

D. Wrong. 1 and 2 are correct, but adding 4 wrongly treats the clauses as identical.

Which pairing best matches the constitutional text?

Question 1

Under the Solid Waste Management Rules, 2026, consider:

1. Source segregation includes wet, dry, sanitary and special care streams.

2. Dry waste contains only recyclable material.

3. Used sanitary waste is to be securely wrapped and stored separately.

4. Separation at source permits remixing during transport.

Which statements are correct?

Correct answer: A

A. Correct. Statements 1 and 3 match the four-stream and sanitary-wrapping requirements. Statement 2 excludes non-recyclable dry waste wrongly; statement 4 contradicts transport segregation.

B. Wrong. Statement 1 is correct, but dry waste includes non-recyclable material, defeating statement 2. The option also omits correct statement 3.

C. Wrong. Statement 3 correctly requires secure separate handling of sanitary waste, but statement 2 wrongly excludes non-recyclable material from dry waste. The option also omits correct statement 1.

D. Wrong. Statements 1 and 3 are correct, but adding 4 introduces the false permission to remix during transport.

Question 2

An aerobic composting bed becomes waterlogged. Which explanation best connects the observed condition with its biological process?

Correct answer: C

A. Wrong. Oxygen can dissolve in water, but that fact does not establish improved oxygen supply in a waterlogged compost bed. Excess water can obstruct the air spaces relevant here.

B. Wrong. Reduced oxygen does not establish an enclosed, controlled digestion system, gas capture or appropriate residual handling.

C. Correct. Microbes need moisture, yet excessive moisture can fill air spaces and encourage anaerobic conditions. The explanation concerns the specified aerobic process, not a universal rule for every decomposition method.

D. Wrong. The manual explicitly identifies moisture as necessary for microbial activity. The problem is excessive moisture, not the existence of moisture.

Question 3

For controlled aerobic composting and biomethanation, consider:

1. Both rely on microbial action on suitable organic matter.

2. Aerobic composting takes place in oxygen.

3. Biomethanation produces methane-rich biogas.

4. Slurry leaving a biogas plant is automatically suitable for unrestricted discharge.

Which statements are correct?

Correct answer: D

A. Wrong. Statements 1 and 2 are correct, but the option omits the methane-rich biogas in statement 3.

B. Wrong. Statement 2 is correct, but 4 makes an unsupported unrestricted-discharge claim and omits correct 1 and 3.

C. Wrong. Statements 1 and 3 are correct, but 4 wrongly removes treatment and use conditions; statement 2 is also omitted.

D. Correct. Statements 1-3 describe the controlled biological processes. Statement 4 fails because residual slurry still requires appropriate treatment and handling for its intended use.

Question 4

A fictional waste facility diverts external stormwater, uses a liner, and collects leachate for treatment. Which inference is justified?

Correct answer: B

A. Wrong. A liner is a containment barrier, not a treatment process that removes every dissolved pollutant.

B. Correct. Diversion limits contact, lining supports containment and collection enables treatment. Actual performance and compliance still need evidence.

C. Wrong. Collection gathers the contaminated liquid; it does not itself treat it. Schedule II(D) separately requires management including collection and treatment.

D. Wrong. Dilution is not proof of pollutant removal. The rules require treatment of the mixed water when runoff mixes with leachate or solid waste.

Question 5

A fictional dry-waste batch has 80 tonnes dispatched. Matched receipts account for 68 tonnes. Of the received mass, 75% is sorted as recyclable; conversion records verify new material equal to 80% of that recyclable fraction. These are successive fractions of the same batch. Which assessment is correct?

Correct answer: A

A. Correct. Received 68; recyclable 68 x 0.75=51; verified conversion 51 x 0.80=40.8 tonnes. Relative to dispatch 40.8/80=51%. Unmatched receipts 80-68=12 tonnes. Their cause and the destination of other fractions require evidence; no dumping verdict follows.

B. Wrong. 60% is the converted share of received mass, not dispatched mass. The receipt stage contributes another factor 68/80=85%; 0.85 x 0.75 x 0.80=51%.

C. Wrong. 85% is the matched received share, not the verified converted share. It skips sorting and actual transformation.

D. Wrong. The 51% calculation is right, but 49% not verified converted is not proof of dumping. It includes different recorded or unresolved stages, and the unmatched-dispatch amount is 12 tonnes, not 39.2 tonnes.

Question 1

Using the National Municipal Accounts Manual's accounting concepts, consider:

1. Adding new fixed-asset units is generally capital expenditure.

2. An electricity bill must be capital expenditure if it exceeds the price of new lamps.

3. Every repair must be revenue expenditure, even an eligible useful-life improvement.

4. An eligible improvement extending an existing asset's useful life can be capitalised.

Which statements are correct?

Correct answer: B

A. Wrong. Statement 1 is correct, but statement 2 substitutes bill size for the nature of expenditure. The option also omits correct statement 4, concerning eligible useful-life improvements.

B. Correct. Statement 1 reflects adding fixed-asset units, and statement 4 reflects an eligible useful-life improvement. Statement 2 is false because relative bill size does not decide classification. Statement 3 is false because the label repair cannot override the substance of an eligible capital improvement. Actual capitalisation remains subject to the applicable policy.

C. Wrong. Statement 4 is correct, but the option omits correct statement 1. Adding new fixed-asset units is generally capital expenditure under the manual's concept; useful-life improvement is not its only route.

D. Wrong. Statements 1 and 4 are correct, but statement 3 wrongly makes every repair revenue expenditure. Routine maintenance differs from an eligible improvement that extends useful life. The name repair alone does not settle classification.

Question 2

Under the 2026-27 Union Budget guide, consider:

1. A Union grant to a State can be revenue expenditure even when the State creates capital assets with it.

2. Every payment producing a physical asset anywhere must be capital expenditure in the Union's books.

3. Union loans and advances to States are included in capital payments.

Which statements are correct?

Correct answer: C

A. Wrong. Statement 1 is correct, but the Union guide also includes loans and advances to States in capital payments, making statement 3 correct.

B. Wrong. Statement 3 is correct, but statement 2 ignores the explicit treatment of grants creating grantee assets as Union revenue expenditure. Statement 1 is also omitted.

C. Correct. The guide explicitly supports both the grantor-book distinction in statement 1 and the inclusion of loans and advances in statement 3. Statement 2 wrongly substitutes an asset-anywhere rule for the stated Union accounting treatment.

D. Wrong. Statements 1 and 3 are correct, but statement 2 contradicts the grant treatment in statement 1 and the official guide.

Question 3

A fictional municipal file records a budget provision, a received purchase-only grant and an unpaid supplier bill. Consider:

1. Budget provision alone proves that the corresponding cash has arrived.

2. Recording the bill alone does not prove a cash payment.

3. Grant receipt alone makes its balance available for any operating expense.

4. An unpaid bill may still give rise to a recognised liability under accrual accounting.

Which statements are correct?

Correct answer: A

A. Correct. Statement 2 separates a recorded bill from evidence of cash payment. Statement 4 recognises that an obligation may be recognised under accrual accounting before cash is paid. Statement 1 confuses provision with receipt, while statement 3 disregards the supplied purchase-only condition. The actual entry depends on the underlying transaction and policy.

B. Wrong. Statement 2 is correct, but statement 1 turns a budget provision into evidence that cash arrived. The option also omits correct statement 4: absence of payment does not itself preclude accrual recognition of a liability.

C. Wrong. Statements 2 and 4 are correct, but statement 3 treats restricted grant money as available for any operating use. Cash arrival does not remove the supplied grant restriction.

D. Wrong. Statement 4 is correct, but the option omits correct statement 2. A bill's recording alone is not proof that it has been paid; liability recognition and cash payment are distinct.

Question 4

Two fictional lighting options are compared over three years:

A: Rs1 lakh purchase plus Rs20,000 each year.

B: Rs1.25 lakh purchase plus Rs10,000 each year.

Only those costs are supplied; service quality and useful life are not established. Which conclusion is justified?

Correct answer: D

A. Wrong. A's lower purchase price is real but does not settle three-year outlays or service quality. A totals Rs1.60 lakh, compared with B's Rs1.55 lakh on the supplied figures.

B. Wrong. B totals Rs1.55 lakh and A Rs1.60 lakh, a difference of Rs5,000, not Rs25,000. The Rs25,000 figure is B's additional purchase cost, not its three-year saving.

C. Wrong. The supplied horizon is three years, not an established whole useful life. Relevant costs and quality evidence may be missing. A simple listed-outlay sum cannot prove complete lifetime cost.

D. Correct. A totals Rs1,00,000 + 3 x Rs20,000 = Rs1,60,000. B totals Rs1,25,000 + 3 x Rs10,000 = Rs1,55,000. The Rs5,000 difference is established, but suitability and full-life value are not.

Question 5 · HARD

A fictional town compares equally suitable lamps for three years. A Rs1 lakh grant is already received. It may fund all or part of either purchase, combined with local purchase funds, but no running costs. Local funds are Rs20,000 for purchase and Rs15,000 yearly for running.

A: purchase Rs1 lakh; running Rs30,000 yearly.

B: purchase Rs1.20 lakh; running Rs10,000 yearly.

No other funds or costs apply; ignore discounting. Which assessment is correct?

Correct answer: B

A. Wrong. The grant covers A's purchase, but its Rs30,000 annual running requirement exceeds the identified Rs15,000 recurring funds by Rs15,000 each year. Upfront grant coverage does not establish continuing affordability.

B. Correct. B uses the Rs1 lakh grant plus Rs20,000 local purchase funds, exactly within the capital limit. Its Rs10,000 annual running requirement is within Rs15,000. Total listed project outlays are A: Rs1,00,000 + 3 x Rs30,000 = Rs1,90,000; B: Rs1,20,000 + 3 x Rs10,000 = Rs1,50,000. B is Rs40,000 lower. The grant changes local financing, not these total project costs.

C. Wrong. B does fit the identified funding limits, but Rs5,000 is the unused portion of the annual Rs15,000 allocation after paying Rs10,000. It is not B's running cost.

D. Wrong. A's purchase is covered, but its annual running cost breaches the recurring limit. A also has Rs40,000 higher, not Rs20,000 lower, total listed three-year cost. The purchase-price difference alone is not the three-year comparison.

Question 1

Under Article 243Q, consider:

1. A Nagar Panchayat is the municipal form for a transitional rural-to-urban area.

2. A Municipal Council is the constitutional municipal form for a larger urban area.

3. The Governor must classify urban areas solely by population, ignoring density, local revenue and non-agricultural employment.

4. A Governor's notified industrial township may fall within the proviso allowing a municipality not to be constituted.

Which statements are correct?

Correct answer: A

A. Correct. Statement 1 identifies the transitional-area form. Statement 4 reflects the industrial-township proviso. Statement 2 confuses a Municipal Council for a smaller urban area with a Municipal Corporation for a larger one. Statement 3 excludes classification factors that Article 243Q expressly includes.

B. Wrong. Statements 1 and 4 are correct, but statement 3 excludes classification factors that Article 243Q expressly includes. Population is not the sole criterion; density, revenue generated for local administration and non-agricultural employment are among the listed factors.

C. Wrong. Statement 1 is correct but the option omits statement 4. The Governor's notified industrial-township proviso can permit a municipality not to be constituted, having regard to the area's size and the municipal services provided or proposed by an industrial establishment, among relevant factors.

D. Wrong. Statements 1 and 4 are correct, but statement 2 confuses the municipal forms. Article 243Q provides a Municipal Corporation for a larger urban area, not a Municipal Council.

Question 2

Under Article 243X, State legislation may provide for:

1. Municipal taxes subject to specified procedures and limits.

2. Assignment of specified taxes levied and collected by the State Government.

3. Grants-in-aid from the State's Consolidated Fund.

4. Unrestricted municipal taxing power solely because a service appears in the Twelfth Schedule.

Which statements are correct?

Correct answer: D

A. Wrong. Statement 1 is correct, but statement 4 invents an unrestricted taxing power. A listed service does not replace the authorising law, procedures or limits. Statements 2 and 3 are also correct.

B. Wrong. Both listed statements are correct, but this answer omits statement 1. Article 243X permits State law to authorise municipal levies with procedures and limits.

C. Wrong. Statements 1, 2 and 3 describe constitutional financial routes. Statement 4 does not: a Twelfth Schedule entry does not by itself confer an unlimited power to tax.

D. Correct. Article 243X distinguishes municipal levies, assigned State revenues and State grants. Each depends on the legal framework; a service listing is not a free-standing unlimited taxing power.

Question 3

Article 243R permits State law to provide municipal representation for:

1. Lok Sabha and State Assembly members whose constituencies include all or part of the municipal area.

2. Rajya Sabha and State Legislative Council members registered as electors within the municipal area.

3. Every MP from the State, solely by virtue of membership of Parliament.

A student also claims that Article 243R requires every municipal chairperson to be directly elected by the residents.

Which assessment is correct?

Correct answer: B

A. Wrong. Categories 1 and 2 match the constitutional criteria, but the chairperson claim is false. Article 243R leaves the manner of the chairperson's election to State legislation rather than imposing one direct-election method.

B. Correct. Category 1 uses constituency overlap; category 2 uses registration as an elector within the municipal area. Neither is a blanket entitlement for every MP from the State. State legislation may provide the chairperson-election method, so the universal direct-election claim fails.

C. Wrong. Category 2 qualifies, but category 3 removes the relevant local connection. Category 1 should be included. The option correctly rejects the chairperson claim, but its representation pairing is wrong.

D. Wrong. Category 3 does not qualify solely on the stated ground, and the universal direct-election claim is also false. Categories 1 and 2 are the constitutionally specified routes in this question.

Question 4

A municipality is dissolved with fourteen months of its original term remaining. Applying Articles 243U and 243ZA, consider:

1. Its replacement election must ordinarily be completed before six months from dissolution expire.

2. The replacement municipality receives a fresh five-year term.

3. Municipal electoral rolls and elections fall under the State Election Commission.

4. An ordinary term's expiry automatically permits the same six-month delay.

Which statements are correct?

Correct answer: C

A. Wrong. Statement 1 is correct, but statement 2 is false. The municipality constituted following premature dissolution serves the original term's unexpired remainder, not a fresh five years. Statement 3 is also correct.

B. Wrong. Statement 3 identifies the right election authority, but statement 2 gives the replacement a term the Constitution does not provide. Statement 1 is also correct.

C. Correct. The short-remainder exception does not apply to the stated fourteen-month remainder. The dissolution election deadline is ordinarily six months, the replacement serves the unexpired remainder, and the State Election Commission is the constitutional election authority.

D. Wrong. Statements 1 and 3 are correct, but statement 4 confuses dissolution with ordinary expiry. The ordinary election must be completed before the term expires.

Question 5 · HARD

A fictional metropolitan lighting scheme reserves expenditure approval to participating municipalities. Its 30-member Metropolitan Planning Committee has 20 members elected by local residents from elected municipal members and Panchayat chairpersons. It prepares a draft corridor development plan. Assume the required population proportions and all unmentioned requirements are met.

Which proposal repairs the stated election defect while respecting the supplied spending arrangement?

A. Wrong. Twenty of thirty already meets the metropolitan two-thirds floor. Twenty-four borrows the district committee's four-fifths number and, more importantly, does not repair the resident-electorate defect. The metropolitan elected share must be elected by as well as from the specified municipal-member/Panchayat-chairperson pool.

B. Wrong. The membership number and electoral pool are repaired, but the proposal also shifts expenditure approval away from the municipalities contrary to the supplied scheme. Article 243ZE's draft-planning role does not by itself create that spending power. No other authority for the transfer is supplied.

C. Wrong. The number and retained municipal spending approval are not the problem. The proposal imports the district-level Panchayat-member pool from Article 243ZD into the metropolitan rule. Article 243ZE instead specifies elected municipal members and Panchayat chairpersons in the metropolitan area.

D. Correct. Twenty of thirty meets the two-thirds metropolitan floor. Election by and from the specified metropolitan eligible pool repairs the defect, unlike election by residents generally. Keeping municipal expenditure approval respects the fictional scheme; preparing the draft development plan does not automatically transfer that power. This answer addresses only the stated defect and supplied spending arrangement, not every possible legal requirement.

Question 1

A relief team applies WHO's tank-selection and preparation guidance. Consider:

1. Previous use for a food-grade liquid is enough to establish that a tank is ready to carry drinking water.

2. A tank formerly used for water but left idle still requires cleaning and disinfection.

3. A tank formerly used for fuel passes the selection stage once the same cleaning and disinfection process is completed.

4. Ease of cleaning and protected storage remain relevant even when the previous liquid was food-grade.

Which statements are correct?

Correct answer: B

A. Wrong. Statement 2 is correct, but statement 1 confuses a prior-use selection criterion with completed preparation and overall suitability.

B. Correct. Statements 2 and 4 follow the guidance: idle former-water tanks still need cleaning and disinfection, while cleanability and protected storage are separate selection considerations.

C. Wrong. Statement 4 is correct; statements 1 and 3 are not. Food-grade history does not establish readiness, and cleaning/disinfection does not remove the exclusion of former fuel tanks.

D. Wrong. Statements 2 and 4 are correct, but statement 3 is not. The former-fuel restriction is a selection rule, not simply an instruction to carry out more preparation.

Question 2

A flood damages a small piped network. Its valves can separate damaged branches from unaffected areas. Applying WHO's rehabilitation guidance, consider:

1. Isolating affected branches can reduce wastage while allowing supply to unaffected areas.

2. An initial survey focused on visible major damage establishes that no hidden damage remains.

3. A full network survey may be impracticable while restoring basic emergency supply.

4. Users should be told which sections are affected and what they should do to protect their health and safety.

Which statements are correct?

Correct answer: D

A. Wrong. Statement 1 is correct, but statement 2 mistakes the initial focus on visible major damage for proof that all hidden damage has been ruled out.

B. Wrong. Statements 3 and 4 are correct, but statement 2 is not. The emergency survey's limited scope is not an assurance that no hidden damage remains.

C. Wrong. Statements 1 and 3 are correct, but this answer omits statement 4. Informing users about affected sections and protective actions is part of the guidance.

D. Correct. Statements 1, 3 and 4 describe compatible duties: isolate affected sections where possible, restore a basic supply even when a full survey is initially impracticable, and keep users informed. None of these establishes that the whole network is free of damage.

Question 3

Two emergency tanker contracts use different payment bases. P pays for vehicle operating time. Q pays for the quantity and quality of water delivered. Under WHO's tanker-management guidance, which assessment is justified?

Correct answer: C

A. Wrong. WHO recommends basing contract fees on quantity and quality delivered, not operating time. Ease of observing an input does not make it the recommended fee basis.

B. Wrong. The fee basis is appropriate, but WHO separately calls for clarifying responsibilities for fuel, insurance, maintenance and driver wages. An outcome-based fee does not settle those allocations.

C. Correct. Q matches the recommended fee basis. WHO separately says to agree on a method of appraising performance, so selecting that basis does not complete the contract design.

D. Wrong. Equal tank capacity does not make an operating-time fee equivalent to one based on quantity and quality delivered. The recommendation concerns the payment basis, not just vehicle size.

Question 4

Under WHO's guidance on waste from cleaning, disinfecting and rinsing water tanks, consider:

1. Reducing the chlorine concentration addresses the aquatic-life concern identified in the guidance; the remaining organic load need not affect disposal planning.

2. Organic material as well as high chlorine concentrations can make discharge into rivers and ponds harmful.

3. The guidance includes disposal to a sewer network or transport to a sewage treatment plant among its listed routes.

4. The disposal route need not account for local erosion or flooding if no chemical disinfectant was used.

Which statements are correct?

Correct answer: A

A. Correct. Statements 2 and 3 distinguish the waste's hazards from its disposal route. WHO names organic material and high chlorine levels, and lists sewer disposal or transport to a treatment plant among its options. It also lists a septic tank overflowing into an underground soakage system. The appropriate route depends on the site.

B. Wrong. Statement 3 is correct, but statement 1 treats chlorine as the only relevant aquatic-life concern. Organic material also matters in the guidance.

C. Wrong. Only statement 2 here is correct. Reducing chlorine does not remove the need to consider organic material, and sudden discharge can cause erosion or flooding independently of disinfectant use.

D. Wrong. Statements 2 and 3 are correct, but statement 4 confuses chemical hazard with the separate physical effects of releasing a large volume suddenly.

Question 5

A hypothetical operation must deliver 44,000 litres in a 360-minute shift. Tanker A holds 6,000 litres and takes 100 minutes per complete delivery-and-return cycle; B holds 4,000 litres and takes 80 minutes. Both are prepared and available at shift start. Suitable tanker C holds 5,000 litres and takes 90 minutes per cycle, but first needs 90 minutes of preparation within the shift. Each load counts only if its complete cycle ends within the shift. Vehicles start empty; preparation cannot overlap a cycle. Ignore other delays and losses; source and receiving capacity are sufficient. Consider:

1. A and B together can initially deliver 34,000 litres, not their proportionally calculated fractional-cycle output.

2. After preparation, C can complete four cycles because its driving-and-delivery time fits four times into 360 minutes.

3. If A instead loses the first 70 minutes to maintenance, its shift delivery falls by 4,200 litres.

4. With that 70-minute loss for A and the stated preparation for C, total delivery is 43,000 litres, below the requirement.

Which statements are correct?

Correct answer: B

A. Wrong. Statement 1 is correct: A completes 3 cycles and B 4, delivering 18,000 + 16,000 = 34,000 litres. Statement 2 forgets C's preparation time: only 270 minutes remain, allowing 3 cycles, or 15,000 litres.

B. Correct. Statement 1 uses whole completed cycles. For statement 4, A has 290 minutes after maintenance and completes only 2 cycles, delivering 12,000 litres. Add B's 16,000 and C's 15,000: 43,000 litres, a 1,000-litre shortfall. Without A's maintenance delay, the three would deliver 49,000 litres. These are hypothetical planning figures, not a guarantee of quality.

C. Wrong. Statement 2 ignores preparation time. Statement 3 incorrectly scales output continuously with time: A drops from 3 completed cycles to 2, so the loss is one 6,000-litre load, not 4,200 litres.

D. Wrong. Statement 4 is correct, but statement 3 is not. Because only complete cycles count, a 70-minute delay changes A's output by a whole load rather than by a proportional per-minute volume.

Question 1

The Twelfth Schedule includes water supply. Which approach best identifies the legally assigned water-network operator in a particular town under Article 243W?

Correct answer: C

A. Wrong. The Schedule lists matters; Article 243W allows endowment by State law subject to conditions, rather than automatically assigning every operational duty.

B. Wrong. State legislative competence does not establish that the State government must itself run every network or that municipalities have no assigned role.

C. Correct. Article 243W is permissive and conditional. The applicable State law and allocation must establish who has the particular operating duty.

D. Wrong. A complaint channel is evidence about reporting, not by itself the legal allocation of the operating duty.

Question 2

A hypothetical service area has 200 households; 100 have direct household network connections. A survey finds that 90 of these connected households received the stated daily supply, and no other households did. What are direct-connection coverage, receipt among connected households, and receipt among all households, respectively?

Correct answer: A

A. Correct. Connection coverage is 100/200; receipt among connected households is 90/100; receipt among all households is 90/200. These give 50, 90 and 45 percent.

B. Wrong. The receipt ratios are reversed: the connected-group denominator is 100, not 200.

C. Wrong. It substitutes receipt among connected households for the handbook's connection-coverage ratio.

D. Wrong. The final ratio uses the connected count of 100 rather than the 90 households receiving the stated supply.

Question 3

A water complaint was marked closed within 24 hours. The utility's tracking proforma has no complainant's written endorsement of satisfactory resolution. When testing the Service Level Benchmarking handbook's complaint-redressal standard, which conclusion is justified?

Correct answer: D

A. Wrong. A timely closure timestamp does not replace written complainant endorsement of satisfactory resolution.

B. Wrong. Reliability of the complaint-record system and the percentage of timely satisfactory resolutions are distinct; A does not establish achievement of 80 percent.

C. Wrong. The complaint was received and belongs in the denominator. Missing endorsement concerns evidence for the numerator, not whether a complaint was received.

D. Correct. The handbook requires timely satisfactory resolution with written complainant endorsement on the tracking proforma. The record gap alone does not prove that no repair occurred.

Question 4

Two continuity reports use detailed valve-operation records. P assumes adequate pressure at consumers' premises. Q also assesses consumer-end pressure and supply hours through a statistically valid sample across all zones. Which pairing follows the handbook's continuity reliability table?

Correct answer: B

A. Wrong. P assumes consumer pressure; that is grade B, not A.

B. Correct. These are the handbook's B and A methods respectively. Better measurement can confirm, increase or reduce a reported supply figure.

C. Wrong. Continuity has no applicable C grade, and grade A specifies a statistically valid sample across all zones rather than a household census.

D. Wrong. The grades are correctly identified, but reliability is not the level of service. Q need not show more supply hours.

Question 5

In one month, Zone E receives 80 water complaints; 64 are satisfactorily resolved with written complainant endorsement within 24 hours. Zone W receives 240 complaints; 120 meet that condition, another 24 are satisfactorily resolved and endorsed on the next working day after a holiday, and 48 only later. All these events occur within the month. These are the city's only zones and complaints. Applying the handbook, consider:

1. The city's timely-redressal indicator is 65 percent.

2. Excluding W's next-working-day cases gives the correct city indicator of 57.5 percent.

3. Averaging the two zone percentages gives the correct city complaint indicator of 70 percent.

4. Falling below the handbook's suggested 80 percent benchmark does not, by itself, establish breach of a legal entitlement.

Which statements are correct?

Correct answer: B
