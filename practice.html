19 Nov 2026 · The sanitation chain

Read the lesson

Question 1

A town reports that households use private improved toilets connected to a sewer. The collected wastewater is discharged untreated into a river. Under the World Health Organization and United Nations Children's Fund Joint Monitoring Programme household sanitation ladder, which assessment is best supported?

Correct answer: C

A. Wrong. Offsite conveyance does not establish safe management. The wastewater is expressly discharged untreated.

B. Wrong. Limited means improved facilities shared between households, not a general label for any downstream failure. The facilities here are private.

C. Correct. The facilities are improved and non-shared, but excreta are not safely managed. This matches the basic rung rather than safely managed.

D. Wrong. This confuses facility type with service performance. Untreated discharge is a serious downstream failure, but does not convert the stated improved technology into an unimproved one.

Question 2

Septic tanks in a settlement are emptied by a service that delivers the sludge to a functioning treatment facility. The tanks discharge their liquid effluent directly into an open drain, without further treatment. Which response best addresses the remaining gap?

Correct answer: A

A. Correct. The liquid and sludge routes require separate appropriate management. Keep the functioning sludge route and assess a suitable correction for the untreated liquid pathway.

B. Wrong. This addresses solids accumulation rather than the identified discharge route. Emptying alone does not establish safe management of liquid leaving through the outlet.

C. Wrong. Infiltration can be appropriate, but suitability depends on site conditions including soil and groundwater. A standard design alone cannot establish this.

D. Wrong. A covered drain may alter exposure along conveyance, but covering is not treatment and does not establish safe management at the destination.

Question 3

After gravity settling, wastewater still contains biodegradable organic material. An engineer supplies air and mixes the liquid in a biological-treatment tank. Which explanation best fits the role of the air?

Correct answer: C

A. Wrong. This assigns physical separation of dissolved organic material to air bubbles alone. In the stated biological-treatment tank, the air supports aerobic microbial activity; it is not a substitute for that breakdown process.

B. Wrong. This mistakes an operating condition for disinfection and removes the microorganisms' central role. Supplying air does not itself certify pathogen control.

C. Correct. Aerobic organisms need oxygen to carry out the breakdown of biodegradable organic material. Prior settling does not remove all material on which they act.

D. Wrong. This confuses oxygen supply inside treatment with a substitute for reducing the oxygen-demanding load discharged downstream. The treatment task remains.

Question 4

A town claims that septage removed from household tanks during the past year was delivered and treated appropriately. Which evidence package most directly tests the complete offsite claim?

Correct answer: B

A. Wrong. This combines activity and potential capacity. It does not show that the removed septage actually reached treatment or received appropriate treatment.

B. Correct. The linked records follow the material to treatment; relevant performance evidence and inspections test what happened there during the claimed period. Credibility and coverage of that evidence still matter.

C. Wrong. Delivery evidence is useful, but liquid-outlet results do not alone establish appropriate treatment and management of the solid fraction. The reception photographs do not close that gap.

D. Wrong. These can substantiate service at the property and payment for removal. They stop short of verifying destination and subsequent treatment.

Question 5

Six households share improved toilets. Inspections verify appropriate downstream treatment. A daytime survey records women users' satisfaction, but does not assess access after dark. Consider these inferences under the World Health Organization and United Nations Children's Fund Joint Monitoring Programme household sanitation ladder:

1. The service is in the limited category despite the verified downstream treatment.

2. Reducing sharing to two households per toilet would move the service to the basic category.

3. The survey is useful evidence but does not settle the question of safe access after dark.

4. The limited classification establishes a downstream treatment failure that the inspection must have missed.

Which inferences are correct?

Correct answer: A

A. Correct. Shared improved facilities fall in the limited household-service category. The daytime survey has value, but its scope does not establish safe access after dark. Neither conclusion contradicts the verified treatment evidence.

B. Wrong. Inference1 is supported, but2 treats reduced sharing as non-sharing. Sharing between two households still meets the limited-category condition.

C. Wrong. Inference2 mistakes less sharing for the absence of sharing. Inference4 mistakes a classification based on sharing for evidence that downstream treatment failed.

D. Wrong. Inference3 keeps the survey within its limits, but4 improperly overrides verified treatment evidence using a category based on a different characteristic.

Question 1

A borewell sample looks clear. A laboratory reports that Escherichia coli (E. coli) was not detected in that sample. No chemical analysis has been done. Which conclusion is best supported?

Correct answer: B

A. Wrong. A result for one microbial indicator cannot establish the absence of untested chemicals or all other microbes throughout a supply.

B. Correct. Keep the conclusion within the sample and parameter actually tested. Appearance and an E. coli non-detection result do not settle chemical suitability or establish the absence of all other microbial hazards.

C. Wrong. Groundwater can contain harmful dissolved chemicals, but possibility is not evidence that this borewell does. Appropriate analysis is still needed.

D. Wrong. Bounded evidence is useful. Its limits do not make it worthless or require simultaneous testing of every imaginable contaminant.

Question 2

Consider these possible contaminant-pathway pairs:

1. Arsenic or fluoride: release from geological materials into groundwater.

2. Lead: leaching from water-supply components in contact with water.

3. Nitrate: entry associated with fertiliser use or malfunctioning septic systems.

How many pairs are correctly matched?

Correct answer: C

A. Wrong. Each of the three describes a possible pathway; the pairs are not claims that these are the only sources.

B. Wrong. None needs to be excluded. Arsenic and fluoride can be geogenic, lead can enter from supply components, and nitrate can be associated with fertilisers or septic contamination.

C. Correct. The location at which a hazard enters can differ: the aquifer, the catchment or subsurface flow path, or the supply infrastructure.

D. Wrong. All three mechanisms are recognised. Natural origin does not itself establish safety, and contamination need not originate at the borewell.

Question 3

With reference to drinking-water treatment, consider these statements:

1. Boiling that addresses microbial hazards also establishes removal of dissolved arsenic.

2. A filter's ability to remove one class of organisms does not establish its ability to remove dissolved chemicals.

3. Reverse-osmosis performance for a particular chemical should be checked against the system's specified removal capability.

Which statements are correct?

Correct answer: D

A. Wrong. Statement 2 is correct, but statement 3 must also be retained. A particular reverse-osmosis system must be evaluated for the chemical concerned; the technology name is not a universal performance guarantee.

B. Wrong. Statement 3 is correct, but statement 2 is also correct. Removing organisms does not by itself establish removal of dissolved chemicals. Different hazards require different capabilities.

C. Wrong. Statements 2 and 3 are correct, but statement 1 confuses microbial inactivation with arsenic removal. Boiling does not establish removal of dissolved arsenic.

D. Correct. Both the limited inference from filter capability and the contaminant-specific performance check are sound. A device label is not a substitute for correct operation, maintenance and appropriate performance verification.

Question 4

Consider the following statements about managing drinking-water safety:

1. WHO's framework combines health-based targets, preventive water safety plans and independent surveillance.

2. Under the December 2019 Jal Jeevan Mission Operational Guidelines, agency monitoring includes laboratory and field testing of source and household-tap samples.

3. Those guidelines assign community field-kit surveillance the role of replacing agency monitoring.

Which statements are correct?

Correct answer: A

A. Correct. WHO combines targets, preventive water safety plans and independent surveillance. The December 2019 JJM definitions include both laboratory and field testing in agency monitoring, alongside community surveillance as a distinct activity.

B. Wrong. Statement 2 is correct, but statement 1 also belongs. WHO explicitly describes the three complementary functions; testing alone is not the whole preventive framework.

C. Wrong. Statement 1 is correct, but statement 2 also belongs. The dated JJM definition includes laboratory AND field testing by the responsible agency, not laboratory work alone.

D. Wrong. It includes the invented replacement claim in statement 3. Community surveillance does not abolish agency monitoring. WHO independent surveillance and JJM community surveillance are not asserted to be identical institutional arrangements.

Question 5

In a hypothetical village, a household stores water collected from its borewell supply the previous evening. Properly collected samples taken today show fluoride above the applicable limit at the source, with E. coli not detected there; the household storage sample contains E. coli. Both look clear.

Consider these inferences:

1. Source E. coli non-detection cancels the fluoride exceedance as a safety concern.

2. Contamination after collection is possible, but contamination of the earlier source batch is not ruled out.

3. Boiling alone resolves both the fluoride and microbial findings.

4. Chemical-hazard control and the control and investigation of microbial contamination are distinct needs.

5. The results establish that the source was free of E. coli when the household collected its water.

Which inferences are correct?

Correct answer: B

A. Wrong. It adds inference 1, the parameter-override mistake. An E. coli non-detection result cannot cancel a fluoride exceedance; the results address different hazards.

B. Correct. Inference 2 keeps both temporal and post-collection explanations open: stored water came from an earlier source batch, not the water sampled at the source today. Contamination during collection, transport or storage is also possible. Inference 4 recognises distinct chemical and microbial needs.

C. Wrong. It adds inference 3, the treatment-substitution mistake. Microbial inactivation by boiling does not establish removal of dissolved fluoride.

D. Wrong. It adds inference 5, the time-matching mistake. Today's source sample does not establish the source condition when the stored batch was collected yesterday. These are not paired samples of the same water parcel.

Question 1

In a hard-rock district, farmers observe that water levels in wells near a heavily pumping borewell fall while it runs and recover after it stops, while wells farther away are unaffected. Which of the following best explains this pattern?

Select the best explanation.

Correct answer: B

The fall-while-pumping, recover-when-it-stops pattern is the signature of drawdown: pumping lowers head locally and water flows towards the well through connected fractures (USGS). Option A describes regional seasonal decline, which would not spare farther wells or reverse when pumping stops. Option C invokes stacked aquifers - real geology, but it would not produce this pumping-linked interference. Option D asserts permanent sealing of fractures, which is inconsistent with the observed recovery: the demonstrated effect here is reversible hydraulic-head drawdown, not permanent structural closure.

Source: USGS - Aquifers and Groundwater; USGS - Aquifer Compaction due to Groundwater Pumping

Question 2

With reference to how rocks store and transmit groundwater, consider the following statements:

  1. Porosity measures the share of void space in a rock; permeability measures how easily water moves through connected voids.
  2. Clay typically has very low porosity, which is why it yields little water to wells.
  3. In a confined aquifer under artesian pressure, water in a well can rise above the top of the aquifer without pumping.
  4. The water table is the lower boundary of the saturated zone.

Which of the statements given above is/are correct?

Correct answer: A

Statement 1 is correct - the USGS defines the two properties separately, and movement requires connected pores. Statement 2 is the trap: clay is highly porous; its many very small pores hold water tightly (high specific retention, low permeability), so the poor yield is a permeability story, not a porosity one. Statement 3 is correct - confining layers trap pressure and water rises towards the piezometric surface; only when that surface stands above the ground does the well become a flowing artesian well. Statement 4 is inverted: the water table is the upper surface of the saturated zone, not its lower boundary. Option by option: B and D both keep the clay trap (statement 2); C keeps the inverted boundary (statement 4); only A pairs the two correct statements.

Source: USGS - Aquifers and Groundwater; USGS SIR 2007-5050

Question 3

Consider the following pairs of institutions or initiatives and their features:

  1. Central Ground Water Board : maps and characterises aquifers under the National Aquifer Mapping and Management Programme (NAQUIM)
  2. Atal Bhujal Yojana : groundwater management centred only on constructing recharge structures, without community water budgets or demand-side measures
  3. Jal Sanchay Jan Bhagidari : a whole-of-society push mobilising communities for rainwater harvesting and recharge

How many of the above pairs are correctly matched?

Correct answer: B

Pair 1 is correct: aquifer mapping under the National Aquifer Mapping and Management Programme (NAQUIM) at 1:50,000 scale is CGWB's programme since 2012, with 1.0 completed over the mappable area and 2.0 detailing priority areas. Pair 2 is conceptually false: Atal Bhujal Yojana's core was exactly the opposite - participatory groundwater management in which all 8,203 gram panchayats prepared water budgets and Water Security Plans and updated them annually, with GP-level trainings and demand-side behavioural change; structures alone without budgets is the supply-side approach the pilot moved away from (PIB). Pair 3 is correct: the PIB presents Jal Sanchay Jan Bhagidari as the current whole-of-society effort, mobilising communities, corporate social responsibility (CSR) support and local bodies for rainwater-harvesting and recharge structures. Two pairs correctly matched.

Source: PIB 10 Aug 2026 (NAQUIM); PIB 30 Jul 2026 (Atal Bhujal Yojana completion)

Question 4

With reference to the Dynamic Ground Water Resources Assessment 2025, consider the following statements:

  1. An assessment unit with a Stage of Extraction above 100 percent is categorised as Over-exploited.
  2. An assessment unit with a Stage of Extraction of 85 percent is categorised as Critical.
  3. Between 2017 and 2025, the share of Safe assessment units rose while the share of Over-exploited units declined.
  4. The assessment estimates India's overall Stage of Extraction at over 100 percent.

Which of the statements given above is/are correct?

Correct answer: C

Statement 1 is correct: extraction above 100 percent of the annual extractable resource means Over-exploited. Statement 2 misplaces the boundary: Safe runs up to and including 70 percent, Semi-critical covers above 70 to 90, so 85 percent is Semi-critical; Critical begins above 90, not at 85 or at 90 itself. Statement 3 is correct on the trend: Safe units rose from 62.6 to 73.14 percent and Over-exploited units fell from 17.2 to 10.8 percent between 2017 and 2025. Statement 4 is the exaggeration: the national Stage of Extraction is 60.63 percent - stress is concentrated in specific blocks and states, which is why the categories exist. Option by option: A and B both keep the boundary error (statement 2); D keeps the exaggeration (statement 4); only C pairs the two correct statements.

Source: PIB 29 Jan 2026 (Dynamic Groundwater Resources Assessment 2025); PIB 11 Mar 2026 (category boundaries)

Question 5

A rural assessment unit in a State where the Central Ground Water Authority (CGWA) regulates groundwater extraction records an annual recharge of 1.20 billion cubic metres (BCM), an annual extractable resource of 1.00 BCM and an annual extraction of 1.05 BCM. A new large-scale industry - not a Micro, Small or Medium Enterprise (MSME) - applies for a No Objection Certificate (NOC) to abstract groundwater for its manufacturing process. Consider the following statements:

  1. The unit is categorised Over-exploited, because its Stage of Extraction is 105 percent, not 87.5 percent.
  2. The industry can be granted the NOC for its process water, provided it installs digital water flow meters and submits a rainwater-harvesting proposal.
  3. The same industry may still be granted an NOC for drinking and domestic use by its workforce and for greenbelt development, subject to other applicable conditions.
  4. A new packaged drinking water plant holding MSME status can be granted an NOC in this unit, since the MSME exception covers all small industries.
  5. At least 65 percent of the works taken up in this block under the Mahatma Gandhi National Rural Employment Guarantee Scheme, measured by the number of works, must be water-related works.

Which of the statements given above is/are correct?

Correct answer: A

Statement 1 is the calculation the question is built on: the Stage of Extraction divides extraction by the annual EXTRACTABLE resource (1.05/1.00 = 105 percent, Over-exploited), not by total recharge (1.05/1.20 = 87.5 percent, which would wrongly read as Semi-critical). Statement 2 is false: compliance conditions such as flow meters and rainwater-harvesting proposals are genuine NOC requirements, but they cannot lift the categorical bar - in an Over-exploited unit a new non-MSME industry simply is not eligible for a process-water NOC. Statement 3 is true: para 4.1 expressly keeps NOCs possible for a new industry's workforce drinking/domestic and greenbelt use - conditional eligibility, subject to the notification's other conditions, not an automatic grant. Statement 4 is false: the MSME exception does not extend to new packaged drinking water industries, which are barred even with MSME status (CGWA SOP, FAQ 6). Statement 5 is false on its measure: the September 2025 MGNREGA Schedule amendment earmarks a minimum of 65 percent of works IN TERMS OF COST in Over-exploited and Critical rural blocks, not by the number of works. Option by option: B drops the true purpose carve-out (3) and keeps the works-count error (5); C drops the correct classification (1) and keeps the conditions-can-lift-the-bar error (2); D keeps both the packaged-water error (4) and the works-count error (5); only A pairs exactly the two correct statements.

Source: CGWA Notification S.O. 3289(E) 24.09.2020; CGWA SOP v9.1; MGNREGS S.O. 4288(E) 23.09.2025

Question 1

After an intense monsoon burst, the streets of a built-up city can flood within minutes, while open countryside nearby shows no flooding. Which of the following best explains this difference?

Select the best explanation.

Correct answer: C

Urbanisation replaces infiltrating soil and vegetation with impervious surfaces. The USGS describes the result: more stormwater arrives in drains and streams, more quickly. The NDMA quantifies it - developed catchments can raise flood peaks 1.8 to 8 times and volumes up to 6 times. Option A describes riverine flooding, which the NDMA explicitly separates. Options B and D fail as blanket claims: the NDMA attributes the minutes-scale onset to faster flow times from developed catchments, and only separately notes that urban heat islands may increase rainfall somewhat over cities - it does not present extra rainfall as the cause of the minutes-scale flooding.

Source: NDMA Urban Floods overview; USGS Impervious Surfaces and Flooding

Question 2

With reference to how urbanisation changes the movement of rainwater, consider the following statements:

  1. Impervious surfaces reduce the area available for infiltration, which also reduces groundwater recharge.
  2. A developed catchment delivers stormwater to streams more slowly than a forested one, because drains regulate the flow.
  3. Detention permanently removes runoff from the drainage system.
  4. Retention collects and holds runoff with no surface outflow.

Which of the statements given above is/are correct?

Correct answer: A

Statement 1 is correct - both the USGS and MoHUA describe reduced infiltration cutting recharge and lowering the water table. Statement 2 is wrong: urban catchments deliver water faster, so the peak arrives earlier and higher, not later. Statement 3 is wrong: detention holds the extra runoff temporarily and releases it at a slower rate, reducing the peak - it does not remove the runoff. Statement 4 is correct - the NDMA's 2010 glossary defines retention as holding runoff with no surface outflow.

Source: USGS; MoHUA engineering manual 2019; NDMA 2010 guidelines glossary

Question 3

Consider the following pairs of stormwater measures and the mechanisms they rely on:

  1. Infiltration trench : restores on-site soaking of runoff into the ground
  2. Detention pond : permanently removes runoff from the drainage system
  3. Clearing encroachments and dumped waste from natural drains : restores some of the conveyance capacity the drain originally had

How many of the above pairs are correctly matched?

Correct answer: B

Pairs 1 and 3 hold: infiltration trenches sink water at source (MoHUA engineering manual), and encroachments and dumped debris shrink a drain's real capacity below its design capacity, so clearing them restores conveyance (NDMA; MoHUA O&M manual). Pair 2 is the trap: detention holds runoff temporarily and releases it slower - it reduces the peak outflow but removes nothing.

Source: MoHUA engineering and O&M manuals 2019; NDMA 2010 guidelines glossary

Question 4

With reference to the NDMA's 2010 guidelines on urban flooding, consider the following statements:

  1. The NDMA describes them as the first time urban flooding was addressed as a separate disaster, delinked from riverine floods.
  2. They recommend that drainage be planned at the level of administrative wards, since municipal bodies own the drains.
  3. Under them, the catchment is the basis for drainage design and the watershed the basis for management actions.
  4. In their sample flood-characterisation system, the rainfall duration is normally taken at least equal to the catchment's time of concentration.

Which of the statements given above is/are correct?

Correct answer: D

Statement 1 is correct - the NDMA says its 2010 guidelines, prompted by the 2005 Mumbai floods, addressed urban flooding as a separate disaster for the first time. Statement 2 is wrong: the guidelines call for catchment and watershed-based planning precisely because water ignores ward and agency boundaries. Statement 3 is correct - the guidelines assign the catchment as the basis of design and the watershed as the basis of management. Statement 4 is correct - their sample flood-characterisation system normally takes the rainfall duration at least equal to the time of concentration.

Source: NDMA Management of Urban Flooding guidelines 2010

Question 5

A city doubles the width of a major stormwater drain downstream. In the next monsoon, a ward upstream still floods during short, intense bursts of rain. Consider the following inferences:

  1. Continued paving and building upstream may have raised the runoff peak beyond even the enlarged capacity.
  2. The runoff coefficient of a surface depends mainly on the intensity of the rainfall falling on it.
  3. Siltation and dumped waste in the drain, or an outfall constrained by high water in the receiving stream, can throttle even an enlarged drain.
  4. The season's total rainfall was close to normal, so the flooding points to drainage failure alone and not to anything about the rainfall itself.

Which of the inferences given above is/are valid?

Correct answer: B

Inference 1 is valid: the rational method (Q = CIA) shows a higher runoff coefficient upstream raises the peak for the same rain; the NDMA warns development keeps raising flows. Inference 2 is the trap: the coefficient C describes the surface, not the rainfall - intensity enters separately as I. Inference 3 is valid: the MoHUA manuals document siltation and waste blockage cutting real capacity below design, and a tail-water-constrained outfall limits discharge however wide the drain. Inference 4 fails: urban flooding depends on intensity and duration, not seasonal totals - a short intense burst can overwhelm drains in a statistically normal monsoon.

Source: NDMA 2010 guidelines; MoHUA engineering and O&M manuals 2019

Question 1

The sun has set over a large city, yet its streets stay warmer than the surrounding countryside. Which of the following best explains why urban areas often cool more slowly at night?

Select the best explanation.

Correct answer: A

Option A is correct: urban materials such as steel and stone have higher heat capacities than rural materials like dry soil and sand, so cities store more of the day's solar energy and release it slowly through the night - the EPA's compendium notes downtown areas can absorb and store twice the heat of their rural surroundings by day. Option B fails: rural areas do not systematically receive less solar radiation; the difference lies in how surfaces handle the same energy, not in how much arrives. Option C fails: greenhouse gas concentrations do not spike over cities at sunset; that confuses the local heat island with the separate, global greenhouse effect. Option D fails: the night-time contrast is no instrument artifact - it is measured directly by stations sited in both urban and rural areas, and satellites add an independent quantity (surface temperature) that shows its own heat-island pattern; the two measurements observe different things, and neither makes the effect vanish.

Source: US EPA Compendium of Strategies; NASA ARSET UHI Q&A Part 1 (Q7)

Question 2

With reference to how scientists measure urban heat islands, consider the following statements:

  1. Satellites such as Landsat and MODIS estimate land surface temperature, not the temperature of the air.
  2. Surface heat islands and atmospheric heat islands are two names for the same measurement.
  3. Canopy-layer temperatures - the air people actually experience at street level - are measured by instruments at weather stations or by mobile traverses.

Which of the statements given above is/are correct?

Correct answer: B

Statement 1 is correct: thermal satellite sensors estimate the temperature of surfaces - roofs, roads, ground - expressed in Kelvin, not air temperature. Statement 2 is incorrect: surface and atmospheric heat islands differ in how they form, how they are measured and their impacts; atmospheric heat islands sit in the canopy and boundary layers of the air and are measured with stations, traverses, towers, radiosondes and aircraft. Statement 3 is correct: canopy-layer air temperature, the layer people live in, comes from in-situ instruments at meteorological stations or mobile surveys, not directly from satellites.

Source: NASA ARSET UHI Q&A Part 1 (Q38); US EPA, What Are Heat Islands

Question 3

Consider the following pairs of heat island mitigation measures and the mechanisms they rely on:

  1. Cool roofs : high reflectivity sends incoming sunlight back before it is absorbed as heat
  2. Urban trees : shade surfaces directly and cool the air through evapotranspiration from leaves
  3. Urban water bodies : water's high heat capacity keeps its surface temperature fairly steady between day and night, unlike pavement

How many of the above pairs are correctly matched?

Correct answer: C

All three pairs are correctly matched. Cool roofs work through reflectivity (albedo) - they send incoming sunlight back rather than absorbing and storing it as heat; where a material also has high emissivity, that separately helps it re-emit any heat it has absorbed. Trees cool twice over: by shading surfaces and by evapotranspiration, the evaporation of water from leaves, which carries heat away. Water bodies matter through heat capacity: the EPA's compendium notes that water maintains a fairly constant temperature day and night because of its high heat capacity, in contrast to the dips and spikes of built surfaces. These are the standard mechanisms in the EPA's heat island guidance; Ahmedabad's Cool Roofs Program applies the first of them.

Source: US EPA, What Are Heat Islands; US EPA Compendium of Strategies; Ahmedabad HAP 2019 (Cool Roofs Program)

Question 4

With reference to Ahmedabad's Heat Action Plan and its evaluation (a historic case study), consider the following statements:

  1. It was piloted in 2013, after a severe heat wave in 2010, and fully implemented from 2014.
  2. The plan's alert thresholds were developed without reference to Ahmedabad's own temperature-mortality experience.
  3. A 2018 evaluation estimated about 2,380 fewer deaths in 2014-2015 than pre-plan mortality rates would have predicted.

Which of the statements given above is/are correct?

Correct answer: B

Statements 1 and 3 are correct. Statement 1: after the 2010 heat wave - Azhar et al. (2014) estimated 1,344 additional deaths that May - the Ahmedabad Municipal Corporation and partners piloted the city's first Heat Action Plan in 2013, which its 2018 evaluation (Hess et al.) describes as South Asia's first, with full implementation beginning in 2014. Statement 2 is incorrect: the thresholds were built precisely on local evidence - retrospective epidemiological analyses of health-relevant temperature thresholds for Ahmedabad. IMD's Ahmedabad centre supplies the forecasts and the AMC (through its nodal officer) issues the alerts, but the thresholds themselves rest on the city's own temperature-mortality data. Statement 3 is correct: the 2018 evaluation, comparing the 2014-2015 heat seasons against a 2007-2010 baseline, estimated about 2,380 fewer deaths than pre-plan rates predicted (95% CI 324-4,435; about 1,190 per year on average). The study is ecological - confounders such as air pollution and heat-wave timing were not controlled - so this is an association with wide uncertainty, not proof the plan caused the decline. The examinable facts are the study's existence, period and estimate; the caveat belongs in the lesson.

Source: Ahmedabad HAP 2019 easy-read; Hess et al. 2018 (doi:10.1155/2018/7973519)

Question 5

A city government studies its heat island using midday satellite thermal imagery (land surface temperature) together with night-time air-temperature readings from weather stations. Consider the following inferences drawn from this data:

  1. If the midday imagery shows dry farmland outside the city reading hotter than city surfaces, the city no longer has a heat island.
  2. Night-time readings showing warmer air downtown than outside the city indicate an atmospheric heat island, even if the midday surface-temperature contrast is small.
  3. The hottest rooftop clusters on the midday imagery are candidate sites for cool-roof or green-roof retrofits.
  4. Very hot rooftop readings on the midday imagery show that the air inside the homes below is equally hot.

Which of the above inferences are valid?

Correct answer: B

Inferences 2 and 3 only are valid. Inference 1 fails: dry, sparsely vegetated rural surfaces can heat faster than city surfaces in daytime - NASA's training material describes exactly this arid-land pattern - yet the same city can still end the night warmer, because its materials release stored heat slowly; a single midday surface map cannot retire a heat island. Inference 2 holds: warmer canopy-layer air downtown at night is precisely how an atmospheric heat island shows up, and it can coexist with a modest daytime surface contrast - surface and atmospheric heat islands are different quantities, so one small reading does not cancel the other. Inference 3 holds: locating the hottest surfaces from LST maps and targeting reflective or planted retrofits at them is the standard mitigation-targeting use of these maps. Inference 4 fails: a rooftop pixel measures the roof surface, a different quantity from indoor air; the air inside homes depends on ventilation, shading and construction, and a conventional roof can run dramatically hotter than the surrounding air while interiors stay far cooler. Option A wrongly validates inference 1; option C wrongly adds inference 4; option D wrongly validates inference 1.

Source: NASA ARSET UHI Q&A Part 1 (Q7); US EPA, What Are Heat Islands and Compendium of Strategies
