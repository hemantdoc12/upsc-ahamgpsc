Monetary policy and the inflation target

Prices crossed the line. What does the framework actually require?

Fictional central-bank cases test the inflation target, the failure rule and the policy corridor against the notified framework.

Every bank, company, print path and policy scenario in the questions is fictional. Named notifications, RBI documents and official data support the framework concepts and dated figures only; nothing here is a market forecast, a prediction of the December MPC decision, or a finding about any institution.

Fifteen connections

01 / 15

A band around a number, not a point

India's inflation target is four per cent headline CPI with a tolerance band of two per cent on either side: two to six per cent. Read the band first. A print above four per cent is not a breach; the framework asks where the reading sits against six and two, not against four.

Gazette notification S.O. 1580(E), 25 March 2026

02 / 15

Who sets the target

The target is notified by the Central Government, in consultation with the Reserve Bank, under section 45ZA of the RBI Act, 1934. It is not the Bank's own rule. The current notification (S.O. 1580(E), 25 March 2026) is the third five-year setting, following 2016 and 2021.

Gazette notification S.O. 1580(E), 25 March 2026

03 / 15

The measured index is headline CPI

The framework reads the all-India Consumer Price Index (combined), not the Wholesale Price Index and not a core measure. October 2026 shows why the choice matters: CPI rose 6.09 per cent while WPI rose 12.29 per cent. Only the first enters the target.

Gazette notification S.O. 1580(E) · MoSPI CPI press release, October 2026

04 / 15

Breaches are read on quarterly averages

The framework does not react to a single month. October 2026 at 6.09 per cent is one month above the ceiling. Even one full quarter above six per cent on average would not by itself complete the failure test.

DEA framework notification, 27 June 2016 · MoSPI CPI press release, October 2026

05 / 15

Statutory failure needs three consecutive quarters

Under the 27 June 2016 framework notification, the Bank fails the target only when average inflation stays above the upper tolerance level (or below the lower) for any three consecutive quarters. A single strong or weak month cannot establish that.

DEA framework notification, 27 June 2016

06 / 15

What failure triggers

Section 45ZN then requires the Bank to report to the Central Government: the reasons for missing the target, the remedial actions it proposes, and the estimated time to return to it. The additional MPC meeting of 3 November 2022, called to draft such a report, is the working precedent.

DEA framework notification, 27 June 2016

07 / 15

A committee decides, by vote

Section 45ZB provides a six-member Monetary Policy Committee constituted by the Central Government by gazette notification. Each member has one vote, the quorum is four, and on a tie the Governor holds a casting vote. Unanimity is not required; the October 2026 hike happened to be unanimous.

RBI overview: Monetary Policy Committee · MPC resolution, 7 October 2026

08 / 15

The instrument is the repo rate inside a corridor

The operating instrument is the policy repo rate under the liquidity adjustment facility: 5.50 per cent since 7 October 2026. Around it, the standing deposit facility at 5.25 per cent marks the corridor floor and the marginal standing facility at 5.75 per cent marks the ceiling.

MPC resolution, 7 October 2026

09 / 15

Floor and ceiling are not interchangeable

The SDF absorbs surplus funds from banks at the floor; the MSF lends to banks at the ceiling. Swapping the two inverts the corridor logic: the floor must sit below the policy rate and the ceiling above it, or overnight rates would escape the band.

MPC resolution, 7 October 2026

10 / 15

Transmission takes time and can be partial

A repo change reaches prices through market rates, banks' deposit and lending rates, credit growth and demand. Each link can lag, and pass-through can differ between deposit and loan rates. A hike is not a switch; it is pressure applied over quarters.

MPC resolution, 7 October 2026

11 / 15

Stance is a signal, not an instrument

Alongside the October hike the MPC changed its stance to calibrated tightening. A stance signals the likely direction of future moves; it is not itself a rate change and binds no specific future decision.

MPC resolution, 7 October 2026

12 / 15

Real growth and inflation measure different things

Real GDP grew 7.8 per cent in Q1 2026-27 while October CPI ran at 6.09 per cent. There is no contradiction: real growth strips out price rise, and the two series answer different questions. Nominal measures include the price rise.

MPC resolution, 7 October 2026 · MoSPI CPI press release, October 2026

13 / 15

CPI-WPI divergence is information, not the target

WPI at 12.29 per cent in October, the highest in the new 2022-23 base series, was driven by fuel. WPI tracks goods at wholesale and excludes services, so it is not the target variable; read it as pipeline pressure that may reach CPI with a lag, not as a breach.

PIB provisional WPI, October 2026

14 / 15

Monetary policy leans on demand, not supply

A fuel-led or food-led shock can hold headline inflation high even when demand pressure is contained. Rate action works mainly on aggregate demand, so the framework's band and quarterly averaging give the committee room to judge how much of a rise is demand-driven. That judgement remains conditional on evidence, not automatic.

Gazette notification S.O. 1580(E) · DEA framework notification, 27 June 2016

15 / 15

Credibility is part of the framework

The 2026 notification extended the same target to 2031 rather than redesigning it. A stable, credible band shapes how households and firms set wages and prices, which is itself a channel through which inflation stays anchored.

Gazette notification S.O. 1580(E), 25 March 2026

Five Prelims questions

Questions 1-2 form Prelims 1/3; questions 3-4 form Prelims 2/3; question 5 alone is HARD. Choose first, then read the explanation for every option.

Question 1 / 5

With reference to India's inflation target as notified in March 2026, consider:
1. The target is four per cent headline CPI with a tolerance band of two per cent on either side.
2. It was notified by the Reserve Bank of India under its own rule-making power.
3. It applies for the period 1 April 2026 to 31 March 2031.
4. The index targeted is the Wholesale Price Index.
Which of the statements given above are correct?

Correct answer: B

A. Wrong. Statement 1 is correct, but statement 2 is not: the target is notified by the Central Government in consultation with the Bank under section 45ZA, not by the Bank under its own power.

B. Correct. Statements 1 and 3 match S.O. 1580(E) of 25 March 2026: four per cent with a two per cent band either side, for 1 April 2026 to 31 March 2031. The index basis is statutory: section 45ZA requires the target to be set in terms of the Consumer Price Index, and the notification applies it.

C. Wrong. Statement 2 misattributes the notification to the Bank, and statement 4 names the wrong index: the target reads on headline CPI, not WPI.

D. Wrong. Statements 1 and 3 are correct, but statement 4 fails: the target is set in terms of the Consumer Price Index under section 45ZA, so a WPI reading cannot be the targeted index.

Question 2 / 5

A fictional economy's monthly CPI inflation prints 5.8 per cent in July, 5.9 per cent in August and 6.1 per cent in September, for a quarterly average of about 5.93 per cent. Which conclusion follows under the notified framework?

Correct answer: A

A. Correct. The framework reads quarterly averages: 5.93 per cent stayed inside the band, and statutory failure needs three consecutive quarters outside it, so one month above six per cent establishes nothing by itself.

B. Wrong. Failure is defined on three consecutive quarters of average inflation outside the band, not on any one month's print.

C. Wrong. The assessment unit is the quarter, not the month; a monthly reading carries information but no formal breach.

D. Wrong. The average of 5.8, 5.9 and 6.1 is about 5.93 per cent, which is below the six per cent upper tolerance level.

Question 3 / 5

In a fictional corridor with the policy repo rate at 5.50 per cent, the standing deposit facility at 5.25 per cent and the marginal standing facility at 5.75 per cent: Bank M parks surplus funds overnight with the central bank, and Bank N borrows overnight from it at the marginal window. Which pairing is correct?

Correct answer: D

A. Wrong. This swaps the windows: 5.75 per cent is the marginal lending rate for borrowing banks, and 5.25 per cent is the deposit rate for parking surplus.

B. Wrong. The repo rate is the policy signal at the centre of the corridor; the standing windows price at the floor and the ceiling, not at the repo rate.

C. Wrong. M parking surplus receives the SDF floor, not the repo rate, and N borrowing at the marginal window pays the MSF ceiling, not the floor.

D. Correct. Surplus placed with the central bank earns the SDF floor of 5.25 per cent; marginal borrowing pays the MSF ceiling of 5.75 per cent, keeping overnight rates inside the corridor.

Question 4 / 5

A fictional central bank raises its policy rate by 25 basis points. Over the following quarter, banks raise deposit rates fully but lending rates only partially. Which inference is best supported?

Correct answer: B

A. Wrong. The facts show full pass-through to deposit rates but only partial pass-through to lending rates, so transmission is neither complete nor instant.

B. Correct. Transmission runs through several links and can be uneven; partial adjustment on the lending side after a quarter is exactly what incomplete, lagged transmission looks like.

C. Wrong. The deposit rates moved fully with the policy rate, so the change clearly can influence bank rates; the evidence is about degree and speed, not impossibility.

D. Wrong. Deposit rates moving with the policy rate is the expected direction; nothing in the scenario supports a fall.

Question 5 / 5 · HARD

In a fictional economy, average CPI inflation prints 6.2 per cent in Q1 and 6.1 per cent in Q2, and the first month of Q3 prints 6.09 per cent with two months of the quarter still to come. Wholesale inflation is 12.3 per cent, driven by fuel, while real GDP grows 7.8 per cent. Which assessment is correct?

Correct answer: C

A. Wrong. Two completed quarters above the ceiling plus one month of a third cannot establish failure; the test needs three consecutive quarters of average inflation outside the band, and Q3 has no average yet.

B. Wrong. Nothing in the framework requires waiting for failure; the committee can act on its assessment of the outlook at any scheduled meeting.

C. Correct. It keeps every condition precise: the failure test is incomplete because Q3's average must also stay above the ceiling to make a third consecutive quarter, early action by the committee is permitted, and the notified target reads on CPI, not WPI.

D. Wrong. The notified target reads on headline CPI; WPI is not the target variable, so it can neither settle the failure question nor substitute for the CPI average.
