Six States get new Governors, 12 Oct 2026

The news is a list of names. Behind it sit the text on who appoints a Governor and for how long, a Constitution Bench ruling on removal, two commissions that tried to reform the office, and a Presidential Reference answered less than a year ago on what a Governor must do with a Bill. What happened

Press-reported

The appointments

On Monday 12 Oct 2026 President Droupadi Murmu appointed Governors for six States. Rajendra Vishwanath Arlekar, Governor of Keralam, becomes full-time Governor of Tamil Nadu. Om Prakash Mathur, Governor of Sikkim, becomes Governor of Keralam. Four new names: Braj Mohan Ram (Odisha), Sudha Yadav (Karnataka), Dhal Singh Bisen (Uttarakhand) and Dushyant Gautam (Sikkim). A Rashtrapati Bhavan communique said the appointments take effect from the date each Governor takes charge.

Who they replace

Ram replaces Hari Babu Kambhampati in Odisha, who took charge in January 2025. Yadav succeeds Thaawarchand Gehlot in Karnataka. Bisen replaces Lt Gen (retd) Gurmit Singh in Uttarakhand. Gautam succeeds Mathur in Sikkim. The communique does not specify new assignments for Kambhampati, Gehlot or Gurmit Singh (India Today).

Who they are

Arlekar is a former Goa Assembly Speaker and has been Governor of Himachal Pradesh and Bihar; he held Tamil Nadu as additional charge from March, when R N Ravi moved to West Bengal. Mathur is a veteran BJP leader and former Rajya Sabha MP from Rajasthan, in Sikkim since July 2024. Yadav is a former BJP MP from Mahendragarh and a former member of the National Commission for Backward Classes; the press notes she will be Karnataka's second woman Governor after V S Ramadevi (1999 to 2002). Bisen is a former Lok Sabha MP from Balaghat. Gautam is a former BJP national general secretary and former Rajya Sabha MP.

01
PM IIE

Who appoints a Governor

The President named Governors for six States on 12 Oct. Under Article 155 a Governor is appointed by the President by warrant under hand and seal, and Article 153 provides for a Governor for each State. The Constitution gives the State government no formal role in the appointment, which is why the choice is read as a Union decision about a State office.

Static hooks, go deeper, common confusion

Static syllabus hooks not from the press; check the bare text

  • Art 153: a Governor for each State
  • Art 154: the State's executive power is vested in the Governor
  • Art 155: appointment by warrant under hand and seal

Go deeper

Read Articles 153 to 156 in one sitting. Notice what the text says about the appointment, and what it leaves out.

Common confusion

Governors are appointed, not elected. That separates the Indian office from an elected State governor in a presidential system.

02
PM II

Who can be Governor

A Governor must be an Indian citizen and at least 35 years old. The office is not compatible with a seat in Parliament or a State legislature, or with any other office of profit. Of the four new names, the press describes former Lok Sabha or Rajya Sabha members and a former BJP general secretary: the bar is on holding a seat while Governor, not on political experience.

Static hooks, go deeper, common confusion

Static syllabus hooks not from the press; check the bare text

  • Art 157: citizen of India, completed 35 years
  • Art 158: not a member of Parliament or a State legislature; no office of profit
  • Art 158(2): a legislator who is appointed vacates the seat on taking office (check)

Go deeper

Compare Article 158 with Articles 102 and 191 on disqualification of legislators. One bars holding a seat, the other lists the grounds for losing it.

Common confusion

Age and citizenship are the only formal qualifications. Any talk of other 'conditions' is convention, not text.

03
P

Term and pleasure

Article 156 says a Governor holds office during the pleasure of the President, and the usual term is five years. The term does not end the office automatically: the incumbent continues until a successor takes charge. That is why the communique says the appointments take effect on the date each Governor takes charge. The press says it does not name new assignments for the outgoing Governors.

Static hooks, go deeper, common confusion

Static syllabus hooks not from the press; check the bare text

  • Art 156(1): pleasure of the President
  • Art 156(2): resignation in writing to the President
  • Art 156(3): five-year term; continues until the successor enters office

Go deeper

Trace how 'pleasure' is used elsewhere in the Constitution, for example for Union ministers, and what the word does to security of tenure.

Common confusion

The five years is a ceiling that can be shortened by the pleasure clause, not a guarantee of five years.

04
PM II

One Governor, two States

Arlekar had held Tamil Nadu as additional charge since March, when R N Ravi was moved to West Bengal, and in that period administered the oath to Chief Minister C Joseph Vijay (press-reported). The proviso to Article 153, added in 1956, lets one person serve as Governor of two or more States. His appointment now makes the Tamil Nadu charge full-time.

Static hooks, go deeper, common confusion

Static syllabus hooks not from the press; check the bare text

  • Art 153 proviso, inserted by the Seventh Amendment (1956) (check)
  • Art 159: the oath or affirmation of the Governor
  • Art 163 and 164: the Governor acts with a Council of Ministers in each State

Go deeper

Ask what happens to a State's administration when its Governor is in another State most of the week. Look for the constitutional answer and the practical one.

Common confusion

Additional charge is not a second appointment of a different kind. It rests on the same proviso.

05
PM II

Moved, not removed

Mathur moves from Sikkim to Keralam, where Arlekar goes to Tamil Nadu. Dushyant Gautam fills Sikkim. The press says Mathur took charge in Sikkim in July 2024 and that the communique does not name new roles for Gehlot, Gurmit Singh or Kambhampati. A move is usually done by a fresh appointment; the Constitution has no separate transfer clause (check).

Static hooks, go deeper, common confusion

Static syllabus hooks not from the press; check the bare text

  • Art 155 and 156: appointment and pleasure
  • The practice of moving a Governor between States rests on appointment, not a named power (check)
  • Art 153 proviso for joint charge

Go deeper

Map the chain of moves on one page: who leaves where, who arrives where, and which States are touched by more than one move.

Common confusion

A move is not the same as a removal, and neither is the same as completing a term.

06
PM IIE

Can a Governor be removed?

Because the Governor serves at the President's pleasure, the question is whether the pleasure can be exercised on a whim. In B.P. Singhal v Union of India (2010), a Constitution Bench dealt with the removal of Governors after a change of government at the Centre. As commonly summarised, it held that the pleasure is not unfettered, that removal must rest on compelling reasons, and that a change in the Union government is not by itself such a reason (check).

Static hooks, go deeper, common confusion

Static syllabus hooks not from the press; check the bare text

  • B.P. Singhal v Union of India (2010), Constitution Bench
  • Art 156(1) read with Art 14: no arbitrary power
  • The Court did not read 'pleasure' as 'security of tenure' either (check)

Go deeper

Read the headnote of Singhal and the passage on reasons. Note who has to be able to explain a removal, and to whom.

Common confusion

'Pleasure' does not mean 'at will without limits'. It also does not mean 'a fixed five years'.

07
M II

What the commissions said

Two commissions shaped the debate. The Sarkaria Commission (1988) urged that the Governor be an eminent person from outside the State, detached from local politics, and be chosen after consulting the Chief Minister. The Punchhi Commission (2010) returned to appointment, tenure and removal in its report on Centre-State relations. Both are recalled from standard syllabus material and flagged for checking.

Static hooks, go deeper, common confusion

Static syllabus hooks not from the press; check the bare text

  • Sarkaria Commission, 1988: eminent person, outside the State, consult the Chief Minister (check)
  • Punchhi Commission, 2010: security of tenure and the removal process (check specifics)
  • Inter-State Council as the forum for such questions

Go deeper

Pick one recommendation from each report and test it against the six appointments in the news. Do not decide; just see which would change the process.

Common confusion

Commission recommendations are not law. Where they have been adopted, it has been by practice.

08
PM II

Aid and advice

Article 163 makes the Council of Ministers, headed by the Chief Minister, the body that aids and advises the Governor, except where the Constitution requires the Governor to act in his discretion. In Shamsher Singh v State of Punjab (1974), a seven-judge bench held that the Governor and the President are constitutional heads who act on advice, outside the discretionary areas.

Static hooks, go deeper, common confusion

Static syllabus hooks not from the press; check the bare text

  • Art 163(1): aid and advice, except in matters of discretion
  • Art 163(2): the Governor's decision on what is discretionary is final
  • Shamsher Singh v State of Punjab (1974)

Go deeper

List the powers where the text says 'in his discretion' and the powers where it is silent. The list is shorter than most people expect.

Common confusion

'Discretion' in the text and 'discretion' in the newspapers are not the same thing.

09
PM IIE

Bills and assent

When a State legislature passes a Bill, Article 200 lets the Governor assent, withhold assent, or reserve it for the President. For a Bill other than a Money Bill, the first proviso allows return to the House for reconsideration. Some Bills must be reserved, for instance where they would endanger the position of the High Court. Article 201 then sets out what the President does with a reserved Bill.

Static hooks, go deeper, common confusion

Static syllabus hooks not from the press; check the bare text

  • Art 200: assent, withhold, reserve; first proviso on return
  • Art 200 second proviso: Bills that must be reserved (check)
  • Art 201: the President's course on a reserved Bill

Go deeper

Draw the flow of a Bill from passage to law. Mark each place where the text names a time limit. Count them.

Common confusion

Withholding assent and rejecting a Bill are not the same act. The first proviso ties the two together, as the 2025 opinion makes clear.

10
M IIE

The 2025 Presidential Reference

On 20 Nov 2025 a five-judge Bench (CJI Gavai, Justices Surya Kant, Vikram Nath, Narasimha and Chandurkar) answered Special Reference No. 1 of 2025 under Article 143. As LiveLaw and The Hindu report it: courts cannot set timelines for the Governor or the President under Articles 200 and 201, and 'deemed assent' is not available. The Governor's choice on the merits is not justiciable, but a court may issue a limited direction to decide in a reasonable time where inaction is prolonged and unexplained. The Reference followed an earlier two-judge ruling in the Tamil Nadu Governor case that had set timelines.

Static hooks, go deeper, common confusion

Static syllabus hooks not from the press; check the bare text

  • Art 143: advisory jurisdiction
  • Special Reference No. 1 of 2025, opinion of 20 Nov 2025
  • Art 361: personal immunity, not a bar to this limited review (as reported)

Go deeper

Set the earlier Tamil Nadu ruling and the 2025 opinion side by side. Note what each says about time, and about courts.

Common confusion

The opinion is advisory under Article 143. It is persuasive and widely followed, but it is not the same as a judgment between parties.

11
PM II

Ordinances

When the legislature is not in session, Article 213 lets the Governor promulgate an ordinance if satisfied that the situation needs immediate action. The ordinance has the force of a law and lapses six weeks after the legislature reassembles, or earlier if it disapproves. Some ordinances need the President's prior instructions (check).

Static hooks, go deeper, common confusion

Static syllabus hooks not from the press; check the bare text

  • Art 213: ordinance-making power of the Governor
  • Six weeks from reassembly; lapse on disapproval
  • Compare Art 123 for the President

Go deeper

Look at how often a State re-promulgates the same ordinance and what the Court said about it in D.C. Wadhwa (1987) (check).

Common confusion

An ordinance is not a Bill. It is a stopgap with an expiry date set by the Constitution.

12
PM II

Chief Minister and Assembly

Under Article 164 the Governor appoints the Chief Minister, and the other ministers on the Chief Minister's advice. In Nabam Rebia (2016), a Constitution Bench dealt with the Governor's power under Article 174 to summon the Assembly and held that it is, in the main, exercised on the advice of the Council of Ministers, with only a narrow space for discretion. This is where the Governor's role in hung houses and floor tests is usually argued.

Static hooks, go deeper, common confusion

Static syllabus hooks not from the press; check the bare text

  • Art 164(1): Chief Minister appointed by the Governor
  • Art 174: summoning, proroguing and dissolving the Assembly
  • Nabam Rebia v Deputy Speaker (2016), Constitution Bench

Go deeper

Read Article 174 and then the operative paragraphs of Nabam Rebia. Decide for yourself how narrow 'narrow' is.

Common confusion

Appointing a Chief Minister and summoning a session are two different powers with two different rules.

13
PM II

Immunity and mercy

Article 361 gives the Governor personal immunity: no court answers for the exercise of the office's powers, and no criminal proceedings lie during the term. Article 161 gives the Governor the power to grant pardons and to suspend, remit or commute sentences for offences against State-law subjects. Article 72, the President's power, is wider and covers court-martial and death sentences (check).

Static hooks, go deeper, common confusion

Static syllabus hooks not from the press; check the bare text

  • Art 361: protection of the President and Governors
  • Art 161: pardon and related powers over State-law offences
  • Art 72: the President's wider pardon power (check)

Go deeper

Compare the texts of Articles 72 and 161 line by line. Underline each word that differs.

Common confusion

Immunity of the person is not immunity of the office, a point the 2025 opinion draws on (as reported).

14
PM II

Governor as Chancellor

The Governor is the Chancellor of many State universities, but the Constitution does not say so. The role comes from each State's university law. That is why the Governor's powers over appointing vice-chancellors vary State by State, and why the role has been contested in several States (check the individual cases).

Static hooks, go deeper, common confusion

Static syllabus hooks not from the press; check the bare text

  • State university Acts, not the Constitution, make the Governor Chancellor
  • Art 163: the Governor acts on advice, except where the text gives discretion
  • Entry 32, List II and Entry 25, List III: universities (check)

Go deeper

Pick one State's university Act and read the Chancellor's powers. Then ask whether a State could remove the role by amending its own law.

Common confusion

A Chancellor's power is statutory. It is not a constitutional power of the Governor.

15
M II

Art 356 and the Governor's report

Article 356 lets the President take over a State if satisfied, on a Governor's report or otherwise, that the government cannot be carried on in accordance with the Constitution. In S.R. Bommai v Union of India (1994), a nine-judge bench held that the proclamation is open to judicial review and set limits on its use. The Governor's report is where the office meets the biggest Union power over a State.

Static hooks, go deeper, common confusion

Static syllabus hooks not from the press; check the bare text

  • Art 356: failure of constitutional machinery
  • S.R. Bommai v Union of India (1994), nine judges
  • Art 355: the Union's duty to protect States

Go deeper

Read what Bommai says about the material a proclamation must rest on, and where the Governor's report fits.

Common confusion

The report is the usual basis but the text says 'or otherwise'. The President is not bound to wait for one.

Today's MCQ: answer and why

One Prelims-style question on today's item. This is the only drafted question on the page. The carousel slide shows the question. The key lives here.

With reference to the appointment and powers of Governors of States in India, and the Supreme Court's 2025 opinion on the Presidential Reference on assent to Bills, consider the following statements:

  1. Under Articles 155 and 156, the Governor is appointed by the President by warrant under his hand and seal, and holds office during the pleasure of the President.
  2. The Constitution does not allow the same person to be appointed Governor of two or more States.
  3. In its opinion of 20 November 2025, the Supreme Court held that courts cannot fix timelines for a Governor's decision on a Bill or treat the Bill as deemed assented, but may issue a limited direction in cases of prolonged, unexplained inaction.
  4. Under Article 200, the Governor may assent to a Bill, withhold assent, or reserve it for the President; according to the same opinion, withholding assent to a Bill (other than a Money Bill) must be accompanied by returning it to the Assembly.

Which of the statements given above are correct?

Answer: (c) 1, 3 and 4 only

(c) 1, 3 and 4 only. Correct. Statement 1 is Articles 155 and 156. Statement 3 is the 2025 opinion as reported: no timelines and no deemed assent, but a limited direction is possible for prolonged, unexplained inaction. Statement 4 is Article 200 with the first proviso, read the way the opinion reads it.

(a) 1 and 4 only. Fails because it drops statement 3. Mistake tested: assuming that because courts cannot fix timelines, they can do nothing at all when a Governor sits on a Bill.

(b) 1, 2 and 4 only. Fails on statement 2. The proviso to Article 153 allows one person to be Governor of two or more States, and Arlekar held Tamil Nadu alongside Keralam from March. Mistake tested: assuming one Governor per State is absolute.

(d) 2, 3 and 4 only. Fails on statement 2 and also rejects statement 1. Mistake tested: reading a Governor's term as a fixed, secure five years, when Article 156 ties the office to the President's pleasure (with the limits Singhal 2010 draws).
