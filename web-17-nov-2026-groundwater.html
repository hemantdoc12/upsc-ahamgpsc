One hidden resource, fifteen exam angles · 17 Nov 2026 · about eight minutes

The water beneath: how India stores, moves and spends its groundwater

Less to read. More to connect. Some of the monsoon ran off and flooded our streets. The rest took the other path - down through the soil, past the roots, into the rock. That hidden stock waters most of India's farms and much of its drinking supply, and it is spent without anyone ever seeing the balance.

A puzzle first, a term later. Clay can hold more water than sand, yet a sandy well usually out-yields a clay one - and a unit pumping five percent more than its extractable resource is a different policy problem from one using 87.5 percent of its recharge. Five questions on the mechanics of storage and flow, the 2025 national account, the NOC rulebook, and the scheme links Prelims loves.

Fifteen connected angles

01 · 01 - HOOK

The rain that did not run off

Yesterday we chased the rain that concrete turns into a flood. Today we follow the rain that took the other path - down through the soil, past the roots, into the rock. That hidden stock sustains farms and drinking-water supplies across India. It is also the one we spend without ever seeing the balance.

02 · 02 - CONCEPT

Where groundwater actually lives

Beneath your feet lies an unsaturated zone - pores holding air and films of water that roots can sip. Deeper down is the saturated zone, where every pore and crack is full. The upper surface of that saturated zone is the water table. Dig below it and the hole fills: you have made a well. Groundwater usually occupies pores and fractures in rock and soil rather than flowing in open underground rivers.

03 · 03 - MECHANISM

Porosity is not permeability

Porosity measures how much void space a rock has. Permeability measures how easily water moves through connected voids. Clay is the classic paradox: it is highly porous, yet yields little to wells, because its many very small pores hold water tightly - high specific retention, low permeability. A permeable, well-connected sandstone can store and transmit freely. Underground, water can travel metres in a day through one formation and centimetres in a century through another.

04 · 04 - MECHANISM

Two kinds of aquifer, two kinds of pressure

An unconfined aquifer has the water table as its ceiling; a static well open to that interval shows its water level at the water table. A confined aquifer is trapped between low-permeability layers and carries pressure from its uphill recharge zone. Drill into it and water rises above the top of the aquifer - the level it rises to maps the piezometric surface. Only when that surface stands above the ground does the well discharge on its own: a flowing artesian well.

05 · 05 - NUANCE

Soil moisture is not recharge

Much of the rain that soaks in never becomes groundwater. It lingers in the root zone as soil moisture, drawn back by plants and evaporation. Recharge is only the fraction that percolates below the roots and crosses the water table. This is why green fields can sit over falling wells - and why the monsoon remains the major source of India's recharge while paved cities leak that contribution away.

06 · 06 - MEASUREMENT

How India keeps the account

Each year the CGWB and the states estimate, assessment unit by assessment unit (generally blocks, tehsils, taluks or mandals), how much water is recharged, how much is extractable, and how much is drawn out. The 2025 assessment puts annual recharge at 448.52 billion cubic metres, extractable resources at 407.75, and extraction at 247.22. The ratio of extraction to extractable resource - the Stage of Extraction - stands at 60.63 percent for the country.

07 · 07 - MEASUREMENT

Four labels that decide policy

Each assessment unit is labelled by its Stage of Extraction: at or below 70 percent is Safe, above 70 and up to 90 is Semi-critical, above 90 and up to 100 is Critical, and above 100 is Over-exploited - drawing more than the annual extractable resource, which is recharge minus what must be left for natural discharge. The trend matters as much as the level: between 2017 and 2025, Safe units rose from 62.6 to 73.14 percent and Over-exploited units fell from 17.2 to 10.8 percent.

08 · 08 - MECHANISM

The cone of depression

In the standard teaching model, a pumping well lowers the water around itself into a cone - the water table in an unconfined aquifer, the pressure surface in a confined one; real drawdown shapes vary with the aquifer. Where wells tap hydraulically connected parts of an aquifer, drawdown reaching a neighbouring well can reduce its available head and yield. Pumping can also compact fine-grained aquifer systems, permanently cutting storage. Recovery can take a season or decades: extraction is shared even when ownership is not, and that interconnection is the governance problem in one diagram.

09 · 09 - CONCEPT

A quarter of the world's groundwater

India is often cited as the world's largest groundwater user - a parliamentary committee put its share at roughly a quarter of global withdrawals. The 2025 assessment shows where it goes: of 247 billion cubic metres extracted, 215 (87 percent) is for irrigation, 28 (11 percent) for domestic use including drinking, and 4 (2 percent) for industry. Dependence this large makes every percentage point on the Stage of Extraction a policy signal, not a statistic.

10 · 10 - CASE STUDY

Atal Bhujal Yojana: the pilot that ended

From April 2020 to October 2025, a World Bank-supported central sector pilot put groundwater management in community hands: 8,203 water-stressed gram panchayats in 229 blocks across seven states wrote water budgets and Water Security Plans, held over 1.28 lakh GP-level trainings, and mandated at least 33 percent women in decisions. A block-level assessment for 2023-25 found 180 of 229 blocks with improved groundwater levels; a QCI study separately recorded institutional gains. The pilot has concluded - as a model other states can adopt, not an ongoing scheme.

11 · 11 - INSTITUTION

The scientist and the regulator

Two central bodies share the file. The Central Ground Water Board is the science arm: it monitors levels, assesses resources with the states, and completed aquifer mapping over the entire mappable area - about 25 lakh sq km - under NAQUIM 1.0, with NAQUIM 2.0 now detailing priority areas. The Central Ground Water Authority, constituted under the Environment (Protection) Act 1986, regulates abstraction through no-objection certificates - directly in about 20 States and UTs, while the rest work through their own state authorities. Water itself is a State subject.

12 · 12 - POLICY

What the 2020 rulebook actually says

The CGWA's 24 September 2020 notification, para 4.1, does three things in Over-exploited units: it denies NOCs for groundwater abstraction to new industries outside the MSME category; it still allows NOCs for a new industry's workforce drinking and domestic use and for greenbelt development; and it bars new packaged drinking water industries even if they are MSMEs. A 2023 amendment separately exempts industries, mining and infrastructure projects drawing up to 5 cubic metres a day solely for drinking or domestic use in all units. On the funding side, a September 2025 MGNREGA amendment earmarks minimum shares of works by cost for water conservation: at least 65 percent in Over-exploited and Critical rural blocks, 40 in Semi-critical, 30 in Safe.

13 · 13 - MAINS USE

From supply-first to demand-side

India's groundwater debate is shifting from drilling more to spending less: participatory water budgeting, cropping-pattern correction in over-exploited blocks, micro-irrigation, recharge structures, and power-tariff reform that stops making pumping effectively free. For GS3 answers, pair the measurement (Stage of Extraction, block categories) with the instrument mix (purpose-specific NOC rules, MGNREGA cost earmarks, community models like the concluded Atal Jal pilot).

14 · 14 - ESSAY SEED

An invisible commons

Groundwater fails the two tests of easy governance: nobody sees it, and everybody's use is somebody else's cost. The tragedy is not that India pumps - it is that the account was invisible until the wells ran dry. Measurement, community budgeting and priced extraction are how an invisible commons becomes a managed one.

15 · 15 - ONE NEXT READ

Read the ledger where it is short

The National Compilation of Dynamic Ground Water Resources 2025 is a long technical document - go straight to its summary and methodology sections for the definitions and bands used today. Then read just para 4.1 of the CGWA's 24 September 2020 notification for the Over-exploited-unit NOC rule. Two targeted sections teach more than two whole documents.

Five-question Prelims challenge

The companion reels have no answers. Choose an option here to reveal the correct answer and explanation. These are original practice questions, not past UPSC questions.

Question 1

In a hard-rock district, farmers observe that water levels in wells near a heavily pumping borewell fall while it runs and recover after it stops, while wells farther away are unaffected. Which of the following best explains this pattern?

Select the best explanation.

Correct answer: B

The fall-while-pumping, recover-when-it-stops pattern is the signature of drawdown: pumping lowers head locally and water flows towards the well through connected fractures (USGS). Option A describes regional seasonal decline, which would not spare farther wells or reverse when pumping stops. Option C invokes stacked aquifers - real geology, but it would not produce this pumping-linked interference. Option D asserts permanent sealing of fractures, which is inconsistent with the observed recovery: the demonstrated effect here is reversible hydraulic-head drawdown, not permanent structural closure.

Source: USGS Water Science School, Aquifers and Groundwater (cone of depression and well interference; pumping faster than replenishment lowers water levels); USGS, Aquifer Compaction due to Groundwater Pumping (compaction is a real, often irreversible process in fine-grained aquifer systems - not the reversible drawdown observed here)

Question 2

With reference to how rocks store and transmit groundwater, consider the following statements:

  1. Porosity measures the share of void space in a rock; permeability measures how easily water moves through connected voids.
  2. Clay typically has very low porosity, which is why it yields little water to wells.
  3. In a confined aquifer under artesian pressure, water in a well can rise above the top of the aquifer without pumping.
  4. The water table is the lower boundary of the saturated zone.

Which of the statements given above is/are correct?

Correct answer: A

Statement 1 is correct - the USGS defines the two properties separately, and movement requires connected pores. Statement 2 is the trap: clay is highly porous; its many very small pores hold water tightly (high specific retention, low permeability), so the poor yield is a permeability story, not a porosity one. Statement 3 is correct - confining layers trap pressure and water rises towards the piezometric surface; only when that surface stands above the ground does the well become a flowing artesian well. Statement 4 is inverted: the water table is the upper surface of the saturated zone, not its lower boundary. Option by option: B and D both keep the clay trap (statement 2); C keeps the inverted boundary (statement 4); only A pairs the two correct statements.

Source: USGS Water Science School, Aquifers and Groundwater (porosity and permeability defined separately; water table as the upper surface of the saturated zone; confined aquifers under artesian pressure, flowing artesian wells); USGS SIR 2007-5050 (small pores give high specific retention - clay holds water tightly and yields it poorly)

Question 3

Consider the following pairs of institutions or initiatives and their features:

  1. Central Ground Water Board : maps and characterises aquifers under the National Aquifer Mapping and Management Programme (NAQUIM)
  2. Atal Bhujal Yojana : groundwater management centred only on constructing recharge structures, without community water budgets or demand-side measures
  3. Jal Sanchay Jan Bhagidari : a whole-of-society push mobilising communities for rainwater harvesting and recharge

How many of the above pairs are correctly matched?

Correct answer: B

Pair 1 is correct: aquifer mapping under the National Aquifer Mapping and Management Programme (NAQUIM) at 1:50,000 scale is CGWB's programme since 2012, with 1.0 completed over the mappable area and 2.0 detailing priority areas. Pair 2 is conceptually false: Atal Bhujal Yojana's core was exactly the opposite - participatory groundwater management in which all 8,203 gram panchayats prepared water budgets and Water Security Plans and updated them annually, with GP-level trainings and demand-side behavioural change; structures alone without budgets is the supply-side approach the pilot moved away from (PIB). Pair 3 is correct: the PIB presents Jal Sanchay Jan Bhagidari as the current whole-of-society effort, mobilising communities, corporate social responsibility (CSR) support and local bodies for rainwater-harvesting and recharge structures. Two pairs correctly matched.

Source: PIB, groundwater extraction shares and NAQUIM status (10 Aug 2026; NAQUIM 1.0 completed over about 25 lakh sq km of identified mappable area, NAQUIM 2.0 over 143 priority study areas); PIB, Atal Bhujal Yojana completion and impact (30 Jul 2026; participatory management with water budgets and Water Security Plans in all 8,203 GPs; Jal Sanchay Jan Bhagidari presented as the current whole-of-society push)

Question 4

With reference to the Dynamic Ground Water Resources Assessment 2025, consider the following statements:

  1. An assessment unit with a Stage of Extraction above 100 percent is categorised as Over-exploited.
  2. An assessment unit with a Stage of Extraction of 85 percent is categorised as Critical.
  3. Between 2017 and 2025, the share of Safe assessment units rose while the share of Over-exploited units declined.
  4. The assessment estimates India's overall Stage of Extraction at over 100 percent.

Which of the statements given above is/are correct?

Correct answer: C

Statement 1 is correct: extraction above 100 percent of the annual extractable resource means Over-exploited. Statement 2 misplaces the boundary: Safe runs up to and including 70 percent, Semi-critical covers above 70 to 90, so 85 percent is Semi-critical; Critical begins above 90, not at 85 or at 90 itself. Statement 3 is correct on the trend: Safe units rose from 62.6 to 73.14 percent and Over-exploited units fell from 17.2 to 10.8 percent between 2017 and 2025. Statement 4 is the exaggeration: the national Stage of Extraction is 60.63 percent - stress is concentrated in specific blocks and states, which is why the categories exist. Option by option: A and B both keep the boundary error (statement 2); D keeps the exaggeration (statement 4); only C pairs the two correct statements.

Source: PIB, Dynamic Groundwater Resources Assessment 2025 (29 Jan 2026; recharge 448.52 BCM, extractable 407.75 BCM, extraction 247.22 BCM, Stage of Extraction 60.63%; Safe units 62.6% to 73.14% and Over-exploited 17.2% to 10.8% between 2017 and 2025); PIB, categorisation boundary confirmation (11 Mar 2026; Safe at or below 70%, Over-exploited above 100%). The Semi-critical and Critical bounds (above 70 to 90, above 90 to 100) come from the 2025 compilation's methodology table via an indexed mirror, not visually inspected firsthand.

Question 5

A rural assessment unit in a State where the Central Ground Water Authority (CGWA) regulates groundwater extraction records an annual recharge of 1.20 billion cubic metres (BCM), an annual extractable resource of 1.00 BCM and an annual extraction of 1.05 BCM. A new large-scale industry - not a Micro, Small or Medium Enterprise (MSME) - applies for a No Objection Certificate (NOC) to abstract groundwater for its manufacturing process. Consider the following statements:

  1. The unit is categorised Over-exploited, because its Stage of Extraction is 105 percent, not 87.5 percent.
  2. The industry can be granted the NOC for its process water, provided it installs digital water flow meters and submits a rainwater-harvesting proposal.
  3. The same industry may still be granted an NOC for drinking and domestic use by its workforce and for greenbelt development, subject to other applicable conditions.
  4. A new packaged drinking water plant holding MSME status can be granted an NOC in this unit, since the MSME exception covers all small industries.
  5. At least 65 percent of the works taken up in this block under the Mahatma Gandhi National Rural Employment Guarantee Scheme, measured by the number of works, must be water-related works.

Which of the statements given above is/are correct?

Correct answer: A

Statement 1 is the calculation the question is built on: the Stage of Extraction divides extraction by the annual EXTRACTABLE resource (1.05/1.00 = 105 percent, Over-exploited), not by total recharge (1.05/1.20 = 87.5 percent, which would wrongly read as Semi-critical). Statement 2 is false: compliance conditions such as flow meters and rainwater-harvesting proposals are genuine NOC requirements, but they cannot lift the categorical bar - in an Over-exploited unit a new non-MSME industry simply is not eligible for a process-water NOC. Statement 3 is true: para 4.1 expressly keeps NOCs possible for a new industry's workforce drinking/domestic and greenbelt use - conditional eligibility, subject to the notification's other conditions, not an automatic grant. Statement 4 is false: the MSME exception does not extend to new packaged drinking water industries, which are barred even with MSME status (CGWA SOP, FAQ 6). Statement 5 is false on its measure: the September 2025 MGNREGA Schedule amendment earmarks a minimum of 65 percent of works IN TERMS OF COST in Over-exploited and Critical rural blocks, not by the number of works. Option by option: B drops the true purpose carve-out (3) and keeps the works-count error (5); C drops the correct classification (1) and keeps the conditions-can-lift-the-bar error (2); D keeps both the packaged-water error (4) and the works-count error (5); only A pairs exactly the two correct statements.

Source: CGWA Notification S.O. 3289(E), 24.09.2020, Gazette (para 4.1: in Over-exploited units no NOC for new industry abstraction except MSME; NOC remains possible for workforce drinking/domestic and greenbelt use; new packaged drinking water industries barred even if MSME); CGWA Standard Operating Procedure v9.1, FAQ 3-6; MGNREGS Schedule I amendment S.O. 4288(E), 23.09.2025 (minimum 65% of works in Over-exploited and Critical rural blocks, in terms of cost; gazettetracker.com transcription of the Gazette, labelled as mirror)
