The new scheme

The Union Cabinet has approved CMPS - interest subvention on plant loans, per-container assistance on sales, and testing support - to build a domestic container manufacturing ecosystem and cut import dependence (PIB, 28 Oct 2026).

The components

Interest Subvention Assistance (Rs.859 crore) gives a 3% per annum interest subvention on eligible term loans for greenfield facilities and brownfield expansion. Operational Expenditure Assistance (Rs.8,891 crore) bridges the cost gap on each container sold - Rs.49,500 for a 20-ft, Rs.86,625 for a 40-ft, Rs.37,125-74,250 for a 20-ft specialised container. Testing and certification assistance (Rs.150 crore) supports common facilities such as dynamic longitudinal impact testing (PIB).

Who qualifies

Greenfield facilities must have an annual capacity of at least 1,00,000 TEUs; brownfield expansions must add at least 25,000 TEUs within three years of in-principle approval, and comply with global container standards. Manufacturers must meet a minimum local-content requirement of 70% through FY 2031-32, rising to 90% from FY 2032-33 (PIB).

The wider stack

The scheme sits under the Ministry of Ports, Shipping and Waterways and alongside the Bharat Container Shipping Line (MoU of February 2026: SCI, CONCOR, JNPA, V.O. Chidambaranar and Chennai Port Authorities, Sagarmala Finance Corporation), the Merchant Shipping Act 2025, Coastal Shipping Act 2025 and Indian Ports Act 2025, and the PM Gati Shakti, National Logistics Policy and Sagarmala network, plus ONOP, Maritime Single Window and e-Samudra on the digital side (PIB).

The hub: one manufacturing scheme ties the Union Budget, port-led logistics, the 2025 maritime laws and the question of whether India should build the box that carries its trade.

Today's MCQ: answer and why

One Prelims-style question on today's item. This is the only drafted question on the page. The carousel slide shows the question. The key lives here.

With reference to the Container Manufacturing Promotion Scheme (CMPS), consider the following statements:

  1. It was approved with a budgetary outlay of Rs.10,000 crore for a period up to FY 2039-40.
  2. It provides a uniform amount of assistance per container, irrespective of container type.
  3. Its minimum local-content requirement rises from 70% to 90% from FY 2032-33.
  4. It is administered by the Ministry of Steel.

Answer: (a) 1 and 3 only

(a) is correct: CMPS was approved with an outlay of Rs.10,000 crore up to FY 2039-40, and its minimum local-content requirement rises from 70% to 90% from FY 2032-33 (PIB, 28 Oct 2026).

(b) includes false statement 2: assistance varies by container type - Rs.49,500 for a 20-ft, Rs.86,625 for a 40-ft and Rs.37,125-74,250 for a 20-ft specialised container (PIB).

(c) includes false statements 2 and 4: rates vary by type, and the scheme sits under the Ministry of Ports, Shipping and Waterways, not the Ministry of Steel (PIB).

(d) includes false statement 4 for the same reason. Source: PIB release and background note of 28 October 2026.
