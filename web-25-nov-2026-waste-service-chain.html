02 / 15

Four streams are more than two labels

Rule 5 of the 2026 rules names wet waste, dry waste, sanitary waste and special care waste as four source-separated streams. The rules specify commencement on 1 April 2026. A clean paper item, kitchen peel, used sanitary pad and household compact fluorescent lamp (CFL) therefore should not be collapsed into a single wet/dry exercise. Secure wrapping and separate sanitary storage matter. These are scoped requirements in the named rules, not a claim that every waste type falls under this rulebook.

03 / 15

Keep the distinction during transport

Separating material at home loses value if collection remixes it. Rule 8 describes separate compartments for wet/dry waste, arrangements for other specified streams and prevention of intermixing through transport to the designated facility. A photograph of separate household bins does not establish what happened later. Nor do our teaching colours claim a mandatory national bin-colour code. Check the applicable authorised local instructions in real use.

04 / 15

Wet and biodegradable are not a treatment certificate

A stream label helps route material; it does not certify that contamination, particle size, moisture, operating conditions or product quality are suitable. Biological treatment depends on what microbes can break down and on controlled process conditions. Plastic mixed into a kitchen-waste load does not become compostable merely because the truck is labelled wet waste. This is a mechanism lesson, not a plant-selection threshold or claim about a real facility.

Solid Waste Management Rules, 2026, Gazette hosted by MPCB · DDWS Biodegradable Waste Management Manual

05 / 15

Aerobic composting needs air as well as feed

The rules define aerobic composting as controlled microbial decomposition of organic matter in oxygen. The technical manual explains microbial production of carbon dioxide, water, heat and humus-like material. Heat is a process consequence, not proof by itself that every part of a heap is mature or safe. Keep the word aerobic: the manual also discusses anaerobic decomposition, so we do not assert that all processes called composting use the same oxygen conditions.

Solid Waste Management Rules, 2026, Gazette hosted by MPCB · DDWS Biodegradable Waste Management Manual

06 / 15

More moisture is not always better

Microbes need moisture. Yet very high moisture can occupy spaces through which air would otherwise reach decomposing material, encouraging anaerobic conditions. This explains why adding unlimited water is not a sound remedy for an aerobic composting problem. Observe the feed and process before selecting corrective action. No numerical operating threshold or guaranteed treatment time is taught here.

DDWS Biodegradable Waste Management Manual

07 / 15

Anaerobic digestion is controlled absence of oxygen

Controlled anaerobic digestion differs from deliberately aerated composting. The manual describes biomethanation in an enclosed, controlled environment, producing biogas mainly containing methane and carbon dioxide plus slurry. A foul-smelling mixed dump is not thereby an engineered biogas plant. Gas capture, appropriate use and the residual stream are parts of the system, not optional evidence-free assumptions.

08 / 15

An output can still need further treatment

A biogas unit does not make its residual slurry vanish. The manual describes use as biofertilizer after suitable treatment. The mere word biological does not establish unrestricted safe discharge or use. Similarly, recovered compost needs appropriate quality evidence before a particular use is claimed. Distinguish the existence of an output from proof that it meets the relevant requirement.

09 / 15

Dry waste contains more than recyclables

The 2026 definition includes recyclable and non-recyclable material within dry waste. Therefore dry does not mean every item can enter the same recycling process. Material, contamination and the available appropriate route matter. A sorting reject is not automatically authorised for any convenient destination. Avoid turning a useful classification into a universal technology or disposal permission.

10 / 15

Sorting and recycling are different events

A material recovery facility separates suitable fractions. The rules define recycling as transforming segregated non-biodegradable solid waste into new material or product. An MRF receipt therefore proves neither that transformation occurred nor that every kilogram was recyclable. Seek the relevant onward record and process evidence. A facility name or portal entry is a lead, not a substitute for establishing the claimed event.

11 / 15

A landfill is an engineered system, not a renamed dump

The rules distinguish dump sites from sanitary landfilling. Schedule II(D) connects lining with leachate collection and treatment, and separation of external stormwater. A soil cover alone does not establish all these protections. Our diagram would be conceptual, not a construction design or permission to build at a chosen location. Applicable authorisation, design and monitoring requirements remain separate.

12 / 15

Follow water through the waste

Rain passing through waste can carry dissolved and suspended contaminants into leachate. Diverting external runoff reduces contact; lining and collection help contain the contaminated liquid; treatment deals with what is collected. These measures perform different jobs. Mixing clean runoff with leachate does not make the pollution disappear by dilution. The rules require treatment of the mixed water when runoff mixes with leachate or solid waste.

13 / 15

A residue route needs its full conditions

Rule 14(4) specifies eligible landfill material and includes transitional and processing-linkage provisos. It should not be flattened into either every reject may be landfilled or no exception or transition exists. This lesson tests protective mechanisms and the need to check eligibility, not the wording of an exceptionless disposal ban. Read the applicable complete provision for an actual consignment.

14 / 15

A missing receipt is a question, not a verdict

Suppose a fictional dispatch sheet records 60 tonnes and matched receiving records account for 45 tonnes. The 15 tonne documentation difference merits reconciliation of batch identities, weighing bases, timestamps and missing records. It does not alone prove illegal dumping or theft. Conversely, a receiving entry does not prove actual recycling. Evidence should answer the exact stage and assertion being tested.

15 / 15

Use a chain of evidence in Mains

Build an answer around the input stream, suitable process, control conditions, residual route and independently checkable output. Distinguish collection from treatment and documented receipt from verified conversion. Explain which missing record would change the conclusion. Cost, visible cleanliness or a digital dashboard alone cannot settle all environmental and service outcomes. The framework is a way to organise evidence, not an allegation about any named city.

Five Prelims questions

Questions 1-2 form Prelims 1/3; questions 3-4 form Prelims 2/3; question 5 alone is HARD. Choose first, then read the explanation for every option.

Question 1 / 5

Under the Solid Waste Management Rules, 2026, consider:
1. Source segregation includes wet, dry, sanitary and special care streams.
2. Dry waste contains only recyclable material.
3. Used sanitary waste is to be securely wrapped and stored separately.
4. Separation at source permits remixing during transport.
Which statements are correct?

Correct answer: A

A. Correct. Statements 1 and 3 match the four-stream and sanitary-wrapping requirements. Statement 2 excludes non-recyclable dry waste wrongly; statement 4 contradicts transport segregation.

B. Wrong. Statement 1 is correct, but dry waste includes non-recyclable material, defeating statement 2. The option also omits correct statement 3.

C. Wrong. Statement 3 correctly requires secure separate handling of sanitary waste, but statement 2 wrongly excludes non-recyclable material from dry waste. The option also omits correct statement 1.

D. Wrong. Statements 1 and 3 are correct, but adding 4 introduces the false permission to remix during transport.

Question 2 / 5

An aerobic composting bed becomes waterlogged. Which explanation best connects the observed condition with its biological process?

Correct answer: C

A. Wrong. Oxygen can dissolve in water, but that fact does not establish improved oxygen supply in a waterlogged compost bed. Excess water can obstruct the air spaces relevant here.

B. Wrong. Reduced oxygen does not establish an enclosed, controlled digestion system, gas capture or appropriate residual handling.

C. Correct. Microbes need moisture, yet excessive moisture can fill air spaces and encourage anaerobic conditions. The explanation concerns the specified aerobic process, not a universal rule for every decomposition method.

D. Wrong. The manual explicitly identifies moisture as necessary for microbial activity. The problem is excessive moisture, not the existence of moisture.

Question 3 / 5

For controlled aerobic composting and biomethanation, consider:
1. Both rely on microbial action on suitable organic matter.
2. Aerobic composting takes place in oxygen.
3. Biomethanation produces methane-rich biogas.
4. Slurry leaving a biogas plant is automatically suitable for unrestricted discharge.
Which statements are correct?

Correct answer: D

A. Wrong. Statements 1 and 2 are correct, but the option omits the methane-rich biogas in statement 3.

B. Wrong. Statement 2 is correct, but 4 makes an unsupported unrestricted-discharge claim and omits correct 1 and 3.

C. Wrong. Statements 1 and 3 are correct, but 4 wrongly removes treatment and use conditions; statement 2 is also omitted.

D. Correct. Statements 1-3 describe the controlled biological processes. Statement 4 fails because residual slurry still requires appropriate treatment and handling for its intended use.

Question 4 / 5

A fictional waste facility diverts external stormwater, uses a liner, and collects leachate for treatment. Which inference is justified?

Correct answer: B

A. Wrong. A liner is a containment barrier, not a treatment process that removes every dissolved pollutant.

B. Correct. Diversion limits contact, lining supports containment and collection enables treatment. Actual performance and compliance still need evidence.

C. Wrong. Collection gathers the contaminated liquid; it does not itself treat it. Schedule II(D) separately requires management including collection and treatment.

D. Wrong. Dilution is not proof of pollutant removal. The rules require treatment of the mixed water when runoff mixes with leachate or solid waste.

Question 5 / 5 · HARD

A fictional dry-waste batch has 80 tonnes dispatched. Matched receipts account for 68 tonnes. Of the received mass, 75% is sorted as recyclable; conversion records verify new material equal to 80% of that recyclable fraction. These are successive fractions of the same batch. Which assessment is correct?

Correct answer: A

A. Correct. Received 68; recyclable 68 x 0.75=51; verified conversion 51 x 0.80=40.8 tonnes. Relative to dispatch 40.8/80=51%. Unmatched receipts 80-68=12 tonnes. Their cause and the destination of other fractions require evidence; no dumping verdict follows.

B. Wrong. 60% is the converted share of received mass, not dispatched mass. The receipt stage contributes another factor 68/80=85%; 0.85 x 0.75 x 0.80=51%.

C. Wrong. 85% is the matched received share, not the verified converted share. It skips sorting and actual transformation.

D. Wrong. The 51% calculation is right, but 49% not verified converted is not proof of dumping. It includes different recorded or unresolved stages, and the unmatched-dispatch amount is 12 tonnes, not 39.2 tonnes.
