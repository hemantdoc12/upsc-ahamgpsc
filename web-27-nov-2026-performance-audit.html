02 / 15

Separate inputs from activity

Money, equipment and staff time are resources. Operating vehicles or holding inspections are activities. A count of trips says something happened but does not alone establish the amount of suitable service delivered. These labels are an analytical model for this fictional programme, not a claim that every organisation must classify each indicator identically. State what the indicator measures before treating it as success.

03 / 15

An output is not every intended outcome

Delivered and verified loads can be a direct service output. Reliable household service and reduced uncollected waste can be intended results further along the chain. A health improvement would be a more distant claim requiring its own evidence. The number of loads alone does not establish that causal path. CAG performance audit includes inputs, processes, outputs, outcomes and results rather than equating them.

04 / 15

Economy asks more than which bid is cheapest

The 2020 framework describes minimising resource costs while obtaining appropriate quantity and quality in due time and at the best price. A low-price item unavailable when needed, or unsuitable for the job, does not establish economy just by being cheapest. This is an audit concept, not permission to ignore an actual tender, change award criteria after bids or select a preferred supplier.

05 / 15

Efficiency relates resources to delivered output

Suppose fictional teams deliver the same verified service quality. One produces more accepted output per worker-hour. That comparison can support a narrowly defined productivity or efficiency observation. Count the relevant inputs and outputs on consistent boundaries and timing. A greater raw output with more hours does not automatically mean a better output-per-hour ratio.

06 / 15

Effectiveness tests the objective

A programme can use resources carefully yet fail its intended service objective. Conversely, meeting a target does not by itself establish the least resource cost. The 2020 framework relates effectiveness to objectives and intended outputs/outcomes. State the relevant objective and threshold first, then assess the supplied evidence rather than allowing any rising number to stand in for success.

07 / 15

A good indicator can still create a blind spot

In our hypothetical contract, paying only for trip counts might reward more short trips without increasing useful delivered service. That is a plausible incentive risk, not proof that an actual operator behaved this way. Add quality, destination and citizen-service checks relevant to the objective. Do not solve a weak target by collecting more copies of the same incomplete measure.

Use the correct denominator

Cost per trip, cost per accepted tonne and cost per reliably served household answer different questions. A lower value on one ratio need not imply a lower value on another. Define the period and inclusion rules, check units, then calculate. Our numbers are teaching inputs, not market rates, and unit cost alone does not establish compliance or causal impact.

09 / 15

Quality belongs inside the comparison

A fast team whose loads fail the stated acceptance standard cannot count rejected loads as accepted output in a quality-adjusted comparison. Yet a rejection itself needs reliable criteria and evidence. Use the stated service standard consistently for both teams. Quantity, quality and timing belong together in the efficiency framework; this is not a general rule that rejection always means fraud.

10 / 15

More documents do not necessarily repair weak evidence

The 2017 standards distinguish sufficient evidence in quantity from appropriate evidence in relevance, validity and reliability. They expressly warn that merely obtaining more evidence does not compensate for poor quality. Many duplicated self-reports do not become independent confirmation just because the file is thicker. Consider source, controls, direct observation and corroboration in context.

11 / 15

A dashboard inherits the weaknesses of its inputs

Electronic presentation can help organise records, but reliability depends on the underlying system and controls. Confirm that timestamps, batch identities and acceptance records support the claim displayed. A green tile is not an independent witness. Equally, a data error is not by itself proof of dishonest intent; establish cause and effect before making a stronger finding.

Objectivity includes the appearance of familiarity risk

The named SAI India framework addresses objectivity, familiarity and conflicts of interest, including rotation of key audit personnel as a safeguard. In a fictional municipal review, a close relationship with a supplier is a risk to disclose and manage under the applicable rules. It is neither proof that a contract is corrupt nor a universal legal finding of automatic disqualification. Keep the framework and institutional scope explicit.

13 / 15

Before and after is not automatically because of

Suppose complaints decline after new vehicles arrive. The timing is compatible with an improvement but does not isolate its cause. Reporting behaviour, season, service coverage or other changes could affect the comparison. The audit requirement for relevant, reliable evidence supports checking alternative explanations; the example is analytical reasoning, not a new causal estimate or finding.

14 / 15

A balanced finding states what is and is not established

An audit-style conclusion can identify the criterion, supported observation, significance and unresolved cause, then recommend a targeted check. The 2017 standards call for objective, fair and balanced assessment and discussion of preliminary findings. Do not upgrade a suspicious pattern into a legal determination of fraud, or suppress an evidenced failure because intent is not yet established.

CAG Auditing Standards 2017

15 / 15

Connect the institution to public value

A strong Mains answer links lawful authority, eligible finance, suitable process, rights-aware service, measurable objectives and impartial evidence. Economy, efficiency and effectiveness are complementary questions, not interchangeable praise words. Conclude conditionally: what decision the evidence supports, which risk remains and which record or check would change the assessment. No actual procurement, penalty or court recommendation follows from this invented file.

Five Prelims questions

Questions 1-2 form Prelims 1/3; questions 3-4 form Prelims 2/3; question 5 alone is HARD. Choose first, then read the explanation for every option.

Question 1 / 5

Using the Comptroller and Auditor General (CAG) 2020 performance-audit concepts, consider:
1. Economy includes appropriate quantity, quality and timing of resources.
2. Efficiency concerns the resource-output relationship.
3. Effectiveness concerns objectives and intended results.
4. The cheapest purchase price alone establishes all three.
Which statements are correct?

Correct answer: D

A. Wrong. 1 is correct, but 4 reduces distinct performance questions to purchase price and ignores quality/timing/results. 2 and 3 are also correct.

B. Wrong. 2 and 3 are correct, but the 2020 economy definition also supports 1.

C. Wrong. 1 and 2 are correct, but 4 does not establish any complete assessment merely from cheapest purchase price; 3 is omitted.

D. Correct. 1-3 match the named framework. 4 ignores resource suitability and the separate questions of resource-output relationship and intended results.

Question 2 / 5

A fictional reviewer has many identical unverified operator reports but no destination or acceptance evidence. Under the named 2017 audit-evidence framework, which conclusion is strongest?

Correct answer: B

A. Wrong. Duplicating a source does not make its content independently confirmed or relevant to a missing stage.

B. Correct. Sufficiency and appropriateness are linked; quantity alone does not repair poor-quality evidence. The claim needs suitable verification.

C. Wrong. The standards recognise that internally generated evidence can be more reliable where related controls are effective. It is not automatically worthless.

D. Wrong. The evidence gap limits the conclusion. It does not establish the legal elements or cause of fraud.

Question 3 / 5

Two fictional teams perform equally suitable accepted work. A delivers 90 units in 30 worker-hours; B delivers 100 units in 40 worker-hours. Which conclusion follows?

Correct answer: C

A. Wrong. Raw output ignores hours: A 90/30=3; B 100/40=2.5 accepted units per hour.

B. Wrong. Completion of assigned work does not make the calculated resource-output ratios equal.

C. Correct. The defined productivity ratio favours A. Programme objectives, outcomes and procurement economy require additional evidence.

D. Wrong. A resource-output ratio is an efficiency-related measure; it does not establish purchase prices, quantity/quality/timing or overall procurement economy.

Question 4 / 5

A fictional review-team member has a close relationship with a supplier. Using the scoped Supreme Audit Institution (SAI) India 2017 ethics framework as a teaching reference, which response is best supported?

Correct answer: A

A. Correct. The framework links objectivity with familiarity/conflict safeguards. The facts call for applicable risk management, not an unsupported legal or corruption verdict.

B. Wrong. A relationship can be a relevant risk but does not alone prove corrupt conduct or causation.

C. Wrong. Purchase price does not remove objectivity or appearance risks.

D. Wrong. The named SAI scope is not a universal municipal recusal statute. Apply the actual institution's relevant rules.

Question 5 / 5 · HARD

Two fictional options serve comparable areas over the same period. X costs Rs 90,000, uses 300 worker-hours and delivers 180 tonnes meeting the acceptance standard. Y costs Rs 84,000, uses 240 worker-hours and delivers 160 accepted tonnes. The stated objective is at least 170 accepted tonnes. Which assessment is correct?

Correct answer: D

A. Wrong. X 90,000 / 180=Rs 500 per accepted tonne; Y 84,000 / 160=Rs 525. A lower total cost does not establish a lower unit cost.

B. Wrong. X 180/300=0.60 tonnes per worker-hour; Y 160/240=2/3, about 0.667. X meets 170; Y does not.

C. Wrong. Better output per worker-hour does not replace the specified 170 tonne objective. Y delivers 160.

D. Correct. X unit cost is Rs 500 and output 180 meets 170. Y labour productivity is about 0.667 versus 0.60, but its 160 accepted tonnes misses 170. This does not alone establish complete economy, service outcomes or the overall procurement winner.
