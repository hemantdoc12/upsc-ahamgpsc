One news item, fifteen exam angles · 11 Nov 2026 · about eight minutes

Coal into gas: the Rs 37,500 crore bet on coal gasification

Less to read. More to connect. The Ministry of Coal closed Round 2 of its Rs 37,500 crore surface gasification scheme with two NTPC proposals - urea, and methanol with DME - and opened Round 3 the same day. What gasification actually makes, why India wants it, and the traps in the fine print.

Two schemes, not one. An Rs 8,500 crore incentive scheme predates the 2026 one. The record-keeping is the exam.

Fifteen connected angles

01 · News

Round 2 closes, Round 3 opens

On 10 November 2026 the Ministry of Coal announced that Round 2 of the Scheme for Promotion of Surface Coal/Lignite Gasification Projects had concluded with two applications, both from NTPC Limited: one for urea, one for methanol and dimethyl ether (DME). Round 3 opened the same day and runs to 11 January 2027.

02 · Schemes

The scheme's design

The Union Cabinet approved the scheme on 13 May 2026 with a total financial outlay of Rs 37,500 crore. Its purpose is to accelerate surface coal and lignite gasification projects through successive application rounds, encouraging wider industry participation and investment.

03 · Economy

Why gasify coal at all

India has abundant coal but imports most of its oil, much of its gas, and a significant share of its urea and methanol. Gasification converts domestic coal into synthesis gas, a feedstock for fertilisers, chemicals, fuels and hydrogen, substituting imports and adding value to an indigenous resource.

04 · Science & Technology

What syngas is

Gasification reacts coal with a controlled amount of oxygen and steam at high temperature. The product is synthesis gas, or syngas: mainly carbon monoxide and hydrogen, with some carbon dioxide and methane. It is an intermediate, not a final fuel.

05 · Science & Technology

Surface versus underground

Surface gasification uses mined coal fed into a reactor. Underground coal gasification (UCG) converts the coal in the seam itself, through boreholes, and brings the gas up. The Rs 37,500 crore scheme covers surface projects only; UCG remains largely at pilot stage in India.

06 · Science & Technology

From syngas to urea, methanol and DME

Hydrogen from syngas combined with nitrogen gives ammonia; ammonia plus carbon dioxide gives urea. Syngas also converts directly to methanol, and methanol dehydrates to dimethyl ether, a clean-burning fuel that can blend with or substitute LPG.

07 · Economy

The 100 MT by 2030 target

The scheme is tied to the national target of achieving 100 million tonnes of coal gasification capacity by 2030. Successive application rounds are the mechanism the Ministry of Coal is using to draw project proposals toward that target.

08 · Schemes

Two schemes, not one

The Rs 37,500 crore scheme of May 2026 is not the first. An earlier Rs 8,500 crore financial incentive scheme for coal/lignite gasification is already running its own rounds and categories; the Ministry of Coal's What's New page listed its Round III for Categories II and III as recently as 13 October 2026.

09 · Economy

High-ash coal and the technology question

Indian coal typically carries high ash content, which stresses conventional gasifiers designed for low-ash imported coal. Technology choice, fluidised-bed and other ash-tolerant designs, is therefore central to making gasification work at scale in India.

10 · Environment

Cleaner than burning, but still coal

Gasification concentrates pollutants for easier capture and enables chemicals without combustion, so it is cleaner than directly burning coal. But syngas routes still release carbon dioxide in the process, and without carbon capture the climate benefit is limited.

11 · Economy

NTPC's diversification bet

NTPC, a Maharatna central public sector undertaking and India's largest power generator, was the sole applicant in Round 2, with proposals for urea and for methanol with DME. It signals a power utility moving downstream into coal-based chemicals.

12 · Economy

The import-substitution arithmetic

India imports a significant share of its urea and nearly all of its methanol requirement. Domestic syngas-based production links the fertiliser subsidy bill, the chemicals trade deficit and energy security into one policy lever: gasifying Indian coal.

13 · Exam skills

Three pairs UPSC can swap

Gasification (coal to syngas) is not liquefaction (coal to liquid fuels, as in Fischer-Tropsch routes), and neither is coal bed methane, which is methane already trapped in coal seams and extracted by drilling. Round, outlay and ministry are the other easy swaps.

14 · Exam skills

Traps set by this release

Trap one: Round 2 drew many private applicants. False, both applications were from NTPC, a central PSU. Trap two: the Rs 37,500 crore scheme is India's first gasification incentive. False, an Rs 8,500 crore scheme predates it. Trap three: the scheme covers underground gasification. False, it is for surface projects.

15 · News

What happens next

Round 3 stays open until 11 January 2027. Watch for whether private companies apply after an all-NTPC Round 2, for the Ministry's evaluation of the NTPC proposals, and for progress on the separate Rs 8,500 crore scheme's rounds.

Five-question Prelims challenge

The companion reels have no answers. Choose an option here to reveal the correct answer and explanation. These are original practice questions, not past UPSC questions.

Question 1

With reference to coal gasification, consider the following statements:

  1. Coal gasification produces synthesis gas, consisting mainly of carbon monoxide and hydrogen.
  2. Surface gasification converts coal into gas within the coal seam, without mining the coal.
  3. Synthesis gas can be used to produce ammonia, methanol and hydrogen.

Which of the statements given above is/are correct?

Correct answer: B

Statements 1 and 3 are correct: gasification yields syngas, mainly CO and H2, which is the feedstock for ammonia (and hence urea), methanol and hydrogen. Statement 2 is incorrect: in-seam conversion without mining describes underground coal gasification; surface gasification uses mined coal in a reactor.

Source: Established physical chemistry of gasification; the scheme's own title (surface projects)

Question 2

With reference to the Scheme for Promotion of Surface Coal/Lignite Gasification Projects, consider the following statements:

  1. Round 2 of the scheme concluded with applications from five different companies.
  2. Round 3 of the scheme opened on 10 November 2026, with 11 January 2027 as the last date for submission of applications.
  3. The Round 2 applications envisage the production of urea, methanol and dimethyl ether.

Which of the statements given above is/are correct?

Correct answer: D

Statement 1 is incorrect: Round 2 concluded with two applications, both from NTPC Limited. Statements 2 and 3 are correct, per the Ministry of Coal's 10 November 2026 release: Round 3 runs from 10 November 2026 to 11 January 2027, and the NTPC applications envisage urea, methanol and dimethyl ether (DME).

Source: PIB, Ministry of Coal (10 Nov 2026), PRID 2334671

Question 3

Consider the following pairs:

  1. Coal gasification - conversion of coal into synthesis gas
  2. Coal liquefaction - conversion of coal into liquid hydrocarbon fuels
  3. Coal bed methane - methane produced by gasifying mined coal in surface reactors

How many of the above pairs are correctly matched?

Correct answer: B

Pairs 1 and 2 are correctly matched. Pair 3 is wrong: coal bed methane is methane naturally adsorbed within coal seams, extracted by drilling; it is not a product of gasifying mined coal.

Question 5

Consider the following statements about coal gasification initiatives in India:

  1. The Rs 37,500 crore scheme approved in May 2026 is the Union government's first financial incentive scheme for coal or lignite gasification.
  2. Underground coal gasification converts coal into gas within the coal seam itself.
  3. The national target is to achieve 100 million tonnes of coal gasification capacity by 2030.
