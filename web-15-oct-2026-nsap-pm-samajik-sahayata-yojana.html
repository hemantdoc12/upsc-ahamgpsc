One news item, fifteen exam angles

NSAP renamed, disability pension widened, Cabinet decision of 14 Oct 2026

The headline is a rename and a bigger disability pension. Behind it sit a Directive Principle that no court can enforce, a 2016 law that sets the disability bar and the 25 per cent rule, a ceiling on how many people can be paid, and States quietly adding their own. Each angle below is a doorway, not a question.

What happened

Press-reported

The approval

On Wednesday 14 Oct 2026 the Union Cabinet, chaired by the Prime Minister, approved continuing the National Social Assistance Programme for five years, 2026-27 to 2030-31, with a total outlay of Rs 53,428 crore. The proposal was from the Department of Rural Development. PIB says about 3.20 crore beneficiaries across all 36 States and UTs will benefit.

The new names

NSAP becomes Pradhan Mantri Samajik Sahayata Yojana. The Indira Gandhi National Old Age, Widow and Disability Pension Schemes become Pradhan Mantri Vridhavastha, Vidhawa and Divyangjan Pension Yojana. The National Family Benefit Scheme becomes Pradhan Mantri Parivar Labh Yojana.

The disability pension

Assistance rises to Rs 375 a month for ages 0 to 79 and Rs 625 for 80 and above. BusinessLine reports the old rates as Rs 300 and Rs 500. The benchmark-disability criterion becomes 40 per cent against 80 per cent, and the minimum age criterion becomes since birth. Minister Ashwini Vaishnaw said coverage would rise to 27 lakh from 8.33 lakh (BusinessLine). PIB says guidelines will be issued and implemented within three months.

What stays, and what critics say

The pension schemes for the aged and widows, and the family benefit scheme, continue in their existing form. The Hindu reports critics arguing that a fixed five-year allocation does not allow for rising beneficiary numbers or inflation, and that the three pension schemes are capped at 3.09 crore beneficiaries. Those are reported criticisms; this page takes no side on them.

The hub, in one line Reported: NSAP continues to 2030-31 at Rs 53,428 crore, renamed; disability pension at 40% benchmark, Rs 375 and Rs 625. Articles 37 and 41.

01
PM IIE

Art 41: the duty behind the pension

Article 41 asks the State to make effective provision, within the limits of its economic capacity, for the right to work, to education and to public assistance in cases of unemployment, old age, sickness and disablement. PIB's own background note says NSAP was launched to meet the Central Government's responsibility under it. Pensions are the plainest working example of Article 41.

Static syllabus hooks not from the press; check the bare text

  • Art 41: work, education and public assistance (DPSP)
  • Part IV runs from Art 36 to Art 51
  • Art 38 and Art 39: wider social-justice directions (check)

Go deeper

Read Article 41 word by word. Notice the phrase 'within the limits of its economic capacity' and what it does to the strength of the duty.

Common confusion

Article 41 is not a right to a pension. It is a direction to the State, and the pension is a policy choice made under it.

02
PM II

Why a DPSP cannot be sued on

Article 37 says the Directive Principles are not enforceable by any court, but are fundamental in the governance of the country and that it is the State's duty to apply them in making laws. In Minerva Mills (1980) the Supreme Court said the Constitution rests on a balance between Part III and Part IV. That is why a pension scheme is a Cabinet decision and not a court order.

Static hooks, go deeper, common confusion

Static syllabus hooks not from the press; check the bare text

  • Art 37: not enforceable by courts, yet fundamental in governance
  • Minerva Mills v Union of India (1980): balance of Parts III and IV
  • Art 21 read with DPSPs in later cases (check)

Go deeper

Find one case where a Directive Principle was read into a Fundamental Right. Ask what that changes for a scheme like this.

Common confusion

'Non-justiciable' does not mean 'optional' or 'unimportant'. It means a court will not compel it directly.

NSAP in 1995

The National Social Assistance Programme was launched on 15 Aug 1995 to help poor households. PIB says the Centre gives 100 per cent assistance so that support is uniformly available everywhere. In its original form it had three parts: old-age pension, family benefit and maternity benefit (check).

04
PM II

Renamed, not redesigned

Five names change: NSAP and its four sub-schemes. NSAP becomes Pradhan Mantri Samajik Sahayata Yojana. IGNOAPS becomes Pradhan Mantri Vridhavastha Pension Yojana, IGNWPS becomes Pradhan Mantri Vidhawa Pension Yojana, IGNDPS becomes Pradhan Mantri Divyangjan Pension Yojana, and the National Family Benefit Scheme becomes Pradhan Mantri Parivar Labh Yojana. The old-age, widow and family benefit schemes continue in their existing form.

Static hooks, go deeper, common confusion

Static syllabus hooks not from the press; check the bare text

  • PIB release of 14 Oct 2026: the old and new names
  • Continuation in existing form for old-age, widow and family benefit
  • Hindi names appear in the PIB release

Go deeper

Make a two-column table of the five old and new names. Then mark what changes besides the name. For three of the four sub-schemes, the PIB release says: nothing.

Common confusion

A rename is not a revision of rates or eligibility. Only the disability pension has both changed.

05
PM II

Benchmark disability

The Cabinet approved a benchmark-disability criterion of 40 per cent for the disability pension, against 80 per cent before. PIB says the Rights of Persons with Disabilities Act 2016 made this change necessary and linked the pension to 'benchmark disability'. The Act defines benchmark disability as a specified disability of not less than 40 per cent (check).

Static hooks, go deeper, common confusion

Static syllabus hooks not from the press; check the bare text

  • RPwD Act 2016: benchmark disability, 40% or more (check)
  • The Act recognises 21 specified disabilities (check)
  • Earlier NSAP threshold: 80%

Go deeper

Read the definition clause of the 2016 Act. Note who certifies a disability and on what scale.

Common confusion

'Persons with disabilities' and 'persons with benchmark disabilities' are two different groups in the Act.

06
PM II

The 25% rule

PIB reports that the 2016 Act requires disability-related assistance to be invariably 25 per cent higher than similarly placed schemes for other categories. BusinessLine reports the old disability rates as Rs 300 a month for 18 to 79 years and Rs 500 for 80 and above. The new rates are Rs 375 and Rs 625, each exactly 25 per cent higher.

Static hooks, go deeper, common confusion

Static syllabus hooks not from the press; check the bare text

  • Rs 300 to Rs 375: plus 25%
  • Rs 500 to Rs 625: plus 25%
  • Rule traced by PIB to the RPwD Act 2016

Go deeper

Check the arithmetic yourself. Then find which section of the Act carries the 25 per cent rule (not given in the press).

Common confusion

The rule sets a floor relative to other schemes. It does not say what the base scheme should pay.

Who gets covered

BusinessLine reports that the minimum age of 18 years is abolished and that persons with 40 per cent disability can now receive the pension. PIB says eligibility is from birth. Minister Ashwini Vaishnaw is quoted as saying coverage will rise to 27 lakh from 8.33 lakh. That coverage figure is the minister's statement, not an audited number.

Static hooks, go deeper, common confusion

Approved rates are not paid rates. PIB says guidelines come first and implementation follows.

08
PM II

RPwD Act 2016 and the UN Convention

The 2016 Act replaced the Persons with Disabilities Act 1995 and followed India's ratification of the UN Convention on the Rights of Persons with Disabilities. The shift in language, from welfare to rights, is the context for aligning the pension with the Act's benchmark disability.

Static hooks, go deeper, common confusion

Static syllabus hooks not from the press; check the bare text

  • Persons with Disabilities Act 1995 replaced by RPwD Act 2016
  • UNCRPD: India ratified in 2007 (check)
  • Shift from a welfare to a rights approach

Go deeper

Compare the 1995 and 2016 definitions of disability. Count the conditions recognised in each.

Common confusion

A Convention ratified by India binds India in international law. It becomes domestic law through legislation.

It quotes the Azim Premji University handbook as saying that, because the ceiling is set neither by administrative capacity nor by the number of eligible people, eligibility does not become an entitlement in practice. Social security in the Seventh Schedule

Social security and social insurance, and the welfare of labour, are Concurrent List subjects (entries 23 and 24). Relief of the disabled and unemployable is a State List subject (entry 9). So the Union and the States can both legislate and spend here (check the exact entries).

Targeting and exclusion

Who is counted as poor decides who is paid. The Hindu notes that several other welfare schemes use the 2011 Socio-Economic and Caste Census to identify beneficiaries, while NSAP caps the pension beneficiaries instead. Exclusion errors, people eligible but left out, are the usual critique of such ceilings.

Today's MCQ: answer and why

One Prelims-style question on today's item. This is the only drafted question on the page. The carousel slide shows the question. The key lives here.

With reference to the National Social Assistance Programme (NSAP) and the Directive Principles of State Policy, consider the following statements:

  1. NSAP was launched in 1995 to give effect to the Central Government's responsibility under Article 41 of the Constitution.
  2. Under the Rights of Persons with Disabilities Act 2016, benchmark disability is 40 per cent or more, and the Cabinet has aligned the disability-pension criterion with it.
  3. The Cabinet's October 2026 decision revises the rates of assistance for the old-age and widow pension schemes along with the disability pension.
  4. Under Article 37, the Directive Principles are not enforceable by any court, but are fundamental in the governance of the country.

Which of the statements given above are correct?

Answer: (b) 1, 2 and 4 only

(b) 1, 2 and 4 only. Correct. Statement 1 is the PIB background note on Article 41. Statement 2 is the RPwD Act 2016 definition and the PIB account of the change from 80 to 40 per cent. Statement 4 is Article 37.

(a) 1 and 4 only. Fails because it drops statement 2. Mistake tested: remembering the scheme's purpose and the Part IV rule but missing the disability change.

(c) 1, 3 and 4 only. Fails on statement 3. PIB says the old-age and widow pension schemes continue in their existing form. Only the disability pension has revised rates and eligibility. Mistake tested: assuming a rename comes with new rates for all sub-schemes.

(d) 2, 3 and 4 only. Fails on statement 3 and also rejects statement 1. Mistake tested: doubting that a welfare scheme links to a Directive Principle, which PIB itself states.
