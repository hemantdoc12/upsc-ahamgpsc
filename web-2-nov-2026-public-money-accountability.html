Public money: who checks the spending?

Less to read. More to connect. Yesterday: how the Constitution protects the independence of high offices. Today, the follow-through question: once money is approved, who checks how it is spent? One chain - approve, spend, accounts, audit, committees, action taken - mapped to fifteen exam angles.

No news hook - a concept build

Yesterday's removal web ended on a design point: independence is what makes accountability possible. Today applies it to public money. No new event drives this pack; the process is the topic (Art 112 chain).

The chain, one line per link

Money is approved (annual financial statement, Art 112), then spent, then accounted (form set on the CAG's advice, Art 150), then audited (CAG under the 1971 Act), then examined (PAC, Estimates, COPU under Lok Sabha Rules 308, 310, 312A), then acted on (Action Taken Reports). No single link is the whole answer.

From the removal family to the money question

The CAG sits inside yesterday's map: appointed by the President, removable only like a Supreme Court judge, barred from further government office, expenses charged on the Consolidated Fund (Art 148). Those shields are what let the spending questions be asked without fear.

Approval is not accountability

A budget approval answers one question: what may the money be spent on? It does not answer the other three: what was it actually spent on, was the spending by the rules, and did the purpose get achieved? Keep the chain: approve, spend, accounts, audit, committees, action taken (Art 112).

Three money boxes

Article 266(1): revenues, loans and repayments form the Consolidated Fund. 266(2): all other public moneys go to the Public Account. 267: a Contingency Fund at the President's disposal for unforeseen spending, recouped after Parliament authorizes it. 266(3) is the lock: no appropriation from a Consolidated Fund except by law.

Duties come from a 1971 law

Article 149 leaves duties to Parliament's law: the CAG (Duties, Powers and Conditions of Service) Act, 1971. Under it the CAG audits expenditure and receipts (ss. 13, 16), audits bodies substantially financed by grants or loans from a Consolidated Fund (s. 14), and covers government companies through supplementary audit with reports to the legislature.

Who writes the form of accounts

PAC: the composition

Rule 308, Lok Sabha Rules: not more than 22 members - 15 elected by the Lok Sabha, 7 by the Rajya Sabha, by proportional representation through the single transferable vote, one-year term. A Minister cannot be a member. First set up in 1921 after the Montagu-Chelmsford Reforms.

Estimates Committee: all Lok Sabha

30 members, every one from the Lok Sabha - the only financial committee with a single-House composition. Audit is ex-post

The CAG audits after money is spent. The older British-style comptroller function - prior control over each payment - was never the Indian design; the title stayed, the veto never came. A watchdog, not a cashier.

The follow-up loop

A report is not the end. Committees select findings, hear officers, table observations and recommendations; the government replies through Action Taken Reports; the committee checks again. Recommendation, implementation and verification are three different things. An audit paragraph is not a court verdict.

Six essay seeds

  • Accountability is a chain, not a checkpoint

    Approval, spending, accounts, audit, examination, action taken - weakness at any link is weakness of the whole. Argue the chain, not one institution.

  • Independence as enabling condition

    The same design that makes removal hard makes questions possible. Connect Article 148's shields to the quality of Parliament's financial oversight.

  • Ex-post versus ex-ante

    India chose audit after spending over control before it. What does that choice buy, and what does it cost?

  • The Opposition's seat

    A convention gives the PAC chair to the Opposition. What do conventions do that rules cannot - and what happens when conventions thin?

  • Propriety as a standard

    'Was it proper?' is wider than 'was it legal?' Where should propriety audit stop before it second-guesses policy itself?

  • From accounts to outcomes

    The fourth question - was the purpose achieved - turns financial accountability into governance accountability. Trace that widening.

Today's MCQ: answer and why

Which financial committee of Parliament has ALL its members drawn from the Lok Sabha?

Answer: (b) Estimates Committee

(b) is right: the Estimates Committee has 30 members, all elected by the Lok Sabha (Rule 310).

(a) fails: the PAC has 22 members - 15 Lok Sabha plus 7 Rajya Sabha (Rule 308).

(c) fails: COPU also has 22 - 15 Lok Sabha plus 7 Rajya Sabha (Rule 312A).

(d) fails because (a) and (c) fail. Single-House composition is the Estimates Committee's distinguishing mark. Sources: Lok Sabha Rules 308, 310, 312A (sansad.in committee introductions).
