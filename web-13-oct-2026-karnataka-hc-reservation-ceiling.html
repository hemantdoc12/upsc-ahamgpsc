Karnataka High Court and the 50% reservation ceiling

Karnataka High Court strikes down the 2022 SC/ST quota hike that went past the 50% ceiling, 12 Oct 2026

The ruling is a few paragraphs long. Behind it sit a nine-judge bench from 1992, a ceiling no one wrote into the Constitution, a Schedule that once shielded Tamil Nadu's 69%, a data requirement that keeps being reread, and a consultation duty the State met three years late. Each angle below is a doorway, not a question.

What happened

Press-reported

The ruling

On Monday 12 Oct a bench of Chief Justice Vibhu Bakhru and Justice K S Hemalekha of the Karnataka High Court struck down provisions of the Karnataka Scheduled Castes and Scheduled Tribes (Reservation of Seats in Educational Institutions and of Appointments or Posts in the Services under the State) Act, 2022. The Act had raised SC reservation from 15 to 17 per cent and ST reservation from 3 to 7 per cent. With the OBC share unchanged at 32 per cent, the total went from 50 to 56 per cent. The case is Mahendra Kumar Mitra & others v Union of India & others, on two petitions: one by the state president of the Dr Ambedkar Scheduled Castes Federation, one by three civil-engineering graduates who had applied for BBMP Assistant Engineer posts.

What was struck down

Section 3, which raised the quota in educational institutions, was struck down insofar as it takes the aggregate reservation beyond the 50 per cent ceiling, for violating Articles 14 and 15. Section 4, which raised it in State government services, was struck down as unconstitutional for violating Articles 14 and 16.

Why

On the ceiling: the 50 per cent limit set in Indra Sawhney (1992) holds, a subjective opinion that a class is not adequately represented lets the State reserve only within it, and the State's argument that over 90 per cent of Karnataka belongs to SC, ST and OBC communities was rejected as destructive of the principle of equality. The Court declined to list what would be extraordinary enough. On consultation: Articles 338(9) and 338A(9) require the State to consult the National Commissions for SCs and STs on major policy matters, before the decision. The State's letters came nearly three years after the Act.

What the State relied on, and what comes next

The State relied on the Nagamohan Das Commission, the Subhash Adi committee and the Ratnaprabha committee, and argued the ceiling is not inflexible. The Act received the Governor's assent on 11 Jan 2023 and was deemed in force from 1 Nov 2022. Deputy Chief Minister G Parameshwara said the State would examine legal remedies, including an appeal to the Supreme Court, protect ongoing recruitment and admissions in accordance with law, and asked the Centre to review the 50 per cent ceiling and consider Ninth Schedule inclusion. Those are reported statements; this page takes no side on them.

01
PM IIE

The 50% ceiling and the case that set it

Karnataka’s law took total reservation to 56%. The High Court struck down the part above 50%, because the ceiling comes from Indra Sawhney (1992), a nine-judge bench. The press reports the Court saying the ceiling is not an elastic limit tied to population or the extent of backwardness, with room left only for extraordinary situations.

Static hooks, go deeper, common confusion

Static syllabus hooks not from the press; check the bare text

  • Indra Sawhney v Union of India (1992), nine judges: 50% as the rule
  • M.R. Balaji (1962) first spoke of reservation staying under 50%
  • T. Devadasan (1964): carry-forward pushing past 50% struck down
  • Arts 15(4) and 16(4) as the enabling clauses
  • Indra Sawhney also gave the creamy-layer exclusion for OBCs

Go deeper

Read the 1992 majority on the 50% rule and on the ‘extraordinary situations’ exception side by side. Note how little the Court said about what such a situation looks like.

Common confusion

The 50% figure is a judge-made rule from case law, not a number in the Constitution’s text.

02
PM II

Two clauses that enable, and the sections they were struck under

The press reports two different holdings. Section 3, on seats in educational institutions, was struck down, insofar as it went past 50%, for violating Articles 14 and 15. Section 4, on posts in State services, was struck down for violating Articles 14 and 16. Education and jobs run on different articles.

Static hooks, go deeper, common confusion

Static syllabus hooks not from the press; check the bare text

  • Art 15(4): special provision for socially and educationally backward classes, and SCs and STs
  • Art 15(5): 93rd Amendment (2005), reservation in educational institutions; upheld in Ashoka Kumar Thakur (2008)
  • Art 16(4): reservation in appointments where a class is not adequately represented
  • Art 16(4A) and 16(4B): promotion and carry-forward vacancies
  • Art 15(6), 16(6): EWS clauses (103rd Amendment, 2019)

Go deeper

Lay the clauses of Article 15 and Article 16 side by side in a table. Mark which ones enable, which ones restrict.

Common confusion

Art 15 covers education and public places. Art 16 covers public employment. Mixing them up is common.

03
PM II

Claims of SCs and STs, and the efficiency clause

Article 335 is the constitutional counterweight in every services-quota case: claims of SCs and STs are to be taken into consideration, consistently with the maintenance of efficiency of administration. The State in this case relied on a report on backwardness, inadequacy of representation and administrative efficiency. The press does not report the Court deciding anything on efficiency, so any link to Art 335 here is our reading.

Static hooks, go deeper, common confusion

Static syllabus hooks not from the press; check the bare text

  • Art 335 and the 82nd Amendment (2000) proviso on relaxation in qualifying marks and standards
  • Indra Sawhney (1992) on efficiency and Art 16(4)
  • M. Nagaraj (2006): efficiency must not be compromised, one of three things the State must examine for promotion quotas
  • Art 16(4A): promotion for SC/ST

Go deeper

Follow the word ‘efficiency’ through Indra Sawhney, Nagaraj and Jarnail Singh. See whose burden it is to show it.

Common confusion

Art 335 applies to SCs and STs only, not to OBCs.

04
PM IM II

Karnataka’s two quota laws: 2018 and 2022

The 2022 Act raised SC reservation from 15 to 17% and ST from 3 to 7%, leaving the 32% OBC share untouched, so the total rose from 50 to 56% (press-reported). It received the Governor’s assent on 11 Jan 2023 and was deemed in force from 1 Nov 2022. The State relied on the Nagamohan Das Commission, the Subhash Adi committee and the Ratnaprabha committee. Separately, a 2018 Karnataka law on seniority after reserved promotion has its own history.

Static hooks, go deeper, common confusion

Static syllabus hooks not from the press; check the bare text

  • B.K. Pavitra v Union of India (2017): Karnataka’s 2002 consequential seniority law struck down
  • B.K. Pavitra II (2019): the 2018 Karnataka law upheld
  • Central government services reserve 15% for SCs and 7.5% for STs, a national benchmark to set beside 17 and 7
  • Art 31B and the Ninth Schedule as a possible route (see angle 7)

Go deeper

Build a three-row timeline: 2017 and 2019 Pavitra rulings, the 2022 Act, the 2026 judgment. Mark what each decided.

Common confusion

The 2018 law was about seniority in promotion. The 2022 law is about the size of the quota. Different laws, different courts’ questions.

05
PM II

Data is necessary, and not enough

The press reports the Court holding that collecting quantifiable data on backwardness and inadequate representation does not, by itself, permit a breach of the 50% ceiling. Such reports can support measures within the limit, including internal reservation, but are not a legal basis for exceeding it. The commissions’ reports were the State’s case, and the Court sorted what they could and could not carry.

Static hooks, go deeper, common confusion

Static syllabus hooks not from the press; check the bare text

  • M. Nagaraj (2006): quantifiable data on backwardness and inadequacy of representation for promotion quotas
  • Jarnail Singh v Lachhmi Narain Gupta (2018): backwardness of SCs and STs need not be shown again, but inadequacy of representation must be
  • Art 16(4A) and 16(4B)

Go deeper

Read what Jarnail Singh dropped from Nagaraj and what it kept. Compare that with what the High Court says data can and cannot do.

Common confusion

Jarnail Singh removed one data requirement (backwardness) but not the other (inadequate representation).

06
PM IIE

What counts as extraordinary

Indra Sawhney left room to cross 50% only in extraordinary situations. The press reports the High Court saying it was neither apposite nor feasible to list them exhaustively, but that a subjective opinion that a backward class ought to get a few percentage points higher is not one. Population share alone was rejected too: accepting the State’s over-90% argument would be destructive of the principle of equality, the Court said.

Static hooks, go deeper, common confusion

Static syllabus hooks not from the press; check the bare text

  • Indra Sawhney (1992): remote and far-flung areas, with an example of the people living there
  • Dr Jaishri Laxmanrao Patil (2021): Maratha quota breaching 50% struck down, no extraordinary situation shown
  • Patna High Court (June 2024) on Bihar’s 65% law; check the later history
  • Janhit Abhiyan (2022) in angle 9 for the other side of the argument

Go deeper

Collect three cases where a State claimed an extraordinary situation, Maharashtra, Bihar and Karnataka, and note what each State actually offered.

Common confusion

The exception is real, but in practice courts have almost never found it met.

07
PM IIE

The Ninth Schedule route and Tamil Nadu’s 69%

Tamil Nadu’s 69% reservation law was placed in the Ninth Schedule in 1994, which is why it was shielded when it went beyond 50%. The press reports Karnataka’s Deputy Chief Minister asking the Centre to consider Ninth Schedule inclusion and to review the ceiling. Whether that route would survive is a separate legal question, and this page does not predict it.

Static hooks, go deeper, common confusion

Static syllabus hooks not from the press; check the bare text

  • Art 31B and the Ninth Schedule (First Amendment, 1951)
  • Tamil Nadu Backward Classes, SCs and STs (Reservation of Seats and Appointments) Act, 1993; 76th Amendment (1994)
  • I.R. Coelho v State of Tamil Nadu (2007): laws placed after 24 April 1973 can be tested against the basic structure
  • Kesavananda Bharati (1973): basic structure
  • Art 368 and the need for Parliament to act

Go deeper

Trace the Ninth Schedule from 1951 to Coelho. Mark the cut-off date and why the Court chose it.

Common confusion

The Ninth Schedule is not a wall: since Coelho the Court can review laws placed in it.

08
PM II

Federalism: what a State may and may not do

The press reports the Court saying, notwithstanding the State’s legislative competence to enact the Act, consultation with the commissions was mandatory. Competence and compliance are two steps. A State may legislate on its public services and, with Parliament, on education, but both remain under the equality clauses and the Court’s reading of them.

Static hooks, go deeper, common confusion

Static syllabus hooks not from the press; check the bare text

  • Art 246 and the Seventh Schedule
  • List II Entry 41: State public services
  • List III Entry 25: education, after the 42nd Amendment (1976)
  • Art 309: recruitment and conditions of service
  • Art 13 and judicial review of State laws

Go deeper

Place the Karnataka Act on the Seventh Schedule: which list, which entry, who else could have legislated.

Common confusion

Legislative competence is a different question from validity. A State can have the power and still breach a fundamental right.

09
PM IIE

The ceiling that EWS bent

The 103rd Amendment added a 10% quota for economically weaker sections outside SC, ST and OBC, which on its face takes total reservation past 50%. In Janhit Abhiyan (2022) a five-judge bench upheld it by 3 to 2. That is why the 50% ceiling is better described as a rule for caste-based reservation than as an absolute cap. How far that reasoning carries to SC/ST quotas is for the lawyers.

Static hooks, go deeper, common confusion

Static syllabus hooks not from the press; check the bare text

  • 103rd Constitution Amendment (2019): Art 15(6) and 16(6)
  • Janhit Abhiyan v Union of India (Nov 2022), five judges, 3:2
  • Indra Sawhney (1992) had rejected economic criteria alone
  • Basic structure challenge: equality and the ceiling

Go deeper

Read the majority and the minority on whether 50% is part of the basic structure. Separate what each judge said about the ceiling from what they said about EWS.

Common confusion

The judges did not all say the ceiling can be crossed. Majority and dissent differ in their reasons.

10
PM IIE

Who is inside the quota and who is at the front

Raising a quota does not say who inside the community gets it. Indra Sawhney excluded the creamy layer among OBCs. Jarnail Singh (2018) said the creamy-layer idea applies to SC and ST promotions too, and in 2024 several judges in the sub-classification case spoke of it again. The press does not report the Court deciding creamy layer here, so this is context for the quota, not a ground of the judgment.

Static hooks, go deeper, common confusion

Static syllabus hooks not from the press; check the bare text

  • Indra Sawhney (1992): creamy-layer exclusion for OBCs
  • Jarnail Singh (2018): creamy-layer principle for SC/ST promotions
  • State of Punjab v Davinder Singh (Aug 2024): concurring views on creamy layer among SC/ST
  • Karnataka OBC share of 32% unchanged by the 2022 Act (press-reported)

Go deeper

Follow the creamy layer from the 1993 OBC office memorandum through Jarnail Singh to the 2024 opinions. Mark where it is settled and where it is argued.

Common confusion

Creamy layer for OBCs is settled and applied. For SC/ST it is a live debate.

11
PM IM II

Reservation inside the reservation

The press reports the Court saying the State’s reports could support measures to ensure benefits reach marginalised communities, including internal reservation, but not a breach of the ceiling. Internal reservation is the other way to deal with unequal sharing: do not raise the total, divide it. A seven-judge bench in 2024 allowed States to sub-classify Scheduled Castes.

Static hooks, go deeper, common confusion

Static syllabus hooks not from the press; check the bare text

  • State of Punjab v Davinder Singh (Aug 2024), seven judges, 6:1
  • E.V. Chinnaiah (2004) overruled
  • Art 341 and 342: the Presidential lists
  • Karnataka’s Nagamohan Das Commission as the State’s data source (press-reported)

Go deeper

Compare the majority and the single dissent in Davinder Singh on Art 341. Note what each says about who can change the list.

Common confusion

Sub-classifying within the SC list is not the same as adding or removing a caste from the list.

12
PM II

Consult first: Articles 338(9) and 338A(9)

This is a ground separate from the 50% rule. The press reports the Court holding that reservation policy is a major policy matter, that consulting the National Commissions for SCs and STs is mandatory, and that consultation must come before the decision. The State’s letters of 10 Dec 2025 and 20 Jan 2026, nearly three years after the Act, were not enough, and the duty covers all major policy matters affecting these communities, not only adverse ones.

Static hooks, go deeper, common confusion

Static syllabus hooks not from the press; check the bare text

  • Art 338: National Commission for Scheduled Castes
  • Art 338A: National Commission for Scheduled Tribes
  • 89th Amendment (2003) split the single commission in two
  • Art 338B: National Commission for Backward Classes (102nd Amendment, 2018)
  • Art 338(9) and 338A(9): the Union and every State shall consult on major policy matters

Go deeper

Read Articles 338, 338A and 338B together. Note which commission has which powers and which bodies are consulted on which matters.

Common confusion

The consultation duty is in clause (9) of the two articles. Check the clause number against the bare text.

13
PM IIE

Articles 14, 15 and 16 as one equality code

The Court’s language, as reported, is about balance: the 50% limit reflects the balance between the equality clauses and the power to make special provision. Any reservation in excess would breach those clauses, it said. Articles 14, 15 and 16 form one code, and reservation is read as part of equality, not as an exception to it.

Static hooks, go deeper, common confusion

Static syllabus hooks not from the press; check the bare text

  • Art 14: equality before the law and equal protection of the laws
  • Art 15(1) and 16(2): no discrimination on listed grounds
  • State of Kerala v N.M. Thomas (1975): Art 16(4) as an application of 16(1)
  • Indra Sawhney (1992) on formal and substantive equality
  • Basic structure and equality

Go deeper

Read Thomas and Indra Sawhney on whether reservation is an exception to equality or a part of it.

Common confusion

Whether reservation is an exception to equality or part of it has been argued in the cases; Thomas treats Art 16(4) as an application of 16(1).

A quota in a statute becomes real through rosters, notifications and recruitment rules. The press reports the State saying it will examine legal remedies, including an appeal to the Supreme Court, while protecting ongoing recruitment and educational opportunities in accordance with law. What happens to selections made on the higher percentages is not reported and is not assumed here.

15
M IM IIE

Population share, representation and who counts

The State’s argument, as reported, was that over 90% of Karnataka’s population belongs to SC, ST and OBC communities and reservation should match it. The Court called that destructive of the principle of equality. For GS-I the data question matters: who is counted, how often, and what a quota is measured against. The Census 2011 shares for SC and ST are the usual starting point, to be checked.

Static hooks, go deeper, common confusion

Static syllabus hooks not from the press; check the bare text

  • Census 2011: SCs about 16.6% and STs about 8.6% of India; check the Karnataka figures in the Census tables
  • Art 330 and 332: political reservation tied to population share
  • Art 340: Commission on backward classes
  • Representation in cadre versus population share

Go deeper

Compare the Census share of SC and ST in Karnataka with the 15, 3 and then 17, 7 figures. Note what the numbers can and cannot explain.

Common confusion

Population share and adequate representation are different measures, and the courts use the second.
