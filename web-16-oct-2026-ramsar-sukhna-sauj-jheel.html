Sukhna and Sauj Jheel join the Ramsar List

Sukhna Wetland and Sauj Jheel join the Ramsar List, India's tally 103, announced 15 Oct 2026

The headline is a number: 103. Behind it sit a 1971 treaty, two very different wetlands, a bird flyway, a species list, and the plain fact that an international listing is recognition and not a legal shield. Each angle below is a doorway, not a question.

What happened

Press-reported

The announcement

On 15 Oct 2026 Environment Minister Bhupender Yadav announced that Sukhna Wetland (Chandigarh) and Sauj Jheel (Uttar Pradesh) have been added to the Ramsar List of Wetlands of International Importance, taking India's tally to 103 (PIB).

Sukhna Wetland

Ramsar site 2602. The Secretariat describes a peri-urban reservoir at the base of the Outer Himalayas that regulates sediment and water flows. It serves waterbirds on the Central Asian Flyway. Species named include common pochard (vulnerable), Pallas's fish eagle and black pond turtle (both endangered). Named challenges: sediment and invasive species (Ramsar Secretariat).

Sauj Jheel

Ramsar site 2603, a shallow lake in the Ganga floodplain in Uttar Pradesh, near Saman Bird Sanctuary (site 2413). It is an Important Bird and Biodiversity Area with more than 30,000 waterbirds. Notable waterbirds named include sarus crane and greater spotted eagle (both vulnerable). Management actions for Sauj are proposed (Ramsar Secretariat).

What is a claim

The minister's link between the tally and biodiversity, water and climate security and livelihoods is his statement as reported by PIB. This page takes no side on it.

The Ramsar Convention

Adopted 1971 at Ramsar, Iran, in force 1975. Treaty on conservation and wise use of wetlands. India has been a party since 1982.

Static hooks, go deeper, common confusion

Static syllabus hooks not from the press; check the bare text

  • Ramsar Convention on Wetlands, 1971; in force 1975
  • Treaty is about 'wise use', not only protection
  • India has been a party since 1982

Go deeper

Read the treaty's idea of 'wise use' and how a party commits to it when it lists a site.

Common confusion

Ramsar is a treaty, not a law that India's courts enforce directly. Listing a site does not by itself create a legal duty on the site.

Listing is not a legal shield

A Ramsar listing is international recognition. Protection in India comes from national and State law (check).

Static hooks, go deeper, common confusion

Static syllabus hooks not from the press; check the bare text

  • Treaty obligations and domestic law (check)
  • Art 253: Parliament may legislate to implement treaties (check)
  • Art 51(c): respect for international law (check)

Go deeper

Ask what makes a treaty obligation enforceable at home. Then ask what a Ramsar listing adds.

Listing is recognition. It is not a statute, and it does not override national or State law.

Today's MCQ: answer and why

One Prelims-style question on today's item. This is the only drafted question on the page. The carousel slide shows the question. The key lives here.

With reference to wetlands and the Ramsar Convention, consider the following statements:

  1. The Ramsar Convention is an intergovernmental treaty adopted in 1971 for the conservation and wise use of wetlands.
  2. In October 2026 Sukhna Wetland (Chandigarh) and Sauj Jheel (Uttar Pradesh) were added to the Ramsar List, taking India's tally to 103.
  3. Once a wetland is designated a Ramsar Site, the Convention gives it legal protection that overrides national and State law.
  4. Sauj Jheel is a shallow lake in the floodplain of the Ganga, close to Saman Bird Sanctuary, which is itself a Ramsar Site.

Which of the statements given above are correct?

Answer: (b) 1, 2 and 4 only

(b) 1, 2 and 4 only. Correct. Statement 1 is the Convention's basic description (static; check). Statement 2 is PIB and the Ramsar Secretariat. Statement 4 is the Ramsar Secretariat's description of Sauj and Saman.

(a) 1 and 4 only. Fails because it drops statement 2. Mistake tested: not tracking the news item itself.

(c) 1, 3 and 4 only. Fails on statement 3, which is false: a Ramsar listing is international recognition and does not override national or State law. It also drops statement 2. Mistake tested: treating a listing as a legal shield.

(d) 2, 3 and 4 only. Fails on statement 3 and also rejects statement 1. Mistake tested: the same legal-shield error, plus doubting the basic treaty fact.
